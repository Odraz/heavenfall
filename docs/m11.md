# M11: Painted first-person weapons

The weapon in the player's hands is on screen all the time, yet it's the plainest art in the game. Today each class's weapon is a set of Blender renders of simple boxes and tubes in flat colors (`scripts/blender/weapons.py`). The goal is the look of the concept art (`assets/art-src/reference/concept-1.webp`, the Brimstone Shotgun on the right): a painted, inked weapon with worn metal, warm light and glowing runes, held in the class's own hands.

**How.** A first-person weapon is always seen from the same place, so one painting shows exactly what a 3D model would. Each class gets **one painted image** of its weapon in its hands, made by the human with an image generator. Claude cuts it out, and the HUD draws it instead of the Blender frames. The 3D feel comes from the code, not from more frames:
- **recoil** that kicks the weapon back and tips the muzzle up;
- **glowing parts** (runes, coals, hot bands) that breathe while idle and flare on every shot;
- **sway**: the weapon lags a little behind the view when the player turns;
- **light**: the weapon dims a little in shade, like the characters.

Three classes get a second version of their image, swapped in by the code:
- the Binder's barrels spinning (a painted copy);
- the Heretic's launcher with its censer fired;
- the Fallen's pump racked back (optional).

Claude makes the last two from the main images. The Binder's Scourge is the Binder's painted left fist sweeping across the view, with the game's existing red-hot chain ring art as the swinging chain.

**Nothing changes** in the simulation, the protocol, the classes, sounds or the world's rendering. Only the local player's HUD weapon changes. Other players' weapons are part of their world sprites and stay as they are.

All numbers are **initial values**.

## 1. Terms

- **The view**: the whole screen as the painting composes it, **3 840 × 2 160 view pixels** (16:9). The HUD scales it to the screen's height: one view pixel is `100 / 2160` vh. The view's center column (1 920) lies on the screen's vertical center line, like today's `centerX`.
- **The frame**: the part of the view that holds the weapon. It runs from **row 864 (40% of the view height)** to the bottom of the view, and across the columns of the weapon's bounding box plus an 8 px margin. Its bottom is the screen's bottom.
- **The muzzle**: the center of the bore's opening (the Heretic: the censer's center; the Binder: the center of the barrel cluster's front). The muzzle flash and Sacrament's beam start there.
- **The pivot**: the center of the right fist on the grip. Recoil tips the weapon around it. The Binder holds no visible hand, so its pivot is the middle of the gun where it leaves the bottom edge.
- **Idle and alt**: each class's main image is its *idle* frame. Its second image, if any, is its *alt* frame: the same view with one thing changed.

The images aren't composed exactly as the prompts asked: every muzzle is higher than 58%, and the Fallen's and Heretic's weapons reach up to 21–25% of the height. So **placing** each one is part of the preparation. A rough mockup confirmed that a scale and an offset per class put them well (muzzles near (57%, 57%), weapons below 45%).

**Resolution.** The Binder's, Betrayer's and sweep images are about 930 px tall. On a 1080p screen they're upscaled about 1.2× by the browser, on 1440p 1.5×, so they're a little soft. If the human supplies upscaled copies (same names, any size), use them. Otherwise scale with Lanczos, as the rest.

**Storage scale.** The manifest and the HUD work in view pixels, but each class's files are stored at *k* times the view's pixels: `k = min(1, the source's pixels per view pixel after placement)`, rounded up to a multiple of 0.05 and recorded in the script's table. For example, `fp-binder` (926 px tall) placed at scale 1.0 gets k = 926 / 2 160 ≈ 0.45. Storing more pixels than the source has adds bytes, not detail; the HUD sizes the images by the manifest either way. All of a class's layers (alt, glow, hammer, cylinder, green censer) use its *k*, so they line up.

Claude prepares the images by script, extending `scripts/cutout.py` with a `weapons` kind. It writes each class's files to `assets/sprites/weapon-<class>/` and **deletes the old `atlas.png` and `atlas.json`** there.

**Each idle image:**
1. **Key out the green** as for the sprites today (the green keyed to transparency, the spill removed from the edges), then clear opaque specks under 64 px.
2. **Measure** the muzzle, the pivot and the **barrel's axis** (two points on the bore's center line or the sight line, the muzzle and one near the receiver) by looking at the image.
3. **Place** it in the view with a per-class **scale** (the image's height as a fraction of the view's height), **rotation** (around the muzzle, up to ±15°) and **offset**, recorded in the script's table per class. Choose them so that:
   - **the weapon aims at the crosshair**: its axis, extended forward from the muzzle, crosses the crosshair's row (the view's row 1 080) within ±3% of the view's width (±115 px) of the center column. Without this, a weapon looks like it fires somewhere left of where the shots go;
   - the muzzle lands a little right of and below the crosshair, **within 55–63% of the view's width and 56–62% of its height**, at the class's target in the script's table;
   - nothing opaque is above row 864 (40%);
   - the weapon's bottom (forearms, the gun's rear) runs off the view's bottom edge, with no cut end visible, also after the rotation. Scale up or move down to hide one.

   The script prints where each axis crosses the crosshair's row and fails if it's outside the ±3%.

   **Measured** on the images as delivered, placed without rotation, the axes cross:
   - the Heretic's, near the center, though its launcher's face and side still make it look turned left;
   - the Betrayer's, at 42% of the width, clearly left.

   A mockup with the Heretic rotated 10° and the Betrayer 14° (clockwise), their muzzles at (62%, 60%) and (58%, 60%), aims both at the crosshair. Start from those values, the Fallen's and the Binder's muzzles at (58%, 59%), and the scales Fallen 0.85, Heretic 0.9, Binder 1.0, Betrayer 1.1. The Fallen's shotgun also points up and to the left, so check it the same way. A rotation can't change which side of a gun the painting shows: if a weapon still *looks* turned left after it, that's for the human to judge in the previews, and regenerate it with the prompt's *Pointing* fix ([art-prompts.md](art-prompts.md)).
4. **Resample** it into the view at the class's storage scale *k* and **crop** to the frame (§1).

**Glow layers.** For each prepared frame, a glow layer from its emissive pixels:
- A pixel is **emissive** when it's opaque, its hue is 0–35° (red to orange), its saturation is at least 0.6, its value at least 0.85, and its red at least 0.9. Gold, bronze and silver paint fall outside this; check each mask in a preview and adjust the thresholds per class if needed (record it).
- The glow layer is the emissive pixels' color, plus the same pixels blurred by 12 px at 50% strength (a soft halo that may spill outside the outline: the HUD isn't subject to the world sprites' alpha cut).
- The weapon box has a transform, so it's an isolated group: `screen` (§3.1) blends the glow only with the weapon's own images, and outside the outline the halo is drawn with normal alpha over the game. That's intended: it reads as a soft glow and doesn't need to light the scene.
- A class whose emissive pixels are under 0.2% of its opaque pixels gets no glow layer. Expect that for the Betrayer, apart from its gems, and perhaps for the Heretic once the censer is gone (its alt then has none).

## 3. The HUD weapon

### 3.1 Layers

The weapon box (`.weapon`) is placed by the manifest: `left: calc(50% + left vh-units)`, `top: top vh-units`, `width` and `height` likewise, where one view pixel is `100 / 2160` vh. It holds, bottom to top:
1. the idle image;
2. the alt image (if any), opacity *a* (§3.4);
3. the idle glow, `mix-blend-mode: screen`, opacity *g* × (1 − *a*);
4. the alt glow, `screen`, opacity *g* × *a*.

The idle image is hidden (`visibility: hidden`) whenever *a* = 1, so a part that moves in the alt image (the pump, the hand) doesn't show twice. The muzzle flash stays behind the weapon box, as today (M8 §3.3). The weapon and the swing stay under every other HUD element, and the HUD stays readable over them (§3.7). Images are `<img>` elements; their files load only for the local class, when the HUD is built, as today.

**Removed:** the 4 fire frames, `fireFrame`, `FIRE_ANIM_MAX_MS`, `setWeaponFrame` and the atlas positioning. The muzzle is now one point per class.

### 3.2 Transform

Each frame, the weapon box gets one transform, with `transform-origin` at the pivot (in the box's own pixels):

```
translate(bobX + swayX, bobY + swayY + recoilY) rotate(tilt)
```

CSS turns positive angles clockwise. Every muzzle is up and to the left of its pivot, so a clockwise turn lifts it.

All offsets in vh. `bobX`, `bobY` are the walking bob (M8 §3.4, unchanged); `recoilY` the existing recoil (8% of the screen height, 12% for the slug, 14% for the Silver Bullet, falling linearly to 0 over 120 ms, unchanged).

**Tilt** (degrees; the muzzle tips up):

| Attack | Tilt |
|---|---|
| Brimstone Shotgun | 4 |
| Brimstone Slug | 6 |
| Censer Launcher | 5 |
| Chain Gun | 0.8, alternating up and down each shot (+0.8, −0.8, …) |
| Silver Revolver | 6 |
| Silver Bullet | 9 |

After a shot, `tilt = T · (1 − s)²` with `s = min(1, sinceShot / D)` and `D = min(interval, 250 ms)`, where `interval` is the time between shots `shot()` is given (so it halves in a Field of Blood). `Hud.shot` gets the tilt as a new parameter; `game.ts` passes the table's value.

**Sway.** The weapon lags behind the view's turning:
- targets: `swayX = clamp(−0.006 · yawRate, −2, 2)` and `swayY = clamp(0.004 · pitchRate, −1.5, 1.5)`, in vh, where the rates are the view's yaw and pitch change in degrees per second over the last frame (yaw positive to the right, pitch positive up), from the camera the game renders with;
- each eases toward its target with a time constant of 0.08 s;
- zero while dead, and reset to 0 on a teleport or respawn (the bob's teleport rule).

`muzzlePoint` and the flash's position use the same transform: the muzzle turned clockwise by tilt around the pivot, then translated. A pure function computes that, so it's tested (§6).

### 3.3 Glow

- **Idle:** `g = 0.35 + 0.15 · sin(2π · now / 2 400 ms)`.
- **On a shot:** `g = max(idle, 1 − sinceShot / 300 ms)`. The Chain Gun firing 12 times a second keeps it near 1.
- The Field of Blood's red glow on the weapon (M9 §5.1) stays as today.

### 3.4 Alt frames

*a* is a function of the time since the last shot (`sinceShot`) and `interval`:

| Class | Alt | *a* |
|---|---|---|
| Heretic | `empty` | 1 from the shot until 0.5 · interval, then falls linearly to 0 at 0.8 · interval (the fresh censer fades in). With Sacrament (no `shot()`), 0. |
| Binder | `spin` | 1 while `sinceShot` < 150 ms, then falls linearly to 0 over 100 ms. So it stays spinning while the Chain Gun fires. |
| Fallen | `pump` | 1 from 0.3 · interval to 0.6 · interval after a shotgun or slug shot, otherwise 0. A hard cut both ways: a two-frame pump, like Doom's. |

### 3.5 Light

The weapon dims in shade like the characters (M10 §5.4): its brightness is `lightTint(L, false).brightness` (0.85–1.0) from `lightmap.ts`, where `L` is the baked lightmap's value at the local player's position (x, y).
- It eases toward that value with a time constant of 0.25 s.
- It's applied as `filter: brightness(b)` on the weapon box (and the swing's box). It's combined with the Field of Blood's `drop-shadow` when that's on.
- The filter is written only when `b` has changed by at least 0.005 since the last write, so a still player costs no repaints.
- Keep the lightmap's data on the CPU for this (it's baked there already).

### 3.6 The Scourge swing

The Binder's free left fist sweeps across the bottom of the view while a red-hot chain whips around in front of it. Both images (§2) are drawn over the weapon, each in its own box, for `SWING_MS` (300 ms) after `swing()`, as today. With `t` = elapsed / 300 ms and `e = smoothstep(t)`, and positions in vh from the screen's center (x right, y down):

**The chain arc** (the near half of the chain ring):
- It's scaled to **160 vh wide**, its ends at x = ±80 vh and its lowest point at y = +32 vh (its height follows from the ring's proportions).
- It's shown through a **moving window**: the **lead** goes from x = −80 to +80 vh by `e`. Only the part from 60 vh behind the lead up to the lead shows, fading out over its trailing 30 vh (a `mask-image` linear gradient moved each frame).
- So it reads as one length of chain whipped from left to right around the player.
- Its glow layer has the same window, at opacity 0.8.

**The fist:**
- Its center moves from (−70, +45) to (+35, +40) vh by `e`.
- It rotates around its center from −25° to +15° (CSS degrees, clockwise positive).

**Both:**
- Opacity 1, falling linearly to 0 over the last 60 ms.
- They get the same bob, sway and light as the weapon.

**The Chain Gun stays visible** and jolts with the swing: `translate(+3 · sin(πt) vh, +2 · sin(πt) vh)` added to its transform.

**Check** (stage 3): at 150 ms the lead is near the screen's center column, below the crosshair. The chain never covers the crosshair, and no cut end of it shows. The fist trails below the chain, and nothing shows its cut ends. Tune the numbers until that holds, and record the final values.

### 3.7 HUD readability

The painted weapons are big and busy (dark iron, orange glow), and they reach the bottom corners: the Binder's and the Betrayer's reach the ability slots on the right, the Fallen's and Heretic's forearms the bottom edge. The ability slots and the HP frame must **never be hidden** by the weapon, and must stay **readable** over it and over the bright floor and sky:

- **On top.** The weapon box, the flash and the swing's boxes are under every other HUD element: the HP frame, the ability slots with their key labels and cooldown rings, the buff icon, the crosshair, the hints and the chat. Give them an explicit lower stacking order (`z-index: 0`, the HUD's other elements 1 or more), not just DOM order.
- **A backing plate behind the ability slots.** `.abilities` gets a dark plate behind its slots and key labels: `rgba(14, 10, 8, 0.7)`, 8 px padding, a 10 px corner radius, and a soft dark edge (`box-shadow: 0 0 16px 6px rgba(14, 10, 8, 0.5)`). It's the same for every class and doesn't change size or move, so nothing in the row shifts (the HUD's layout rule).
  - The slots stay exactly where they are today: the plate's padding grows outward from them.
  - Its color is close to the HP bar's dark trough, so the two corners match.
- **The same plate behind the own frame** (portrait, HP bar, HP text) if, in any class's screenshot, the weapon or a forearm reaches under it.
- **Placement** (§2) prefers keeping the slots over the weapon's plain dark parts or clear of it. The plate is what guarantees readability, though, so the weapon isn't shrunk or moved only for this.

**Check** (stage 2): the `<class>-idle.png` and `<class>-fire.png` screenshots, and one per class with abilities on cooldown (`<class>-cooldown.png`), at 1 920 × 1 080 and 2 560 × 1 440:
- every slot's icon, key label and cooldown ring is fully visible and clearly readable against the weapon behind it;
- the ready glow and the cooldown dimming are clearly told apart;
- the same holds over the bright marble floor and the sky (look down, then up, in the arcade view).

If anything is hard to read, raise the plate's opacity up to 0.85 and record it. If that's not enough, stop and ask.

### 3.8 Hammers and the cylinder

So a shot feels like a gun firing, the hammer falls and the revolver's cylinder blurs as if it turned one chamber. Both layers are inside the weapon box, over the idle image (and the alt), so they follow its recoil, bob and sway. With `s = sinceShot / interval`:

- **The hammer** turns around its hinge, toward the muzzle, by `fall` degrees: **30° for the revolver, 25° for the launcher** (the sign is whichever moves its tip toward the muzzle; measure it in stage 1).
  - It **snaps down at the shot**, so the fall is the shot. Its angle is `fall` from s = 0 to s = 0.15, then eases back to 0 (cocked, as painted) by s = 0.45, with `(1 − p)²`, where *p* is the progress through that span.
  - The Heretic's recocks before the fresh censer fades in (0.5–0.8, §3.4).
  - Sacrament (no `shot()`) doesn't move it.
- **The cylinder blur** (the Betrayer, if kept) lies over the sharp cylinder.
  - Its opacity is 0 until s = 0.15, rises linearly to 1 at s = 0.22, holds to s = 0.35 and falls linearly to 0 at s = 0.45.
  - So the cylinder spins as the hammer recocks, like a double-action revolver, and is sharp again, the next chamber in place, before the next shot.
- Both work at any interval, so they keep up with a Field of Blood (0.15 s per revolver shot: the hammer is down for 22 ms).
- The Silver Bullet moves them the same way.

### 3.9 The healing censer

While the Heretic heals with Sacrament, the censer in its launcher glows green instead of orange, the green of every heal (M8 §1) and of the beam leaving its muzzle. So the weapon itself says "healing".

- **When:** while the Sacrament beam is drawn from the local player's muzzle (the condition `game.ts` already draws it under). A new `Hud.setHealing(on, now)` is called each frame with it.
- **Opacity** *h* of the green censer and its glow: eases toward 1 over 150 ms when the beam starts and toward 0 over 300 ms when it stops (linear).
- **Layers**, both in the weapon box, so they follow recoil, bob and sway:
  - the green censer over the idle image, at opacity *h* × (1 − *a*). An empty muzzle (§3.4: right after a censer was fired, the beam may start) shows no green coals;
  - the green glow with `screen`, at opacity *g* × *h* × (1 − *a*).
- **The orange glow** (the idle glow layer) is multiplied by (1 − *h*), so the orange doesn't tint the green.
- **No muzzle flash** for Sacrament, as today. The Field of Blood's red glow on the weapon stays as it is.

## 9. Out of scope

- Reloading, switching weapons, inspecting a weapon, or any animation beyond §3.
- 3D models (Meshy or otherwise) for the weapons. They'd pay off only for many-pose animations like reloads; a later milestone can render frames from them with the same manifest.
- Other players' weapons, the world sprites and the class icons.
- An idle breathing motion of the weapon (only the glow breathes).
- Tinting the weapon's color by the light (only its brightness).
- New sounds or a new muzzle flash image.
- Left-handed or field-of-view options.
