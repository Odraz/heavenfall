# Turn Meatgrinder's horde into a decision engine

Meatgrinder doesn't have a feel problem. It has a decision problem. Every framework and studio postmortem reviewed here agrees that a shooter stays fun while the player is still choosing (which target, which tool, where to stand) and learning a pattern that hasn't yet gone automatic. Both stop once one strategy dominates. In Meatgrinder that strategy is "keep moving, hold LMB", and the game currently pays for it: health never comes from enemies, the ranged enemies give nothing for the detour to kill them, a wave cycle lasts about 23 seconds with roughly 10 seconds of quiet, and the only build choice planned is three relics per run. The genre's fixes come from games that kept their core verbs and changed what surrounds them. Doom Eternal puts health and ammo inside the enemies and adds "chess piece" enemies with specific counters. Left 4 Dead adds specials that break the team's habits, along with real valleys and warning cues. Vampire Survivors, Hades and Risk of Rain 2 make a power decision every half-minute or so instead of every two minutes. All of these fit Meatgrinder's premise and its own rules. The cheapest high-value order is: make the horde a resource and the props breakable first, replace the synthesized combat sounds next, then add relics with a frequent stream of small visible upgrades, and only after that add new enemies that create priority and teamwork, with each new enemy confirmed before it's built. Relics alone won't fix it, and the developer is right about that. Every reference game runs three reward channels at once, and relics are only one of them.

## The frameworks agree: feel is solved, decisions are missing

The playtesters reported a feeling ("only running around shooting, holding the mouse button"), but the cause sits one layer lower. MDA says players feel aesthetics first, while designers can only change mechanics, and the dynamics in between are where dominant behaviours appear. The paper's own shooter example is that "weapons, ammunition and spawn points" produce camping and sniping ([MDA paper](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~hunicke/MDA.pdf)). In Meatgrinder, infinite ammo, a hold-to-fire primary, wading slow inside crowds and spawns from all sides together produce one dynamic: circle and hold. Sid Meier's test is blunter. A game is a series of interesting decisions, and Soren Johnson's summary of Meier adds that a choice only counts when it is **"both impactful and informed"** ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/pc/analysis-sid-meier-s-key-design-lessons)). If the answer to "what should I press now?" is LMB nearly every second, the decision rate is close to zero, however good the splatter is.

Raph Koster's model explains why the complaint appears even though the moment-to-moment game is satisfying. Fun is the brain learning a pattern, and once the pattern is chunked into routine it stops being fun ([Game Studies wiki summary](https://game-studies.fandom.com/wiki/A_Theory_of_Fun_for_Game_Design?oldid=4071)). One enemy mix across an 8-minute run is mastered within the first arena. Doom Eternal's creative director says the same in a designer's terms: **"When you're thinking, you're engaged; when you're not thinking, you're bored,"** and "if you're not prioritizing anything on the battlefield and just killing whatever's in front of you, that sucks" ([Shacknews: Hell Razer](https://www.shacknews.com/article/116635/hell-razer-the-making-of-doom-eternal?page=4)). Bungie's "30 seconds of fun", often quoted as praise for a tight core loop, meant the opposite of how it's usually used. Jaime Griesemer's point was that the 30 seconds must be *varied* by enemies, terrain and objectives so that no two stretches play the same ([Engadget interview](https://www.engadget.com/2011-07-14-half-minute-halo-an-interview-with-jaime-griesemer.html)).

Self-determination research gives the most useful map of what is present and what is missing. In-game competence and autonomy predicted enjoyment, and in online multiplayer, **relatedness independently predicted enjoyment and future play** ([Ryan, Rigby, Przybylski 2006](https://pure.ewha.ac.kr/en/publications/the-motivational-pull-of-video-games-a-self-determination-theory-/)). Meatgrinder's M12 work (burst deaths, kill flinches, shake, kill ladders) serves competence and sensation well, and the praise for atmosphere confirms it. "No choices" is autonomy failing, and four players each clearing their own side of an arena is relatedness failing. Swink's game-feel work explains why more juice won't help: polish makes the same mechanics "more perceptually convincing" but doesn't change what they ask of the player ([Designing Game Feel survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.09201)). The working conclusion is to stop tuning feel on the current loop and add decisions around it.

The playtest evidence is thin. Two friends and one session can't separate "this loop is shallow" from "this loop wasn't explained", and the friends' Borderlands remark is open to interpretation. In Quantic Foundry's motivation model it most plausibly points at unmet Power and Strategy motivations (growing stronger, meaningful builds) rather than literal loot ([Gamer Motivation Model reference](https://quanticfoundry.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/Gamer-Motivation-Model-Reference.pdf)). The diagnosis still holds, because every independent framework points at the same gap.

## Holding the trigger wins because the game pays for it

Doom 2016 had the same problem. Hugo Martin says most of its encounters could be beaten with the super shotgun, and **"if one weapon is unbalanced, that takes away from everything else"** ([Shacknews p.8](https://shacknews.com/article/116635/hell-razer-the-making-of-doom-eternal?page=8)). One critic's analysis says 2016 let players "converge to a 'workhorse' weapon that dealt with literally everything", and that Eternal fixed this by making ammo infinitely renewable through the chainsaw but capping it low ([Critical Points](https://critpoints.net/2020/06/07/doom-eternal-reviews-ft-s-g-s)). The useful lesson isn't ammo. It's the *coupling* id built: push-forward combat rejects regenerating health and cover, so **the player can only recover by engaging enemies** ([GDC 2018 talk listing](https://gdconf.com/news/see-doom-devs-break-push-forward-combat-design-gdc-2018)). Glory kills drop health, bigger demons drop more, and demons deliberately aim worse at fast-moving players ([Game Developer: the AI of DOOM 2016](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/cyber-demons-the-ai-of-doom-2016-)). Ultrakill takes this furthest: health comes from blood sprayed at close range, so backing off and not healing are the same thing ([Wikipedia: Ultrakill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrakill)). Boltgun shows the failure case. Its health pickups are scattered around the level, and a reviewer complained that searching for them pulls you out of the action ([GameSpot](https://Gamespot.Com/videos/warhammer-40000-boltgun-video-review/2300-6461438/)).

Meatgrinder rewards the safe place. Health comes back through solo regeneration during the director's relax, the Heretic, and a full heal when an arena clears ([m12.md §2.5](../docs/m12.md)). None of it comes from enemies. The just-specced backpedal slow ([m12-followup-2.md §1](../docs/m12-followup-2.md)) is a sound counter to kiting, but it's a penalty, and Doom Eternal's own reception is a warning there: players called its hard resource pressure "clunky" and "prescriptive" ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/782330/discussions/0/3881596897256675092)). Positive pull works better. The cheapest version is a **mote**, a small visible pickup dropped by kills made close up or with an ability, that heals a little when collected. Cherubs and Choristers would drop a bigger one, so killing them is worth the detour. This also answers playtest 3's separate finding that solo play lacks healing ([2026-10-09.md](../docs/playtests/2026-10-09.md)). It doesn't break the "one visible effect per ability" rule, because a mote is a world object, not a hidden ability rider. Its real cost is to the Heretic's identity as the healer, so motes should stay small enough that the Heretic's burst heals and Shroud remain the team's answer to a bad moment.

The second half of Eternal's fix is the roster. Martin's encounter test is a free playtest metric: **"You know you've got an encounter dialed in right when the player is running by certain enemies to kill another one"** ([Shacknews p.4](https://www.shacknews.com/article/116635/hell-razer-the-making-of-doom-eternal?page=4)). Fatshark gives each enemy category a job: hordes are "fun to kill in masses", elites "break your flow, demand your immediate attention", and specials "enforce and challenge teamplay" ([Darktide Hybrid Combat blog](https://www.playdarktide.com/news/dev-blog-hybrid-combat); [Darktide 101 blog](https://forums.fatsharkgames.com/t/darktide-101-enemy-design-and-prototyping-dev-blog/98452)). Meatgrinder has fodder (Blessed) and two ranged harassers (Cherubs, Choristers). It has no elite and no special. Left 4 Dead built each special to break one specific dominant behaviour. The Boomer exists to "break the rule of 'shoot everything that moves', forcing players to think a bit before firing" ([Booth, GDC 2009](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/GDC2009_ReplayableCooperativeGameDesign_Left4Dead.pdf)), which is close to a design brief for "holding the mouse button". The scope rule still applies, though. Serious Sam shows that simple enemies carry huge hordes when their *combinations* demand different responses and each announces itself by sound ([Giant Bomb](https://giantbomb.com/reviews/serious-sam-the-first-encounter-review)). So the order is: sharpen the existing three into a priority triangle with rewards first, and add a new type only after that playtest and with explicit confirmation.

## Twenty-three-second wave cycles leave no valleys, and nothing to do in them

Valve's pacing work is the most concrete source here. **"Constant, unchanging combat is fatiguing. Long periods of inactivity are boring."** The L4D director builds up, holds the peak for 3–5 s, fades, then relaxes for **30–45 s** with no mobs or specials ([Booth, AI Systems of L4D](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)). Meatgrinder's director already copies this structure, but its numbers are compressed. By the spec's own arithmetic, a wave cycle is about 23 s with about 10 s without contact, and an arena runs 90–120 s across four or five such cycles ([m12.md §2.3](../docs/m12.md)). A pulse that fast feels nearly flat, so the run reads as one long fight.

Longer valleys only help if there is something to do in them. Otherwise the result is "not much to do" in a different form. Deep Rock Galactic's balance of "quieter moments where players can freely explore" against holding out under a swarm ([Frostilyte](https://frostilyte.ca/2021/08/04/mini-review-deep-rock-galactic/)) works because the quiet has an activity attached. Meatgrinder's version answers the playtesters' wish to break stuff: **breakable vases, urns and reliquaries that spill motes**, plus explosive props (oil censers, Doom's barrel in this game's theme) that chain-react through a crowd. Doom's barrel has been on the map since E1M1, with a blast radius and chain reactions in clusters ([DoomWiki](https://doomwiki.org/wiki/Barrel)). The feedback from failed breakables is clear: pots that pay nothing become a chore, and objects that look smashable but don't break disappoint players ([itch.io](https://itch.io/post/7693460); [itch.io](https://itch.io/post/16579353)). In this engine a breakable is a billboard with HP that the existing hit tests can already hit, so it is cheap.

Anticipation costs even less and is mostly missing beyond the golden spawn rays. Booth: **"If an event is exciting, it will be more so if it broadcasts its impending arrival."** His examples include the Tank's music and footsteps, the Witch sobbing, and car alarms with their "double anticipation: of not setting it off, and of the mob that comes when you do" ([Booth GDC 2009](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/GDC2009_ReplayableCooperativeGameDesign_Left4Dead.pdf)). Vermintide's war drums announce a Chaos horde ([Dread Central](https://www.dreadcentral.com/editorials/272672/interview-jesper-kyd-on-vermintide-ii-soundtrack/)), and Deep Rock's Mission Control gives about 20 s of warning before a swarm ([DRG wiki](https://deeprockgalactic.wiki.gg/wiki/Swarm/WIP)). A choir swell from the direction of the next wave, a few seconds before its rays appear, turns "more enemies" into an event. A breakable "alarm bell" reliquary that pays well but summons a wave is the car alarm in this setting. It puts a real choice into the valley, and it uses existing director calls rather than a new enemy. Booth's last rule is to deal these moments at random rather than script them: "Low probability + High drama = Memorable", and designers should resist making every moment happen every run ([Booth GDC 2009](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/GDC2009_ReplayableCooperativeGameDesign_Left4Dead.pdf)).

## Four players clearing four sides is parallel play

Left 4 Dead's co-op principles are explicit. Make cooperation **"the only winning strategy"**, treat the whole team as the player, punish abandoning the team, and use no invisible leashes or out-of-bounds damage. Its specials create helpless states (a pounce, a tongue pull) that "give players near the incapacitated victim the opportunity to be the hero", because "players really enjoy helping each other" ([Booth GDC 2009](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/GDC2009_ReplayableCooperativeGameDesign_Left4Dead.pdf)). Fatshark adds the rule that makes this workable with bots and in solo play: every special can be **"clutched"** by one skilled player, because "not allowing a single player to survive alone would also result in a more toxic gameplay loop". It also warns that "getting charged constantly got too tiring", so disablers need pauses ([Darktide 101 blog](https://forums.fatsharkgames.com/t/darktide-101-enemy-design-and-prototyping-dev-blog/98452)).

Meatgrinder already has the start of this. Wading turns a crowd into a soft pin, and Blasphemy's stun lets a swarmed ally walk out ([classes.md](../docs/classes.md)). Today being caught is a solo punishment, and L4D's lesson is to turn it into a rescue opportunity. The playtest notes support this directly: bots "revive reliably", which made difficulty impossible to judge ([2026-10-09-mp.md](../docs/playtests/2026-10-09-mp.md)). If nothing ever pins a player, nothing ever needs a teammate. Two levers fit the premise. The first is a **disabler with a clutch escape**, a Blessed variant that leaps on the player farthest from the group (the Hunter's stated purpose) and holds them until a teammate hits it or the victim uses their dash or leap. Bots would need a "free the pinned teammate" priority, which Valve calls a "difficult issue" but solved by having bots prioritize humans ([Booth AIIDE 2009](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)). This is a new enemy and needs confirmation. The second is **class tools that answer another player's problem**, Deep Rock's interdependence through utility rather than damage. Its director built classes so that "if you're missing one class, you always feel like you're missing out on some mechanics" ([Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/deep-rock-galactic-interview-season-1-co-op-design-battle-pass-progression/)). The Fallen's taunt pulling a crowd or a disabler off an ally, and the Binder's chains or the Betrayer's piercing shot clearing a pin from range, fit the existing kits without new verbs.

Automatic callouts are the cheapest relatedness tool and suit a game where humans often play beside bots without voice chat. Valve credits its barks ("Hunter's got Zoey!", "Thanks for that") with both situational awareness and "a baseline of camaraderie" ([Booth GDC 2009](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/GDC2009_ReplayableCooperativeGameDesign_Left4Dead.pdf)). Text barks in the existing chat line ("Binder is pinned!", "Wave from the bridge!") cost days, not weeks. Friendly fire, Helldivers' answer to positioning and comedy ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/helldivers-2/friendly-fire)), is a poor fit for 1000-enemy AoE with bots and should be skipped.

## Three relics per run is a tenth of the genre's decision rate

The roguelite reference games put a power decision in front of the player far more often than once per arena. Vampire Survivors' first level-ups cost 5, 15 and 25 XP, so the first several choices come almost immediately ([VS Wiki](https://vampire.survivors.wiki/w/Level_up)). Hades offers three-boon choices throughout a run, themed by god so the player knows what *kind* of effect they're choosing before reading the card ([Wikipedia: Hades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hades_(video_game))). Risk of Rain 2 scatters gold-bought chests across every stage, with items that stack without a cap ([Wikipedia: RoR2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_of_Rain_2)). Brotato runs a shop after every wave ([Brotato Wiki](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Shop)). Together they show three channels running at once. The first is a frequent, small, low-stakes stream: level-ups, chests, shop items. The second is a less frequent, identity-defining pick: god boons, boss items. The third is an event or risk structure that hands out the big rewards, such as RoR2's player-triggered teleporter climax. Three relics per run cover only the second. A reasonable target for an 8-minute run is **some visible "my kit changed" event every 30–60 seconds**. No studio has published that interval; it is extrapolated from these games' rhythms.

The developer's rules shape the frequent channel. Stacking "+7% damage" is invisible in a 1000-enemy horde and breaks "one visible effect". What works is **countable, visible increments of one attack**: one more shotgun pellet, one more chain target, one more pierce on the Silver Bullet, a wider censer cloud. These drop from breakables and heavies and apply on pickup, so there's no menu, because a four-player real-time game can't pause the way Vampire Survivors does. Borderlands supplies the readability principle. Gearbox's brief was to "make the weapons look like the way they're gonna play", and coloured elemental numbers let "the players know exactly what's going on" ([Killscreen](https://www.killscreen.com/interview-gearbox-software-explains-their-love-guns-and-borderlands-2/)). Diablo supplies the excitement. Its designers call loot a slot machine, "every time that you killed a monster, you were pulling that lever", and gave rare drops their own sound so that "something cool has dropped" ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/why-video-game-loot-is-so-addictive-according-to-the-c-1846695147)). Diablo 3's Loot 2.0 shows the limit: fewer, better, class-relevant drops beat a flood of junk ([Game-Wisdom](https://game-wisdom.com/critical/loot-2-0-and-diablos-fourth-revival)). For Meatgrinder that means colour-coded drops, a distinct sound per tier, and every drop usable by the class that picks it up.

Relics then become the identity channel they were designed to be: one big visible change to one attack, picked from three in the breather after an arena. They should be grouped into a few readable families, Hades-style, so a pick can be predicted and pairs can combine. Back 4 Blood is the cautionary tale. A reviewer called its card system "a massive overcomplication of Left4Dead's simple and elegant multiplayer fun" ([Bit-tech](https://bit-tech.net/reviews/back4blood-review/1)). Keep the option count small. The cheapest version of the third channel needs no new threat. A **"Hoarder"**, the Treasure Goblin pattern, is a fleeing creature that drops loot "like a pinata as soon as you start hitting it" and escapes if not killed quickly ([BlizzardWatch](https://blizzardwatch.com/2021/02/01/diablo-immortal-treasure-goblins)). It adds a "drop everything and chase" decision without an attack pattern. An optional altar in each arena that raises the danger for a better relic adds a risk you choose to take, in the spirit of RoR2 shrines and Hades' Pact of Punishment ([Wikipedia: Hades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hades_(video_game))). Meta progression can wait. If added, it should unlock variety into the pool rather than permanent power, so friends at different levels stay matched. That is a reasoned inference; no source studies it in co-op.

## The sounds annoy because of their timbre, not their volume

The code rules out the usual causes. The mixer already caps voices at 24 and four per sound, steals by priority with the player's weapon first, varies pitch by ±5%, runs a limiter, and folds mass kills into one scaled sound ([audio.ts](../src/audio/audio.ts); [m12.md §4.3](../docs/m12.md)). That covers what Frostbite's HDR mixing and id's "weapons are never ducked" rule recommend ([DICE slides](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/adaptive-mixing-in-frostbite/3128152); [ADSR: Doom sound design](https://www.adsrsounds.com/news/the-sound-design-of-doom-exclusive-qa/)). What remains is the material. Every effect is rendered **once** from oscillators and noise. The shotgun is a noise burst plus a 5-bit-crushed square wave ([sfx.ts](../src/audio/sfx.ts)), and the Binder's Chain Gun repeats its one buffer 12 times a second. Practitioners describe a sharp transient without a body as "thin and toy-like" ([Violet Recording](https://violetrecording.com/how-to-design-weapon-sounds/)). Pitch jitter alone "is not convincingly natural", and a small set of takes is "quickly exhausted, causing ear fatigue" ([USPTO 10453434](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10453434)). The recorded music makes the synthesized effects stand out more. id recorded "all kinds of meats, vegetables and strange gooey things" for Doom's gore ([ADSR](https://www.adsrsounds.com/news/the-sound-design-of-doom-exclusive-qa/)), and flesh, porcelain, metal and feathers are exactly the materials this game's kills are made of.

The architecture already expects the fix: each sound is a named entry "so a file can replace one later". Replace the weapons, the burst pop, the mass kill, the impacts and the new pottery smashes with **layered recorded samples**: transient, body and a short tail, three to six variants per layer, played from a shuffle bag. Usable sources are the Sonniss GDC bundles (royalty-free, no attribution, no redistribution as raw SFX, no AI training), Kenney (CC0), and Freesound filtered to CC0 ([Sonniss licence](https://sonniss.com/gdc-bundle-license); [Freesound FAQ](https://freesound.org/help/faq/)). Ship Opus with an AAC fallback chosen by a test decode, because Safari's decodeAudioData has been inconsistent with Opus ([WebKit bug 226922](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=226922)). Keep a licence file per sound, and keep raw licensed files out of any public repository.

The weak character sprites are real but secondary. Polish multiplies the appeal of a loop with decisions and can't create one ([Swink, via Liz England](https://lizengland.com/blog/review-game-feel-by-steve-swink/)), and the planned Meshy sprite work can stay where it is. One part can't wait: any new elite, disabler or Hoarder has to read by **silhouette and sound** in a crowd of a thousand billboards. Fatshark calls clear audio and visual cues for specials "crucial" ([Darktide 101 blog](https://forums.fatsharkgames.com/t/darktide-101-enemy-design-and-prototyping-dev-blog/98452)). Art spending should go first to the enemies players must choose to kill.

## Conclusion

The most useful shift is to stop treating "boring" as too little content or too little juice, and treat it as a reward placed in the wrong spot. Every lever with good evidence behind it costs little in this engine. A mote, a breakable vase or a barrel is a billboard with HP. A longer relax and a warning swell are director constants and one sound. A visible +1 pellet is a number the attack already reads. The expensive levers, new enemy behaviours and objectives, matter less until those cheap ones show whether players will leave the safe ring once going in pays off. If they still circle and hold LMB after that, the cause is the enemy roster rather than the incentives, and that is the evidence to bring to the new-enemy decision.

One tension deserves an explicit decision rather than drift: drops and kill-based healing reduce the Heretic's monopoly on sustain. The research favours putting sustain inside the horde. The class design favours a healer who matters. A small heal per mote with the Heretic owning burst recovery satisfies both. That is a design call for the developer and shouldn't be settled during implementation.

## Roadmap

Costs assume AI-assisted implementation: **S** = a few days, **M** = one to two weeks. Each step has one main playtest question. Change values boldly between playtests ("double it or cut it by half", per Meier via [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/pc/analysis-sid-meier-s-key-design-lessons)) so the effect is visible.

| # | Step | What it contains | Cost | Playtest question it answers | Needs scope confirmation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The horde pays you to go in** | A mote pickup system (small heal from close-range and ability kills, bigger from Cherubs and Choristers; magnet pull, colour, soft sound). Breakable vases and urns that spill motes. Oil censers that explode and chain through crowds. The relax lengthened to roughly 15–25 s. A directional choir swell before each wave. | M | Do players leave the safe ring to dive in and to chase Cherubs, and does the share of time spent holding LMB fall? Did anyone smash things for fun? | Breakables and barrels: yes (a new feature, though not an enemy) |
| 2 | **Recorded combat sounds** | Layered samples with variants for every weapon, burst, mass kill, impact and smash. Chain Gun first (12 plays a second). Licence file. Opus plus AAC. Can run in parallel with step 1. | S–M | Are the sounds still annoying? (Have players rank them; see the checklist) | No |
| 3 | **Choices every half-minute** | Relics as planned (pick 1 of 3 after each arena, grouped in families). Plus auto-applied, countable upgrades of one attack (+1 pellet, +1 chain target, +1 pierce) dropped by breakables and heavies, using step 1's pickup system. Results screen shows the build. | M | "When did you last make a choice you weren't sure was right?" Can players name the build they ended with? | Relics already planned. The upgrade stream: yes |
| 4 | **A target worth the detour** | A Hoarder that flees, sheds loot when hit and has no attack. Optionally an arena altar that raises the danger for a better relic. | S–M | Do players run past Blessed to kill something else (Martin's test)? | Yes (a new enemy, even without an attack) |
| 5 | **Needing each other** | A disabler Blessed that pins the player farthest from the group, with a solo clutch escape (dash, leap, a well-timed attack), a pause between pounces, and bot priority to free pinned teammates. Class "save" uses (taunt pulls it off, a piercing shot frees from range). Text barks for pins and waves. | M | "Was there a moment you needed a teammate?" Count help events. | Yes (a new enemy and a new player state) |
| 6 | **Something to do besides kill** | One arena objective (destroy shrines, or hold a ritual circle) that triggers the director's build-up. | S–M | Is "not much to do" gone? Does the team split and regroup? | Yes |

Deferred on purpose: ultimates and halos (M13), Meshy sprites (except silhouettes for new enemies), found weapons that replace class guns (they conflict with distinctive class guns), friendly fire, meta-progression power, a full trader shop. Steps 1 and 2 together are the strongest single playtest build. They address four of the five complaints ("holding the mouse", "break stuff", "annoying sounds", and partly "not much to do") without touching the enemy roster.

## Post-playtest checklist

Observe silently first. Valve's rule is "what people do, not what they say" ([Valve playtesting, GDC 2009](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/GDC2009_ValvesApproachToPlaytesting.pdf)). Then survey each player alone before any group talk.

- [ ] Record the bot count, and play one run with humans only.
- [ ] **LMB share:** roughly what fraction of the time was LMB held? Were Q/E pressed as soon as ready, or saved for a moment? (Log per-input damage share. If one input deals more than about 80% of damage, it dominates. That threshold is a heuristic.)
- [ ] **Dominant pattern:** does movement collapse into a ring or a backpedal, and how many minutes in?
- [ ] **Martin's test:** did anyone run past Blessed to kill a Cherub, Chorister or Hoarder?
- [ ] **Near-misses and help events:** count close calls and saves. Zero of either means challenge or interdependence is missing.
- [ ] **Spontaneous speech:** callouts, laughs and swears in the second half of the run. Silence there is the clearest boredom signal.
- [ ] **Breakables:** were they smashed during the valleys without prompting? Did the payoff feel worth it?
- [ ] Ask each player alone: "Name one moment you'd tell a friend about." "When did you last make a choice you weren't sure was right?" "Was there a moment you needed a teammate?" "When did it start to feel the same?" (Ask for an arena or a minute.)
- [ ] Forced ranking: the four classes, then each class's LMB, RMB, Q and E, from most to least liked. Then each enemy rated 1–7 for "made me change what I was doing". Then the five most frequent sounds ranked from most to least annoying.
- [ ] Before the next build, write down the single hypothesis being tested and the bold change that tests it.
