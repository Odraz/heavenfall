# The Nine Heavens

The levels after the Pearly Gates. This is a living design document, like [lore.md](lore.md) and [classes.md](classes.md): it records intent, not exact rules, and it's still in progress. Milestone specs turn it into exact rules. Numbers are starting points.

## The idea

Heaven's levels mirror Dante's *Inferno*. Each of Dante's circles punishes a sin; each of our heavens enforces the opposite virtue, perfected into tyranny.

Dante's Hell ends in ice, not fire: at its bottom Satan is frozen in a lake where nothing moves. Our Heaven ends the same way. **The closer to the Throne, the colder, paler and stiller Heaven gets**, from warm gold to white ice. Heaven's center and Hell's center turn out to be the same place: perfect stillness, the end of free will.

**The trumpets.** Heavens 1–7 each hold one of the seven trumpets. After the seventh breaks, Heaven stops announcing the Judgment and carries it out quietly: heaven 8 is erasing names from the Book of Life, and heaven 9 is the Throne.

## Design rules

- **Every addition serves fun, repeatable play first, theme second.** A level feature, enemy or boss mechanic must create a decision or a skill the party can get better at, and play differently each run.
- **Every level gives the damned something of Heaven's to turn against it**: a hazard, a prop, a rule.
- **Each new enemy gives the party a new job** (see the table below). One readable rule per enemy, like the class rules in [classes.md](classes.md). Every class can still handle a pure swarm; an enemy may reward a class but never require one.
- **Each class gets a homecoming level**: the Fallen at the Inner Wall (heaven 5), the Heretic Saint at Orthodoxy (6), the Binder and the Betrayer at the Throne (9).

## The heavens

### 1. The Pearly Gates (Limbo): built
- **Look:** ivory, gold and sky blue at golden hour.
- **Enemies:** Blessed (melee swarm), Choristers (ground ranged), Cherubs (flying ranged).
- **Boss:** the Gatekeeper. Judgment hits everyone who can see it: hide. Trumpet 1.

### 2. The Heaven of Chastity (Lust)
Dante's lovers are blown together through an endless storm. Here the air is dead still and nobody touches.
- **Look:** rose and lavender silk, pearl glass; statues of lovers reaching toward each other through glass.
- **Level feature: the Partitions.** Every 20 s, after a 2 s glow, glass walls rise from the floor and split the arena into cells for 8 s. The party regroups, or gathers before they rise. Built on the doors.
- **New enemy: the Chaperone.** Tethers one player and drags them away from the party. The dragged player can still shoot; damaging the Chaperone breaks the tether. The Fallen's intercept moment: leap in and cut them loose.
- **Boss: the Twin Wardens.** Two veiled angels at opposite ends of the arena. If one dies, the other raises it after 10 s: the party splits and kills both within 10 s. Trumpet 2.

### 3. The Heaven of Temperance (Gluttony)
Dante's gluttons lie in filthy rain. Here: an endless banquet where every plate is empty.
- **Look:** white linen, silver, candlelight; tables running to the horizon.
- **Level feature: the Cloches.** About 12 silver dish covers per arena. Shooting one heals whoever broke it: the forbidden food. When to take one is the decision.
- **New enemy: the Multiplied.** The loaves and fishes turned against you: killed, it splits in two, then 4, then 8. Rewards area damage; wastes a Silver Bullet.
- **Boss: Grace, the three-mouthed Cherub** (Cerberus in reverse). Each head says grace and has its own attack: an orb stream, a bite cone, a summoning hymn. Heads can be broken in any order, and each order plays differently. Trumpet 3.

### 4. The Heaven of Poverty (Greed)
Dante's misers and wasters roll great weights into each other forever.
- **Look:** pale gold vaults; everything people ever loved, kept under glass: toys, letters, wedding rings.
- **Level feature: the Weights.** Giant golden spheres roll along tracks, crash in the middle and roll back, hitting players and enemies alike. Lure or pull the swarm into a track.
- **New enemy: the Vaultguard.** A shield like a vault door blocks everything from the front, and the swarm advances behind it. Flank it, launch it or pull it.
- **Boss: the Collector.** Every enemy that dies within 15 m of it becomes gold armor on it: fight the swarm away from it. A Weight rolled into it shatters the armor. Trumpet 4.

### 5. The Heaven of Serenity (Wrath)
Dante's wrathful fight in the swamp of the Styx. Here souls are calmed into nothing on a mirror-flat lake.
- **Look:** a silver-blue lake reflecting the sky, white lotus, mist. At its end stands the Inner Wall (Dante's walls of Dis), the Fallen's old post.
- **Level feature: the Still Water.** Wading slows everyone, and Blessed rise from the water anywhere, not only at spawn points. The lotus islands are safe ground.
- **New enemy: the Soother.** Its lullaby halves the fire rate of players within 8 m (a blue haze on screen): Field of Blood in reverse, and the first target to hunt.
- **Boss: the Serene.** Casts *"Be still"* (Psalm 46:10): anyone moving when the 3 s cast ends is hit. The party stops and lets the swarm come; Blasphemy and Chains carry it through. A Judgment dodged by timing instead of cover. Trumpet 5.

### 6. The Heaven of Orthodoxy (Heresy)
Dante's heretics burn in open tombs. Here doubters are sealed in cold marble. The Heretic Saint's homecoming.
- **Look:** a cathedral-library, shelves 16 m high, sealed books; sarcophagi glowing red through their cracks, Hell's fire underneath.
- **Level feature: the Tombs.** Breaking a sarcophagus releases Hell's fire, burning everything around it for 6 s. A bomb the party sets off; timing is the skill.
- **New enemy: the Inquisitor.** Casts a Verdict: a gold circle under a player that ignites 2 s later. Keeps the party moving inside the swarm.
- **Boss: the Grand Inquisitor.** Takes triple damage while burning: lure it beside a Tomb and break it. Heaven's inquisitor burns in Hell's fire. Trumpet 6.

### 7. The Heaven of Peace (Violence)
Dante's centaurs guard a river of boiling blood and a forest of suicides.
- **Look:** a garden of white marble trees, a river of milk, perfect lawns.
- **Level feature: the Groves.** Nobody inside a grove deals or takes damage. A place to breathe, but enemies shelter there too: knock them out, pull them out.
- **New enemy: the Peacekeeper.** A mounted lancer, after Dante's centaurs. Marks a line on the ground, then charges along it. Dodge it, or bait it into a wall, where it's stunned.
- **Boss: the Lamb** with seven horns (Revelation 5:6). Walks slowly through the garden carrying a grove around itself. Its horns can be shot from outside; each broken horn shrinks the grove. When the last breaks, the Lamb stops being peaceful. Trumpet 7, the last.

After it: *"The seventh trumpet is silent."* Then the twist card: *"Heaven has stopped announcing. The Judgment is being written instead."*

### 8. The Archive (Fraud): no trumpet
Dante's liars fill ten ditches. Here every thought anyone ever had is on record, and Heaven is quietly erasing names from the Book of Life.
- **Look:** vellum and ink, glass galleries, text floating in the air; the color starts draining from the world.
- **Level feature: the Erasure.** Every 20 s a band of floor at the arena's edge goes blank and disappears: the arena shrinks, so fight faster.
- **New enemy: the Eraser.** Ignores the living and goes for the souls of dead players. An erased soul can't be revived until the arena is cleared. Protecting a fallen friend is the drama.
- **Boss: the Recording Angel.** Replays the record: beams strike wherever each player stood 5 s earlier. Don't retrace your steps.

### 9. The Throne (Treachery): no trumpet
Dante's frozen lake, Cocytus, has four zones (Caina, Antenora, Ptolomea, Judecca); our four arenas take one each. The giants chained around it are the Binder's work; Judas at its bottom is the Betrayer's mirror.
- **Look:** white on white and ice blue; obedient angels frozen in the ice like statues; the music fades toward silence.
- **Level feature: Ice.** Everyone slides, players and enemies. Knockbacks carry three times as far: the Fallen shines, and enemies fly off ledges.
- **New enemy: the Ophan,** Ezekiel's burning wheel covered in eyes. Rolls in straight lines and bounces off walls like a billiard ball; shot by the Fallen's shotgun, it bowls through the swarm.
- **Boss: the Seraph of the Throne,** mirroring Dante's Satan: six wings, three faces. Each wingbeat blows everyone across the ice. The three faces take turns; the active face's gaze freezes anyone it sees, so cover depends on where it looks. Behind it the Throne stays veiled in light; the game never shows who sits there.

## The party's jobs

| Enemy | Heaven | The party's job |
|---|---|---|
| Chaperone | 2 | rescue a teammate |
| Multiplied | 3 | kill a crowd |
| Vaultguard | 4 | get around a shield |
| Soother | 5 | find and kill a priority target |
| Inquisitor | 6 | keep moving |
| Peacekeeper | 7 | dodge or bait a charge |
| Eraser | 8 | protect the dead |
| Ophan | 9 | turn a hazard into a weapon |

## Cost

- **Reuses what exists:** the Partitions (doors), the Tombs' fire (the incense cloud), the Groves (floor zones), the Still Water (a property on floor cells).
- **New technology:** breakable props (Cloches, Tombs); moving hazards (Weights, Ophanim); the floor changing during play (the Erasure, the biggest risk: flow fields, line of sight and the terrain mesh all change mid-fight); sliding movement on Ice (medium risk: it touches every client's local movement).
- Ten levels of art is a lot; M10 took a milestone for one. The heavens are built in order, as many as there's time for.
