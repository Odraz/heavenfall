# V1 Foundations and light

**Status: planned** (drafted 2026-10-25). Dependencies: R9 accepted and merged. Rules and budgets: [spec.md](spec.md). Tomas's side: [guide.md](guide.md#v1).

## Playable outcome

The same levels, characters and effects, in a new light: warm sun and cool shade with more range, glowing highlights (halos, orbs, lava, muzzle flashes) that bloom softly, a warm graded image matched to the concept, and walls that darken into their corners. Under it, the game draws less: only the zones the camera can see, from compressed textures. A **Graphics** setting (Low / Medium / High) appears in Settings.

Before/after: the `arena` view looks like today's left half with the concept's light and color, but still today's flat walls. On Low it looks like today and runs at least as fast.

Out of scope: new geometry (V2), new characters (V3), new effects (V4), props (V5). The HUD stays (D7).

## Frame-time share

Post chain: High ≤ +15%, Medium ≤ +8% of the baseline. Culling and compression should bring Low **below** the baseline; the saving is what V2 spends.

## Ordered stages

### 1. Baseline and tools

- Add the `look` view group (spec §9) to `scripts/views.json` with rogue seeds, the HUD hidden, and an output folder per build; capture `screenshots/look/baseline-*.png`.
- Save the screenshots Tomas repaints into concepts: `assets/art-src/reference/look-today-{corridor,terrace,refiner,gate}.png` (1 920 × 1 080, no HUD). Tell Tomas they're ready (guide step 1.2).
- `src/render/quality.ts`: the tier table (spec §6) as data; the Graphics setting in the Settings view, stored per browser; `?quality=low|medium|high` for tests, views and the benchmark (`--quality`).
- The render budget end-to-end test (spec §5 *Measuring*) with today's numbers as the recorded baseline counts.
- Record the baseline counts (calls, triangles, texture MB, download MB, level build ms, Start to control) in the V1 report. The frame-time baseline is a benchmark: ask Tomas to run or allow it (recommended now, before anything changes).

Tests: tier parsing and persistence; the budget test runs.

### 2. Compressed textures

- Encode sprite atlases, terrain layers, the sky, clouds and effect sheets to KTX2 (UASTC for sprites and anything with sharp edges or alpha; ETC1S allowed for sky and clouds), with mipmaps generated at encode time. Keep the PNG/JPEG next to each as the fallback.
- Load through `KTX2Loader` (the Basis transcoder in `public/`, workers as three provides); `textureArray.ts` builds compressed array textures; fall back per file when the browser lacks BC7/ASTC/ETC2.
- `renderStats()` counts real bytes per pixel.
- Low skips the top mip of 2 048 px surfaces at load (spec §6: Low uses 1 024 px).

Tests: loader picks KTX2 and falls back (forced off by a test flag); atlas frames still map to the same pixels (UV rounding with block-aligned sizes); budget test shows the memory drop. End-to-end: loading suite.

### 3. Portal culling

- Split the terrain build (`terrain.ts`) into chunks: one per zone (M10 §3.1 zones), plus each door's frame and leaves with the door. Painted arcades, relief, crowns and windows go into the chunk of the zone they face.
- `src/render/portals.ts`: at each frame, the camera's zone, then every zone behind an **open** door whose rectangle is inside the current clip region, recursively, narrowing the region at each door. Closed doors stop the walk. Cherubs and projectiles are billboards and aren't culled by this (they're cheap and already bounded).
- Contact shadows, atmosphere cards and the sky stay as they are.

Tests: conservativeness (spec §4.1) on all 24 templates' generated floors at fixed seeds; doors opening and closing change the set; per-frame cost bound (no allocation per frame). End-to-end: the budget test shows triangles drawn per view falling.

**Recommended benchmark point** (ask Tomas): culling and compression together, Low vs baseline.

### 4. HDR render path

- Medium and High render the scene into a `WebGLRenderTarget` (RGBA half float, MSAA per tier), then a composite pass to the canvas. Low keeps today's direct path.
- Audit every custom shader for output space (terrain, sky, atmosphere, billboards, contact shadows, particles, item areas, blood pools, gate): they output linear HDR into the target; the composite converts to sRGB once. Today's look is the calibration target: with grading off and exposure 1, a gray card, the floor and an ivory enemy must come out within ΔE 3 of baseline screenshots (a test on rendered pixels in fixed views).
- Contact shadows keep their "darkest value in alpha" trick in the target's alpha channel (M10 §5.4); the composite ignores alpha.
- Tone mapping: compare AgX and ACES on the look views; keep the one that matches the concept's warm highlights without washing ivory to white; record the choice.
- First-person weapon (DOM): unchanged here; stage 6 matches it to the grade.

Tests: the calibration test above; Low path unchanged (pixel compare with baseline).

### 5. Bloom

- Bright pass from the resolved target above a threshold on luminance; mip chain (spec §6 per tier), a 13-tap downsample and tent upsample; added in the composite.
- Give emissive sources HDR intensity so they bloom and walls don't: halos, Chorister orbs, Gatekeeper eye, lava, fire, muzzle flash, ability glows (multipliers per source in one table). The sky's sun area may bloom lightly; clouds and ivory walls must not.
- Readability: bloom must not merge a swarm of ivory enemies into a glow. The `swarm-far` view is inspected at every change.

Tests: threshold keeps a lit ivory wall under it (rendered-pixel test); bloom off on Low.

### 6. Color grade

- Fit a 32³ lookup table from today's left half to the concept's right half (`look-today-arena.png` → `look-concept-arena.png`), and from each later concept pair: a smooth, regularized color transfer, fitted on large areas (floor, walls, sky) only. Store as a PNG strip in `assets/textures/look/grade.png`; expose exposure, saturation and warmth as three numbers in one data file for fine tuning.
- Vignette (Medium, High) and fine film grain (High), both subtle.
- The first-person weapon's light tint (`lightTint` in `fpWeapon.ts`) gets a CSS filter that approximates the grade, judged side by side.
- Readability test (spec §10): state colors, enemy palette and the ivory-on-floor/sky contrast through the LUT.

### 7. Light bake upgrade

- Colored lightmap (RGB instead of a luminance tint), same 4 texels per meter: sun warmth, sky bounce (cooler shade, slightly blue facing the open sky), and **static glow**: Refiner's Fire channels, braziers, sanctuary lamps (unbroken), stained-glass windows' colored pools. Each glow source is a small table entry (color, radius, intensity).
- Vertical faces: ambient occlusion at wall feet and inner corners in vertex colors; a stronger warm/cool split by facing.
- Billboards and contact shadows take the colored light (still per vertex, never darker than M10 §5.4's floor).
- Light shafts and dust motes: brighter, more of them where a window or arcade faces the sun, within M10's counts.

Tests: lightmap bake deterministic and within the level build budget; glow falls off within its radius; darkest floor rule.

## Play checklist

1. Settings → Graphics: play one room on each of Low, Medium, High. Low should feel no slower than before.
2. In the `arena` room on High: do halos, orbs and the lava glow? Does anything glow that shouldn't (walls, clouds)?
3. In a swarm: are ivory enemies still clear against floor and sky? Can you still tell slowed (violet), bound (red-hot chains) and censed (rusty gold) enemies apart?
4. Walk through two doors quickly: does any wall or floor pop in or flicker at the door (culling)?
5. Compare the screenshot pairs in the report with the concepts: is the color right, too warm, too saturated?
