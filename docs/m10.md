# Milestone 10 Specification: Beautiful levels

The levels look dull next to the concept art (`assets/art-src/reference/concept-1.webp` to `concept-3.webp`). Every level is a sealed box: every wall is 16 m tall, so from the middle of a 40 m arena the walls hide everything below about 36° above the horizon. Above them is a flat gradient sky, and the walls themselves are four repeating textures on bare boxes. This milestone opens the world up and makes it look like the concept art, **`concept-1` above all**: a hall of ivory and gold whose tall pointed arches open onto a sky full of clouds.
- walls only as tall as they need to be, with a cornice;
- open edges built as **arcades**: tall pointed arches with a balustrade, looking out over a sea of clouds;
- a painted sky, cloud banks and drifting clouds, distant spires and floating islands on painted cards that shift as you move, and a soft haze across every arena;
- richer marble and gold surfaces, with baked sunlight falling through the arches, and tall windows between pilasters;
- characters that sit in that light: darker in shadow, brighter in the sun, with a soft contact shadow under each;
- light shafts through the arches;
- **real depth where it shows**: thick arches, a cornice that stands out against the sky, pilasters standing forward from the walls, and windows set back into them.

It's built the way a 2.5D game builds it: the arcades and doorway arches are **painted textures with cut-out openings** (like Doom's see-through wall textures), not 3D models. But flat paintings look like painted sheets from the side, and `concept-1` is seen exactly from the side, looking along its arches. So the painting gets **a little simple geometry wherever an edge shows**: each arch opening has a stone reveal 0.6 m deep, the top of the cornice projects from the wall as a box, pilaster strips stand 0.12 m forward, and the painted windows' glass sits 0.25 m back in the wall (§5.3, §6). That's boxes and strips, a few thousand triangles in total, merged into the existing meshes, so it adds no draw call. Better sprites, rendered from professional 3D reference models, are a later milestone (§10).

It changes how the world is drawn. It makes **two simulation changes**: walls get their own heights, which line of sight uses (§3), and a censer that runs out of range beyond an open edge vanishes without breaking (§3.4). It changes no movement, enemies, classes or protocol.

**The overriding rule is performance.** The game exists to fight hundreds to thousands of enemies at once (MVP §12: 1 500 living enemies at 60 FPS on an integrated GPU). Every feature here is chosen to cost almost nothing per frame, and every stage has to prove it (§2).

**You never see into another arena.** Lower walls and open edges must not show one arena's enemies or layout from another (§3.1).

All numbers are **initial values**, as in the MVP.

## 1. Goals and rules

**Goals:**
1. A screenshot of the game next to `concept-1` reads as the same world: tall pointed arches of ivory and gold with real thickness, a bright painted sky and clouds seen through them, warm sunlight falling through the arches in long pools across a marble floor, soft warm haze. Next to `concept-2` and `concept-3`: the same world from an open terrace and on a plaza.
2. Walls are no taller than the "never see another arena" rule and a grand look need (§3.2), not 16 m everywhere.
3. Nothing in this milestone changes the frame rate by more than the gate allows (§2.3).

**Rules:**
- **Bake, don't compute.** Light, shadow and ambient occlusion are computed **once, when the level is built**, and stored in a lightmap and in vertex colors (§5.2). No real-time lights, no shadow maps.
- **No post-processing.** No bloom, outline, SSAO, color-grading or any other full-screen pass. The ink outlines are painted into the textures.
- **No transparency over large areas.** New transparent surfaces are limited to the light shafts, clouds and spires of §7, which fade out up close and are seen mostly through the arches and above the walls (the worst case is the benchmark's arcade view, §2.3), and the small contact shadows of §5.4. The arcades and arches are cut out (alpha test), not transparent.
- **Merge and instance.** Static scenery is merged into a few meshes, or drawn with `InstancedMesh`, so draw calls stay flat as the scenery grows.
- **Geometry only where an edge shows.** Real geometry is added only where a flat painting would visibly fail: arch reveals, cornice crowns, pilaster strips and window recesses (§5.3, §6). Everything else stays painted.
- **What you see is what the host knows,** with bounded exceptions. A wall is as tall in the simulation as it's drawn (§3.4), except an open edge's parapet: it's solid up to `PARAPET` in the simulation, but drawn as a see-through balustrade with a low ledge behind it (§6.1), so you see sky between the balusters but can't shoot through them. Nothing in play is ever beyond it, so this hides nothing and blocks nothing a player could aim at. Scenery that isn't in the simulation may stand only:
  - where **nothing in play can be behind it or under it**: on open edges, beyond which there is only void (arcades above their parapet and their reveals, §6.1); over doorways above the headroom (§6.2); and at or above the zone top of every zone next to it (cornice crowns, §5.3). Nothing in play is ever above its zone top (§3.1);
  - **as relief** (§5.3): at most `RELIEF` = **0.12 m** in front of a straight wall face, never on a pillar and never within 1 m of a convex corner. Every body keeps at least 0.35 m (the smallest radius, the Blessed's) from a wall face, and a straight line between two points at least that far in front of a flat face never comes nearer to it. So a relief can hide only the edge of a sprite touching the wall, a projectile skimming it, or a line grazing past a convex corner.

  It may hide sky; beyond those slivers, it never hides an enemy a shot could reach.
- **Recesses are always allowed.** Geometry set back *behind* a wall's face, inside the wall cell (the window recesses, §5.3), can't hide anything: nothing in play is ever inside a wall. The simulation still stops shots at the wall's face, so a shot into a window stops at the window's mouth; its effects are drawn on the glass (§5.3).
- **Enemies stay readable.** The MVP's rule holds: white and gold enemies with dark outlines must stand out against the world, **now including the sky and the clouds seen through the arches, the open sky above the lower walls, the haze (§4.2) and the shadows (§5.2)**. Cherubs matter most: at 4 m up they're now often seen against open sky and clouds over the walls instead of against stone, and so are their arrows and the Choristers' orbs. If a texture, the sky, a cloud or the haze makes an enemy hard to see against it, it's wrong, not the enemy. Floors stay **mid-to-light and low in contrast**; strong detail goes on walls, arches and the sky.

## 2. Performance

### 2.1 Budgets

Added by this whole milestone, measured against the code at the start of stage 1:

| Item | Budget |
|---|---|
| Draw calls | **at most +10** (the terrain goes from 3 to 1, §5.1; arcades, shafts, clouds (with the distant spires, §7.3) and contact shadows add 1 each; reliefs, crowns and reveals add none, they're part of the terrain mesh) |
| Static scenery triangles (parapets, cliffs, cornices, crowns, reliefs, window recesses, arcades, reveals, arches) | **at most +50 000** (expected for the Pearly Gates: about 30 000, of which about 5 000 for the arch reveals, 5 000 for crowns and reliefs and 15 000 for the window recesses, at about 100 each) |
| GPU texture memory (with mipmaps) | **at most +120 MB** (expected: about +40 MB for the texture array, +23 MB sky, +11 MB clouds and spires (one 2 048 × 1 024 sheet, §7.3), +3 MB light shaft, +1 MB lightmap) |
| Download added to the first load | **at most +12 MB** (the sky and the tiling textures are shipped as JPEG or WebP, §4.3) |
| Per-frame JavaScript | **nothing new** except moving the sky with the camera, and writing the contact shadows' instances in the loop that already fills the billboards (§5.4). Anything animated moves in a vertex shader, driven by one shared time uniform |
| Full-screen passes | **0** |
| Real-time lights and computed shadows | **0** (the contact shadows of §5.4 are painted ellipses) |
| Level build time (heights, baking, merging) | **at most +300 ms** |
| Simulation | the simulation performance test (MVP §13.1) is unchanged and passes |

### 2.2 Cheap by design

- **Lower walls cost nothing.** Pixels that were wall become sky: one texture lookup per pixel, about the same cost.
- **Sky:** the existing dome with a texture instead of a gradient, still one draw call. It's drawn **after** the opaque terrain with the depth test on, so pixels the terrain already covers are skipped. The agent measures this order against drawing it first (§2.3) and keeps the faster.
- **Fog and haze:** one fog curve shape with a near haze (§4.2), the same arithmetic as today's linear fog. The scenery's shaders take the fog color from the sky in the view direction (§4.2): one extra lookup in a tiny mip level of the sky, nearly free. Sprites keep the single fog color.
- **Characters in the light:** one lightmap lookup per billboard *vertex* (4 per sprite), not per pixel (§5.4). Contact shadows are small quads under the characters, one instanced mesh.
- **Textures:** every tiling texture is at most **1 024 × 1 024** at runtime, mipmapped, anisotropy 4 as today. The terrain's and the arcades' looks share **one texture array** (WebGL2 `DataArrayTexture`, one layer per look, chosen per vertex), so adding a look adds no draw call (§5.1).
- **Cut-outs in their own mesh.** The arcades' and doorway arches' painted faces are alpha tested (§6). They're one separate mesh, so the opaque terrain's shader has no alpha test and keeps the GPU's early depth rejection. Their reveals, crowns and pilaster strips are opaque and go into the terrain mesh.
- **Relief is cheap geometry.** Boxes and strips with a handful of faces each, built once with the level, in meshes that are drawn anyway.
- **Lightmap:** one small texture (4 texels per meter), one extra lookup per floor pixel (§5.2).
- **Light shafts, clouds:** a few dozen quads in total, drawn as one instanced mesh each (§7).

### 2.3 The performance gate

The bench's FPS is capped at the display's refresh rate (60), which hides a slowdown until it's large. So this milestone also measures the **uncapped** frame time.

- **Stage 1 first adds `npm run bench -- --uncapped`**: the same benchmark (`?bench=1`, 1 500 enemies) with Chromium started with `--disable-frame-rate-limit` and `--disable-gpu-vsync`, reporting average frame time in ms alongside FPS. If the flags don't uncap it on the machine, say so in `bench.md` and use the capped numbers, with the **1%-low FPS** as the sensitive measure.
- **Three runs.** Stage 1 also adds `--map <id>`, which passes `map=<id>` to the page (today `bench=1` forces `sandbox` in `params.ts`), and `--view arcade`. The benchmark's rules already work on any map: the player starts at the first arena's entry cells, that arena enters combat at once and is topped up to 1 500 Blessed (`sim.ts`, `bench`). In `pearly-gates` the first arena is Arena 1, so only the parameter is new. The gate runs:
  - `sandbox`, as today;
  - `pearly-gates`: the swarm at Arena 1's entry, with the camera turning as today, because the scenery matters most there;
  - `pearly-gates --view arcade`: the same, but the camera doesn't turn: it looks north-east (yaw −45°) at eye level, across Arena 1's north arcade and out at the sky. This is the **worst view for the scenery** (the most sky, clouds, light shafts and cut-out arches on screen at once), which the turning camera only passes through.
- **Baseline:** before changing anything, record the uncapped result of all three runs in `docs/bench.md` (rows *M10 baseline*). Run each 3 times and keep the median; record the spread (slowest − fastest) too.
- **After every stage:** run them again (median of 3 each) and record them. **A stage passes** when, in all three runs, all of these hold:
  - capped average FPS is at least 58 (the MVP's target);
  - average simulation time is at most 8 ms;
  - the **uncapped average frame time is at most 6% above the previous stage's result**, and after the last stage **at most 10% above the baseline**;
  - the budgets of §2.1 hold, checked with `renderer.info` (draw calls, triangles, textures) printed by the benchmark.
- **Noise.** If the 3 runs of a measurement spread by more than 4%, run 5 and keep the median. A stage that fails by less than the spread is run again before anything is cut.
- **If a stage fails**, cut in this order until it passes, and record what was cut in `decisions.md`:
  1. fewer clouds and spires (wisps first, then banks, then spires, §7.2, §7.3);
  2. fewer light shafts;
  3. contact shadows only under players and the Gatekeeper, not under the swarm (§5.4);
  4. the sky drawn first instead of last (if that was faster, keep it);
  5. the lightmap at 2 texels per meter instead of 4;
  6. smaller textures;
  7. fewer texture looks.

  Never cut the sky, the open edges, the arcades or their reveals, the haze or the characters' lighting (§5.4).
- Benchmark runs need an active, uncovered desktop (see the M8.5 note in `bench.md`). A throttled run (under 5 FPS) is repeated, not recorded.
- The final performance on the target laptop is a human checkpoint (§11).

## 3. Wall heights and open edges

Today every wall cell is 16 m tall, in the drawing and in the simulation. From this milestone each wall cell gets **its own height**. It's computed from the map when the map is loaded, the same way on the host and every client, so the protocol doesn't change.

### 3.1 The rule: zones never see each other

- **Zones.** Each arena's floor cells inside its `rect` form one zone. The remaining floor cells (the Lobby and the corridors) are split into connected zones (4-neighbors). Door cells belong to the arena whose `doors` or `exitDoors` list them.
- **Headroom.** The highest point anything can reach above the floor, plus 0.5 m, computed in code from the constants so a future flyer raises it automatically:
  - a player's eye at the top of a jump: `PLAYER_EYE + JUMP_VZ² ÷ (2 × GRAVITY)` ≈ 2.8 m;
  - a Cherub's top: `CHERUB_HOVER` + its height = 4.8 m;
  - a launched enemy's top (M9 §3.2): 1 m + 1.6 m = 2.6 m;
  - a soul's top: `SOUL_RISE` + `SOUL_HEIGHT` + `SOUL_BOB` = 2.9 m.

  So `HEADROOM` = 4.8 + 0.5 = **5.3 m**.
- **Zone top** `T(Z)`: the highest floor in the zone + `HEADROOM`. In a zone with the Gatekeeper's `B` cell, it's at least the `B` cell's floor + the Gatekeeper's height (6 m) + 0.5 m.
- **The guarantee.** With all doors closed, no straight line from a point in one zone's space (floor up to its zone top) reaches a point in another zone's space without passing below the top of a cell in between. Doors are openings, as today: through an open door you see the corridor or arena beyond, as now.

  Why it holds: every *Wall* around a zone is at least its zone top (§3.2), so a line can leave a zone only over a wall, climbing, or over an open edge. A climbing line that crosses a wall of the other zone is above that zone's space from then on. So views can only leak where open edges let a line out of one zone and into another; §3.3 closes those, and the test of §8.1 checks it.

### 3.2 Wall heights

`WALL_MIN` = **8 m**: no wall is lower than this above the floor it stands beside, so the walls read as grand architecture and take the arcade bays of §6 (8 m) under one cornice line.

`CROWN` = **0.5 m**: every wall rises at least this far above the zone tops next to it, so the cornice's projecting crown (§5.3) is entirely above everything in play.

Each wall cell is one of these kinds:

| Kind | Which cells | Height |
|---|---|---|
| **Wall** | A wall cell with a floor cell among its 8 neighbors, except open edges | `max(T(Z)) + CROWN` over the zones of those neighboring floor cells, and at least `WALL_MIN` above the highest of those floors. |
| **Pillar** | Every cell of a group of wall cells (4-connected) that doesn't touch the grid's border | As *Wall*. **Pillars are cover and must work exactly as today:** the boss arena's 6 pillars hide players from Judgment, and Arena 3's hide them from the Choristers. In the boss arena the zone top is 14 m, above the Gatekeeper's eye (7.5 m dais + 5 m = 12.5 m), so every line from that eye to a player stays below the pillar tops (14.5 m) and is blocked exactly when it's blocked today. §8.1 tests it. |
| **Open edge** | See §3.3 | In the simulation: its bay's base (§6.1) + **1.4 m** (`PARAPET`: the height of the balustrade's rail in the prepared `tex-arcade`, §6, measured and recorded as a constant, so the simulation and the drawing agree), or, for an open-edge cell in no bay, its highest neighboring floor + `PARAPET`. A bay's base is the highest floor next to any of its cells, so the parapet is at least `PARAPET` above every floor beside it and matches the balustrade painted across the whole bay. Above a jump's 1.22 m peak, so it can't be climbed and doesn't feel like an invisible wall. Drawn as an arcade (§6.1). |
| **Void** | Wall cells with no floor cell among their 8 neighbors, and everything outside the grid | Nothing: not drawn, and lines pass through. |
| **Door** (closed) | Door cells | As *Wall*, computed from the zones on both sides. |

**One height per run.** A cornice that jumps up and down along one wall looks broken (the table alone would step it beside every walkway, gallery and terrace, and every 0.25 m up a climbing corridor). So after the table, heights are evened out, in one pass, from the table's heights:
- A **run** is a maximal straight line of *Wall* cells that each have a face looking onto floor of the same zone, in the same direction. Each cell takes the highest table height of every run it's in (a cell at a convex corner, or in a wall one cell thick, can be in two).
- A *Wall* cell in no run (behind an inner corner) then takes the highest height among its 4 neighbors, so the corner's silhouette has no notch.
- Every cell of a **pillar** group takes the group's highest height.
- The bays of one straight run of open edges share one arcade height `H` (§6.1), the highest of theirs.
- Raising a wall only blocks more, so the guarantee of §3.1 and every check of §8.1 still hold. Open edges and doors aren't changed.
- **Steps that remain** (where runs of different heights share a cell): the taller part's cornice and crown turn onto its end face, the part standing above the lower wall's top, as at an outer corner; the lower part's stop against that face, as at an inner corner (§5.3).

In the Pearly Gates the table gives, above the map's zero: 8 m (the Lobby and Arena 1), 9.3 m (Arena 2; 11.5 m beside its 3.5 m walkways), 12.8 m (Arena 3; 15 m beside its galleries) and 14.5 m (the boss arena; 15.5 m beside its terraces). Evening out raises each run that touches a walkway, gallery or terrace to the higher value along its whole length. Measured from the floor beside them, walls are about 8–11 m. Walls between arenas are as tall as the taller side needs.

`WALL_TOP` (16 m) stays as the cap and as the height above which projectiles are removed. A map whose computed heights exceed it fails level validation.

### 3.3 Open edges

An open edge is a wall cell where the level meets the sky. It's drawn as an arcade (§6.1), with a cliff below it.

- **Candidates:** wall cells next to floor cells of **exactly one** zone, in a wall group that touches the grid's border (so not a pillar). Each candidate has an **outward direction** `o`: away from the floor cells next to it (the sum of the directions from those floor cells to it, normalized).
- **Facing pairs close.** Candidate *b* is **in front of** candidate *a* when `(b − a) · o(a) > 0`, **strictly**: candidates in line with each other along the same side of the map (dot product 0) are not in front of each other. Two candidates of different zones *face each other* when each is in front of the other and they're within 150 m. Every candidate that faces a candidate of another zone becomes a *Wall* instead. This closes the pockets of empty space between arenas, where one arena could see another across the gap.
- The rest are open edges. In the Pearly Gates they are: the west side of the Lobby, the north side of Arena 1, the north and east sides of Arena 2, the east side of Arena 3 and the south side of the boss arena. The corridors get none (their walls face the arenas'). A prototype of these rules found no line between zones with the test of §8.1. (With `≥ 0` instead of `> 0`, Arenas 1–3 would keep 1–3 open cells each.)
- **The unit test of §8.1 is the judge.** If it finds a line between zones, the agent turns the open edges along it into walls until it passes, and records the change.
- **Bays** (§6.1) are computed here too, in stage 2, from the open edges alone, because the parapet's height depends on them (§3.2). Stage 6 only draws them.

### 3.4 What changes in the simulation

- `map.top` for a wall cell is its height from §3.2 instead of 16 m. For void cells and outside the grid it's −∞, so lines pass through. Closed doors use their *Door* height.
- Line of sight and hitscan (`los.ts`) are otherwise unchanged. **Movement doesn't change:** it uses `solid`, which is unchanged, so walls of any height (open edges included) still block players, enemies and Cherubs completely.
- **What this changes in play:**
  - Shots and lines of sight over an open edge's parapet now pass. They go out into the sky (an arcade's piers and arches above the parapet are drawn only, §1; nothing in play is beyond them).
  - No line between a player's eye and anything in play passes over a *Wall*, *Pillar* or *Door* (§3.1), so every result inside a zone is the same as today. §8.1 tests this. That includes cover: hiding behind a pillar from Judgment, a Chorister or a Cherub works exactly as now.
  - A projectile flying over an open edge carries on until its range ends or it leaves the grid.
- **Censers beyond the level.** Today a censer that reaches the end of its range breaks in the air and leaves an incense cloud (M9 §2.8). Beyond an open edge that cloud would hurt nothing but would still count toward the cap of 6 and push out a useful one. So a censer whose range ends over a **void** cell (§3.2) vanishes without breaking: no damage, no cloud, no revive progress. Over the level, and over an open-edge cell itself, it breaks as today. Clients draw no break, burst or cloud for a censer that vanishes while its last drawn position is over a void cell. A client whose last snapshot of a censer was still over the level may draw a break the host didn't make; that's rare (it needs the censer's range to end within a few meters past the parapet), cosmetic, and accepted.

### 3.5 How it's drawn

The terrain draws the heightfield of §3.2:
- **Vertical faces** wherever a cell is higher than its neighbor, including wall faces that look onto void.
- **Tops** of every wall cell. Wall tops are rarely seen.
- **Open edges:** until stage 6, a plain block at the simulation height (`PARAPET`); from stage 6, an arcade (§6.1).
- **Cliffs:** below every open edge, and below every wall face that looks onto void, the face continues down to **30 m below the map's lowest floor**, with `tex-wall` (it repeats vertically; `tex-riser` doesn't). Its lowest 10 m fade out, so the level floats on the clouds: until stage 3 its vertex colors fade to the fog color; from stage 3 the terrain shader blends them into the sky's color in the view direction (§4.2), the same lookup the fog uses, so the cliff's foot melts into the painted cloud sea instead of a flat color.
- **Doors:** the closed-door columns are drawn to their *Door* height.

## 4. The sky

### 4.1 What it looks like

A bright painted sky like the concept art: big sunlit clouds, floating spires, warm haze at the horizon, rich blue overhead, and a sea of clouds below. It replaces the gradient in `makeSky()` ([scene.ts](../src/render/scene.ts)). Through the arches, the cloud sea below the horizon is what lies under the level.

### 4.2 How it's mapped

`sky-day` (3 168 × 1 344) is a **horizon band** that goes around the player **twice: once as painted over 180°, then mirrored over the other 180°**, with plain color above it. Why not once around 360°: its left and right edges don't match (golden haze and light rays on the left, blue sky on the right), so a cross-fade would smear right where the sun's rays are; and stretched over 360° it has about 8.8 px per degree, while the screen shows about 12 (at 1 080 px) to 18 (at 150% display scaling) px per degree, so it would look soft. Mirrored over 180°, the edges meet themselves with no seam at all, and it has about 17.6 px per degree, about as sharp as the screen.

- **Horizon row:** the row where the cloud sea meets the haze (about 65% down; found by eye and recorded as a constant).
- **Vertical scale:** the same degrees per pixel as horizontally (180° ÷ 3 168 px), so nothing is squashed: the horizon row is at 0° elevation, the top row at about **+50°** and the bottom row at about **−27°**. The agent may adjust the angles if a screenshot looks better, and records the choice.
- **Above the band**, blend from the band's top-row color (averaged across the row) to a **zenith color**, a deeper blue sampled from the top of the band, by about 80°. **Below the band**, blend to the fog color by about −45°.
- **Soft top and bottom edges.** The painting has a cloud cut off by its top edge (top left, on the sun's side), so ending the band at one row would slice it with a straight line. The band's own top 10% (about 5°) fades into the averaged top-row color, and its bottom 10% into the averaged bottom-row color, so whatever touches the edges dissolves into the gradients. Checked with a screenshot looking up on the sun's side.
- **Turned to the sun:** the painting's left edge, where its light rays and brightest haze are, is turned to face the sun's direction (§5.2). Mirrored there, the rays fan out symmetrically on both sides of the sun, so the light in the sky and on the floor agree. The painting's right end (the dome and spires) faces away from the sun. It's mirrored too, so anything near that edge appears twice, mirror-imaged, and the Lobby's arcade (the first view of the game) looks toward it. If the dome's twin reads as an obvious mirror image there, the prepared image is cropped on its right to just before the dome (keeping at least 2 700 px, so still about 15 px per degree), the 180° mapping uses the cropped width, and the choice is recorded.
- **One texture, one lookup per pixel:** the shader samples the band with the view direction (azimuth ÷ 180° for the horizontal position, the elevation for the vertical), with mirrored-repeat wrapping horizontally. Because the texture is mirrored, the jump of `atan2` from +180° to −180° lands on the same texel, so there's no seam; the shader still picks its mip level from the direction's own rate of change (`textureGrad` with continuous derivatives, or `textureLod`), so that jump doesn't draw a line of the smallest mip down the sky.
- **Fog color** (`FOG_COLOR`) is sampled from the band's horizon row, averaged across it. It's the clear color and the fog color of the sprites.
- **Haze.** Today the fog starts at 40 m, so inside a 40 m arena there's none, while `concept-1` and `concept-3` get much of their depth from a soft haze across the room. One fog curve replaces the linear fog for **every** fogged material, scenery, sprites and effects alike (replace three.js's fog shader chunk, so the materials that use it follow): the fog amount rises from 0 at **10 m** to its **40 m value**, then on to 100% at **150 m**, linearly in each part. It's the same arithmetic as today, so it costs nothing.
  - **Scenery** (terrain, arcades, light shafts, clouds and spires): **30%** at 40 m.
  - **Sprites and effects: half that, 15%** at 40 m. The world fades into the haze faster than the enemies, so enemies stand out from it, which matters at the long-range classes' reach: the Betrayer shoots to 60 m and the Fallen's slug to 50 m, where the scenery is about 43% and 37% fogged and sprites about 30% and 23%.
  - **Readability:** a Blessed 40 m away (across an arena) and one 60 m away (across the boss arena) still read clearly. If not, lower the sprites' 40 m value first (down to 0), then the scenery's (never below 15%), and record it.
- **The scenery fogs into the sky behind it.** The scenery's shaders (terrain, arcades, light shafts and clouds; from the stage that brings each) don't fog to one color: they fog to the **sky's color in the view direction**, read from a small mip level of the sky (about 100 px wide). So distant walls, cliffs and clouds melt into the painting, golden where it's golden and blue where it's blue, and a fully fogged cloud vanishes into the sky instead of becoming a flat, fog-colored shape. One extra texture lookup per pixel of scenery.
- **Draw distance:** the camera's far plane goes from 200 to **400 m**, so no cloud card is cut off (§7.2). The sky dome stays inside it and, as today, doesn't write depth, so clouds beyond its radius still draw over it. The near plane (0.05 m) is what limits depth precision, so this costs none worth measuring.
- **Size:** shipped at the source's 3 168 px width (no upscaling, no seam work: the mirroring is done by the sampler), as JPEG or WebP, about 23 MB on the GPU with mipmaps.
- **Readability:** if Blessed don't read against the horizon haze seen through an arch, the prepared image's horizon is made slightly darker or more saturated. The enemies are never changed.

### 4.3 Image formats

Source images may be PNG or JPEG (`.jfif` and `.jpg` included). Wherever this document or art-prompts.md names a source as `.png`, a JPEG with the same base name is accepted.

Images without transparency (the sky, the tiling terrain textures) are converted to **JPEG or WebP** by a build-time script in `scripts/` (as `cutout.py` is today), at a quality of about 88. Images with transparency (the arcade, the clouds, the light shaft) stay PNG.

## 5. Surfaces and baked light

### 5.1 Textures

New tiling textures (prompts in art-prompts.md), each 4 × 4 m and repeating like the existing four, except the cornice:

| File | Used for |
|---|---|
| `tex-floor-plain` | Variation for floors, so large floors don't visibly repeat a 2 × 2 tile pattern. |
| `tex-floor-medallion` | One large gold medallion, at the center of arenas and in front of doors. |
| `tex-wall-window` | Wall with a tall arched window painted in recess, sky-blue glass. |
| `tex-wall-pilaster` | Wall with a fluted pilaster strip. Also every face of every pillar. |
| `tex-cornice` | A horizontal gold-and-ivory cornice band. Tiles left to right only. |

**Preparing them** (Claude, by script, from the human's images):
- `tex-floor-plain`: its gold lines are 4 m apart, but `tex-floor`'s are 2 m apart, and its marble veins break at the edges. Shift it by half its size (so the cross moves to the edges and hides the broken veins), then paint the same thin gold cross through the new center. The result is a 2 × 2 grid like `tex-floor`'s. Match its brightness to `tex-floor`.
- `tex-wall-window`: `tex-wall` and `tex-wall-pilaster` have a carved frieze band along the bottom, which lines up across neighboring wall tiles; the window wall has none. Copy the band from `tex-wall` into its bottom rows (below the window frame). Then **trace the window's glass** (the sky-blue area inside the frame's inner edge, its gold tracery bars included) into a symmetric polyline of at most 24 points in tile coordinates (0 to 1 across and up the tile), written to a generated source file like the arch's outline (§6). The window recess follows it, stretched with the tile (§5.1, §5.3). Checked by drawing the outline over the image.
- `tex-cornice`: crop to the band, and left to right to a **whole number of repeats** of its carved pattern, cut at the same point of the pattern on both sides, so it tiles without a seam. Don't cross-fade it: blending two copies of a repeating pattern leaves ghosted leaves. Choose the number of repeats so that one tile, laid along 4 m of wall and 1.5 m tall, is stretched or squeezed horizontally by at most 15%. Resized into its 1 024 × 1 024 layer (the layer needn't keep the band's proportions). One tile covers **4 m** of wall, aligned to world coordinates like the other walls, so the cornice's pattern lines up with the 4 m wall segments below.

The existing `tex-floor`, `tex-riser`, `tex-wall` and `tex-door` stay; their sources are already 2 048 px.

**The texture array.** The terrain looks (`tex-floor`, `tex-riser`, `tex-wall` and the five new ones) and the two arcade layers (§6.1) are layers of one `DataArrayTexture` at 1 024 × 1 024, mipmapped, RGBA. Each vertex carries its layer. UVs repeat as today, so there are no atlas seams. The terrain becomes **one draw call** instead of three, which pays for the new meshes. `tex-door` stays its own texture on the door meshes. Fog, vertex colors and the lightmap (§5.2) work in a small custom shader that replaces `MeshBasicMaterial`.

**UVs.**
- **Floors:** world-aligned, as today (u = x ÷ 4, v = y ÷ 4), except medallions (below).
- **Walls:** u along the wall, world-aligned, as today. v runs up from **the floor the face looks onto** (today it runs from world height 0), so the frieze and the window band sit on the floor; in the decorated band's window and pilaster segments it's stretched as described below. A face that looks onto void runs from the lowest floor of the map.
- **Pillars:** u is centered on each face (the middle of the face is the middle of the texture), so every face shows the pilaster in its center.

**Choosing a look:** deterministic, from the cell's coordinates and the level data, never random per session, so every client builds the same world.
- **Floors: laid out like a designed floor, not a patchwork.** `tex-floor` (black and gold lines, ornate crossings) and `tex-floor-plain` (thin gold lines) differ too much to mix in blocks. `tex-floor-plain` is the calm base everywhere. `tex-floor` forms **runners**, 4 m wide and aligned to the 4 m grid (so both textures' 2 m gold lines meet), like the inlaid runner down the middle of `concept-1`:
  - in each arena, a cross of two runners through its center medallion block (below), one along x and one along y, across the arena's whole floor (an arena without a center medallion: through the 4 m-aligned strips nearest its `rect`'s center);
  - in the Lobby and each corridor, one runner along its long axis through its middle (the Lobby: toward its door).

  Runners cover every floor height, stairs' tops included. The quiet base keeps swarms readable, and the runners lead the eye from door to center.
- **Medallions:** a 4 × 4 cell block whose corners are on even coordinates (so its edges lie on the floor's 2 m gold lines), whose 16 cells are all floor at one height, none a door cell. Its UVs run 0 to 1 across the block, so the medallion is whole wherever the block is. One at each **arena's center** (the valid block whose center is nearest the `rect`'s center, if one is within 6 m) and one **in front of each door** (the valid block inside the arena nearest the door's middle, if one touches the cells next to the door). Medallions go nowhere else. Derived from the existing level data, so no new level fields are needed.
- **Walls, vertically,** measured from the floor the face looks onto. `concept-1`'s windows fill most of the wall's height, so the decorated band is stretched up toward the cornice instead of repeating a 4 m tile:
  - **The decorated band**, from the floor up to `B` = the underside of the cornice, but at most **7.5 m**. Along the wall, 4 m segments (aligned to world coordinates, as the UVs are) alternate window and pilaster. Each segment shows **one** tile of `tex-wall-window` or `tex-wall-pilaster` over the band's whole height: the tile's frieze rows (its bottom `FRIEZE` ≈ 0.4 m, measured from the prepared images and recorded as a constant) keep their 1:1 scale, so the frieze lines up with plain `tex-wall` beside it, and the rest of the tile is stretched vertically to fill the band (about 1.7× on an 8 m wall, at most about 2×). A pointed lancet window stretched this way still reads as a lancet, and the pilaster's flutes have no vertical pattern to distort.
  - A segment that isn't fully wall (cut by a corner or a door), or whose 4 faces don't all look onto floor at one height (beside stairs), uses plain `tex-wall` instead, **mapped the same way**: on each face, one tile over the band's height, its frieze rows at 1:1 from the floor that face looks onto and the rest stretched up to `B`. So every face in the band shows one frieze at its foot and none higher up, and the stone courses of plain, window and pilaster segments (all three tiles have the same courses) line up wherever their floors match. (Repeating `tex-wall` every 4 m, as today, would put a frieze at 4 m that stops dead at the next decorated segment, and courses 1.7× shorter than their neighbors'.)
  - **From `B` up to the cornice** (only where the band was capped at 7.5 m): `tex-wall` at 1:1, its v starting at `B`, so its frieze sits right on top of the band as a string course, then plain blocks up to the cornice (under 4 m in both maps, so no second frieze; if a future map's is taller, the frieze repeats every 4 m, as today). The pilaster strip of a pilaster segment continues up to the cornice as relief (§5.3).
  - **The top 1.5 m:** `tex-cornice`, one band around every wall, merged into the terrain mesh, with its top part projecting as the crown (§5.3).
- **Pillars:** `tex-wall-pilaster` from the floor to the cornice (painted only, no relief: pillars are cover), and the cornice with its crown on top.
- **Risers** (stairs, ledges, terraces) keep `tex-riser`. **Cliffs** use `tex-wall` (§3.5).

### 5.2 Baked lighting

The terrain already shades by face direction (vertex colors, [terrain.ts](../src/render/terrain.ts)). Replace that with a baked result:

- **Sun:** one fixed direction for all levels, **35° above the horizon**, from the **north-east** (toward −y and +x in map coordinates, rows growing south), so it shines in through the most arcades of the Pearly Gates (Arena 1's north, Arena 2's north and east, Arena 3's east). The Lobby's west arcade and the boss arena's south arcade face away from it: they look out at the sky but get no pools of light. A low sun is what makes `concept-1`'s pools long: through an arch at 35°, the pool of light reaches from about 2 m to 7.6 m from the wall along the sun's direction (at 45° it would stay within about 5 m). An 8 m wall casts about 11.4 m of shadow. The agent may change the elevation between 30° and 45° if a screenshot looks better, and records it.
- **Lightmap (floors).** One RGBA texture covering the map at **4 texels per meter** (508 × 404 for the Pearly Gates), linear filtered and mipmapped, sampled by every floor top (by its x, y). Each texel is computed once at level build:
  - **Shadow:** lit unless a ray from the texel's point (its floor + 0.05 m) toward the sun, until it rises above the map's highest cell top, passes below the top of a cell (walls, pillars, closed doors and parapets at their simulation heights; void and outside the grid don't block), or, from stage 6, through an opaque pixel of an arcade bay's or doorway arch's painted face, through an arch's reveal (§6), or through a solid arcade segment. So the sun falls through the arches onto the floor in arch-shaped pools of light, as in `concept-1`. Crowns and pilaster strips (§5.3) cast no shadow in the lightmap: theirs would be a few centimeters wide.
  - **Ambient occlusion:** darker within 1 m of a cell that rises more than 0.5 m above the texel's floor (wall feet, the bottom of risers and stairs), up to 15% at the edge.
- **Vertical faces** (walls, risers, cliffs, arcades, doors, and the faces of crowns, pilaster strips, reveals and window recesses): vertex colors from the face's direction (faces toward the sun lighter and warm, faces away cooler), darker toward their foot as today. The underside of a crown and the inside of a reveal or a window recess are shaded as facing away from the sun and a further 15% darker, so the arches, windows and cornice read as deep. A window's glass is shaded like the wall it's in.
- **Shadows on walls.** Without them, a corridor or Arena 2's pit would have a dark floor beside a fully sunlit wall: with 8 m walls 4 m apart, most of the sunny wall should be in the other wall's shadow. Every vertical face turned toward the sun (its normal's dot product with the sun's horizontal direction is positive) carries a baked **shadow line**:
  - Each of its vertices stores a `shadowZ` attribute: the height up to which the face is in shadow at that vertex's position along the face, found at level build by testing points up the face from its foot, every 0.1 m, with the lightmap's shadow ray (the same blockers), until one is lit.
  - The terrain's (and, from stage 6, the arcades') fragment shader darkens fragments below the interpolated `shadowZ`, with a soft edge about 0.2 m tall, to the same shadow level and cool tint the floor gets, so a wall's shadowed foot matches the floor beside it. One varying and one comparison per pixel; nothing per frame.
  - Wall geometry has a vertex at least every 1 m along each face (at the cell edges), so the line follows the shadow at 1 m resolution, straight in between. The shadows of pillars on walls are drawn approximately; shadows cast through arch openings onto walls aren't drawn (a face lit above its shadow line is lit all the way up).
  - Faces turned away from the sun get no shadow line: their direction already darkens them.
- **Result:** tinted warm in the light and cool-blue in shadow. Nothing else changes at runtime.
- **Rule:** the darkest shadow is still **mid-brightness**: its luminance (shadow and occlusion together) is at least 60% of the lit floor's, so enemies stay readable in it.

### 5.3 Relief: crowns, pilaster strips and window recesses

Real geometry where a flat painting would read as flat: the cornice against the sky, the pilasters along the walls and the windows in them. Drawn only, opaque, merged into the terrain mesh with the texture array, under the rules of §1.

**Crowns.** The top part of every cornice band, down to the gold line under its row of dentils (found by eye in the prepared `tex-cornice`, recorded as a constant, about 0.45 m and at most `CROWN`), is a **box projecting 0.35 m** from the wall face, its front face showing those rows of the band. Its underside and top are `tex-riser`. The rest of the band stays flush on the wall below it.
- On every *Wall* and *Pillar* face that looks onto floor, on the solid wall above the arcades (§6.1) and on the doorway arches' cornice (§6.2). The closed-door columns (§3.5) are the door's own mesh and get none.
- **Its underside is at least the zone top of every zone next to it**, because walls rise `CROWN` above them (§3.2), so nothing in play can be under it or behind it. (A doorway arch's crown is above its arch, so it follows the arch's rule, §6.2.)
- At an outer corner, the crowns of the two faces meet in a mitre (both run 0.35 m past the corner); at an inner corner, each stops where it meets the other.
- **Faces that look onto void** (cliffs, the backs of walls) get no crown and no cornice.

**Pilaster strips.** Where a wall shows `tex-wall-pilaster` (the pilaster segments of the decorated band, §5.1), the pilaster's fluted strip stands **`RELIEF` (0.12 m) forward** of the wall as a box:
- its width is the pilaster's in the texture (the fluted shaft and its gold edges, measured from the image and recorded as a constant), centered where the painted pilaster is;
- from the floor up to the underside of the cornice, so it reads as a full-height pilaster under the cornice, like the piers between `concept-1`'s windows. Its front face shows the pilaster's columns of `tex-wall-pilaster`, mapped exactly like the painted pilaster behind it (stretched within the band, §5.1, and repeating above it: these columns hold no frieze, so they repeat upward cleanly); its sides show the strip's edge columns, stretched;
- **only on straight walls, never within 1 m of a convex corner, and never on pillars** (§1). A pilaster segment is a whole 4 m of straight wall (§5.1), so its centered strip is at least 1 m from a corner by construction; the test of §8.1 checks it.
- **At the arcades**, the same strip stands on the pier at every joint between two bays and at both ends of each run of bays, 0.6 m wide, from the floor beside it up to the cornice. So the arcade reads like `concept-1`'s: piers standing forward between the arches.

**Window recesses.** Every window segment of the decorated band (§5.1) has its glass set **0.25 m back** into the wall, like `concept-1`'s deep windows:
- **The face** of the window segment is built as a polygon with the traced glass outline (§5.1) as a hole, so it stays opaque, with no alpha test.
- **The glass** is a flat polygon of the same outline 0.25 m behind the face, inside the wall cell, showing the same part of `tex-wall-window` it would show on the face (same UVs), so the painted glass and tracery simply sit deeper.
- **The reveal** joins them along the outline, one quad per segment, in `tex-riser`, shaded as the inside of a window (§5.2). The painted frame stays on the face around the hole.
- A window segment is a whole 4 m of straight wall whose faces look onto one floor height (§5.1), and every wall cell is at least 1 m deep, so the recess always fits inside the wall, even with windows on both sides of a 1-cell wall.

**Effects where a shot meets a wall.** The simulation stops a shot at the wall's face, which can be inside a pilaster strip, behind a crown, or in front of a window's glass. The client draws the effects at that point (tracer ends, censer breaks) moved along the shot's path to the drawn surface: back out of a relief, or on into a recess up to the glass, testing the last 0.5 m of the path on either side against the relief and recess of that cell. So they neither vanish inside the stone nor hang in front of an invisible pane.

### 5.4 Characters in the light

Once the floor has real sunlight and shadow, unlit sprites standing in a shadow look pasted on. Two cheap fixes make them sit in the world, as the figures in `concept-1` do.

**Sprites take the baked light.** Every world billboard (enemies, players, decorations: they share one material, decisions §11.1) samples the lightmap (§5.2) at its anchor's x, y **in the vertex shader**, 4 lookups per sprite, and multiplies its color by a light tint:
- brightness `0.85 + 0.15 × L`, where `L` is the texel's brightness relative to the lit, unoccluded floor (0 in the darkest shadow, 1 in full sun), so a sprite is **never darker than 85%**;
- the same warm-in-light, cool-in-shadow hue as the floor, at half its strength;
- billboards flying more than 1 m above the floor under them (Cherubs; a per-instance flag set where the billboards are filled) use `L` = 1;
- multiplied before the status tints and the glow, so a hit flash, a taunt or a gold ally sheen looks the same in shadow as in light.

**Contact shadows.** A soft dark ellipse on the floor under every character: players (souls excluded), Blessed, Choristers, the Gatekeeper and Cherubs.
- **One instanced mesh**, one draw call, using `fx-glow` darkened (no new image), lying on the floor under the anchor (the client's floor height for that point, + 0.02 m, with a polygon offset), sized 1.4 × the body radius (Cherubs: 1.0 × and half as dark, since they hover 4 m up).
- At most **30% darker** at its center; normal alpha blending, depth-tested, fogged like the floor. Drawn after the opaque scenery and billboards, so a character standing in front of a shadow hides it.
- **No stacking:** in a swarm the ellipses overlap, and stacked they would turn the floor under it dark grey. Where shadows overlap, the floor is no darker than under the darkest single one (for example: draw them at one depth per floor height with a less-than depth test that each writes, so a pixel takes only the first shadow; the agent may choose another way, and records it).
- Hidden while the character is dead or its billboard is hidden; launched enemies (M9 §3.2) keep theirs on the ground.
- Its instances are written in the same per-frame loop that fills the billboards, with nothing else per frame.

**Readability:** a Blessed standing in the darkest shadow is still clearly readable; the shadows under a swarm make the floor around it slightly darker, which only helps the ivory enemies stand out. The gate (§2.3) decides the contact shadows' cost: if they fail it, they're cut back to players and the Gatekeeper first.

## 6. Arches

Everything here is drawn only (§1: allowed because nothing in play is behind or under it). The **painted faces** are one merged mesh of cut-out faces using the texture array, double-sided, with vertex colors as in §5.2: **one draw call**. Their **reveals** and the pier strips are opaque and go into the terrain mesh (§2.2).

**Cut-out edges.** The painted faces are alpha tested with **alpha-to-coverage** (the renderer already has MSAA), not a hard `discard`, so the edges of the arches and balusters are smooth instead of stair-stepped. Plain alpha testing also thins out and loses thin shapes at distance, because each smaller mip level averages them away: the shader scales the texture's alpha up with the mip level it samples (by about 25% per level) and sharpens it by its screen-space rate of change, so the balusters stay visible from across an arena. Checked with a screenshot from 40 m.

The arcade image `tex-arcade` (prompt in art-prompts.md) is **one bay, 4 m wide and 8 m tall**: a half pier at each side (so bays side by side make whole piers), a tall pointed arch opening between them with its apex at about 5.3 m, a balustrade across the bottom 1.4 m of the opening (`PARAPET`, §3.2), and plain stone in the top 1.6 m. **Preparing it** (Claude, by script): the source is 768 × 1 376 (9:16) with 13% plain stone on top; bring it to 1:2 by **adding 160 px of its own stone-block rows on top** (not by stretching), which puts the balustrade's rail at 1.4 m and leaves 22% plain stone for the cornice. Key the green out to transparency as `scripts/cutout.py` does for sprites, resize to 1 024 × 2 048 and split it into two 1 024 × 1 024 layers, *arcade-lower* (0–4 m) and *arcade-upper* (4–8 m). Checked: bays side by side join into whole piers.

**The opening's outline.** The same script traces the arch opening above the rail (the edge of the transparent area: both sides from the rail up, and the pointed arch) into a symmetric polyline of at most 32 points in bay meters, and writes it to a generated source file. The reveals follow it (§6.1, §6.2), and the light shafts start at its apex (§7.1).

### 6.1 Arcades

Every open edge is an arcade, like the left side of `concept-1`.

- **Bays.** Along each side of the level, the open-edge cells with the same outward direction are cut into 4 m segments aligned to world coordinates. A segment of 4 open-edge cells in a row is a **bay**. Partial segments and corner cells are drawn **solid**, as a plain wall (`tex-wall`) up to the arcade's height, so every bay is whole. (Bays are computed in stage 2, §3.3.)
- **Base** of a bay: the highest floor next to its 4 cells. The bay's painted face stands in the vertical plane of the cells' inner edge (the edge next to the floor, flush with the wall faces beside it): *arcade-lower* from the base to base + 4 m, *arcade-upper* from base + 4 to base + 8. Where a cell's floor is lower than the base, the face is solid `tex-wall` from that floor up to the base.
- **Above the bay:** solid `tex-wall` up to the arcade's height `H`, computed like a *Wall* (`max(T(Z)) + CROWN` of its zone, and at least `WALL_MIN` above the base; shared by the bays of one run, §3.2), with `tex-cornice` and its crown on its top 1.5 m. So arcades and walls share one cornice line.
- **The cornice replaces the bay's top, it doesn't cover it.** The painted face and the cornice would stand in the same plane and flicker against each other, so the painted face stops at the cornice's underside (`H` − 1.5 m). When `H` is base + 8, that cuts only plain stone from the bay's top (its ornament ends about 1.6 m below the bay's top).
- **The reveal: the arch is 0.6 m thick.** Behind the painted face, the opening's outline (§6) is extruded **0.6 m outward**, into the open-edge cell: one quad per segment of the outline, from the rail up one side, over the apex and down the other, in `tex-wall` (u along the outline, v across the depth), shaded as the inside of an arch (§5.2). Under the opening, the rail's top is a **sill** 0.6 m deep at the rail's height (`tex-riser`). So, seen along the arcade as in `concept-1`, each arch shows the thickness of its stone instead of a paper edge. Below the rail there's no reveal: the balustrade stands in the face's plane, over open space.
- **No back face.** Nothing in play can see an arcade from outside (open edges face only void, §3.3), and from inside the face and its reveals hide everything behind them. So the reveal is open at its outer end, and nothing else is built there.
- **Behind the face, below the rail:** the cell's top is a ledge at base + 0.2 m (`tex-riser`), seen through the balustrade; the cell's outer face is the cliff (§3.5).
- **Piers** get pilaster strips at every joint between bays (§5.3).
- **In the simulation the cell is still the parapet** (base + `PARAPET`, §3.2), which matches the painted balustrade. The piers, arches and reveals above it block nothing: beyond them there's only void.
- **Shots that hit the painted stone.** A hitscan shot that crosses a bay's painted face (or a doorway arch's, §6.2) above the rail but outside the opening, on a pier, a spandrel or the stone above, flies on in the simulation, but would visibly pass through stone. The client ends its tracer there and draws its impact on the face, testing the shot's segment against the plane of each bay it crosses and the traced outline (§6). Projectiles (censers, orbs, arrows) are drawn as the simulation moves them; flying through a pier is rare, cosmetic and accepted.

### 6.2 Doorway arches

The passage's own sides (the walls beside the door) are the arch's legs, and the pointed top springs from them, so it reads as a gate rather than a pointed window hung over a square hole.

- Over every door (the cells of one `doors` or `exitDoors` list), across the passage, from `L` up, where `L` is the highest floor among the door cells and the floor cells next to them, plus `HEADROOM`: *arcade-upper*, **cropped left and right to the opening's width at its bottom row** (the opening's sides at bay height 4 m, from the traced outline: about 2.3 m of the 4 m bay; the half-piers are cut away), then stretched to the passage's width (about 1.3× for a 3 m door, 1.7× for a 4 m one). So the opening's sides meet the passage's sides at `L` with no ledge, and the pointed arch rises from them.
- **Height:** the arch's top `A` is the higher of `L` + 4 m and the door's *Door* height. Solid `tex-wall` fills between `L` + 4 m and `A` where `A` is higher. `tex-cornice` and its crown run along the top 1.5 m of `A`, and the painted face stops at the cornice's underside, as on the arcades (§6.1).
- **It's 0.6 m thick**, centered on the vertical plane through the middle of the door cells: a painted face on each side, 0.3 m from that plane, and between them the reveal along the opening's outline above `L` (stretched like the face). Seen from either side as you walk through, it's a stone gate, not a sheet.
- The arch may rise above the walls beside the door, like a gate. Where it does, its ends (0.6 m deep) are closed with `tex-wall`, and its cornice and crown turn onto them as at an outer corner (§5.3). Nothing in play can touch it: it's at least `HEADROOM` above every floor next to it.
- **Closed doors:** the door column (§3.5) is drawn around the arch's plane, hiding it up to the *Door* height. Above that, the arch's opening shows nothing of another zone (a line through it is above both zones' space).
- **Open doors:** only toward a zone with no enemies (an arena is entered while idle and left once cleared, and its entry door is closed in combat), so the arch never hides an enemy.

## 7. Atmosphere

All of it is decoration, small in count and on screen, and none of it stands over a floor cell except the light shafts.

### 7.1 Light shafts

| What | Cost |
|---|---|
| One shaft through each bay of a **sunlit arcade** (the dot product of its outward direction and the sun's horizontal direction is positive), at most **24**: if there are more such bays, every *n*-th, spread evenly, chosen deterministically. Each runs from the **apex of the bay's opening** (from the traced outline, §6, about base + 5.3 m) along the sun's direction down to the floor, where its pool of light lies (§5.2), about 7.6 m from the arcade along the sun's direction at 35°. It's 2 m wide, uses `fx-lightshaft.png`, and turns about its own axis to face the camera (in the vertex shader). | 1 draw call (one instanced mesh) |

- **Blending:** not additive. As for the other effects (decisions §11.1: additive light washes out to white on ivory marble), `scripts/cutout.py` turns the image's brightness into opacity, and the shaft is drawn with normal alpha blending, tinted warm gold, at most 25% opacity, depth-tested, without depth writes.
- Their brightness breathes ±10% over about 8 s, in the vertex shader.
- **Overdraw:** a shaft fades out as the camera gets closer than **8 m**, so it never covers much of the screen. An enemy passing through one stays readable.

### 7.2 Clouds

All clouds are camera-facing cards in **one instanced mesh** (1 draw call, shared with the distant spires of §7.3), using `fx-clouds` (four different clouds in a 2 × 2 sheet, one per card; a card may be mirrored left to right for variety). The prepared clouds and spires are one texture, 2 048 × 1 024: the clouds' sheet on the left, the spires' on the right. **Preparing it** (Claude, by script): key out the green like the sprites, then remove the green rim the soft edges leave: every pixel less than 98% opaque, and a 6 px band inside them, takes its color from the nearest opaque pixel further in, and the opacity is remapped to `((a − 0.15) ÷ 0.85)^1.5`. Checked: no rim left over the sky. Normal alpha blending, depth-tested, no depth writes, fogged into the sky behind them (§4.2, so distant ones melt into the painting instead of turning into flat shapes). **Not sorted per frame, but ordered once:** when the level is built, all the mesh's cards (clouds and spires) are put in the instance buffer far to near by their distance from the level's center, so a near cloud is drawn over a far one from anywhere in the level. Each drifts slowly back and forth (up to ±5 m over 60–120 s) in the vertex shader. Placed deterministically from the open edges:

| Group | Count | Size | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cloud sea** | 30 to 50 | 15–40 m wide | 10 to 40 m below the lowest floor, 10 to 120 m beyond the open edges. Gives the clouds below depth as you move. |
| **Cloud banks** | 10 to 20 | 20–50 m wide | 40 to 120 m beyond the open edges, from 10 m below to 10 m above the floor of the nearest open edge, so the arches frame clouds as in `concept-1`. |
| **Wisps** | up to 20 | 4–10 m wide | 3 to 15 m beyond an open edge, from 5 m below to 3 m above its base. |

- **Overdraw:** a card fades out as the camera gets closer than **10 m** to it. A card whose nearest point is beyond the fog's end (150 m, §4.2) would be drawn fully fogged, invisible but still blended over its pixels, so the vertex shader collapses it to nothing instead.
- They replace nothing: the `decor-cloud-tuft` decoration stays as it is.
- **Readability:** Blessed are seen against clouds only through the arches. If one doesn't read against a cloud, the clouds are made warmer or darker (the prepared image or the tint), never the enemy.

### 7.3 Distant spires

The sky painting is at infinity, so it never moves as you walk. `concept-1` and `concept-2` get much of their scale from towers, domes and floating islands at many distances, nearer ones shifting against farther ones. A few painted cards give that for almost nothing.

- **Image:** `fx-spires` (prompt in art-prompts.md): four distant structures in a 2 × 2 sheet (a cluster of slender spires, a domed tower, a floating island with a pavilion, a broken arch), their feet fading into cloud. **Preparing it** (Claude, by script): exactly as `fx-clouds` (§7.2), into the right half of the shared texture.
- **Cards:** in the clouds' instanced mesh (§7.2): no new draw call, the same blending, fog into the sky, overdraw fade and collapse beyond 150 m. They don't drift. They turn about their vertical axis only, so towers stay upright when seen from below. A card may be mirrored left to right.

| Count | Size | Where |
|---|---|---|
| 6 to 12 | 15–40 m wide, 30–80 m tall | 60 to 120 m beyond the open edges, spread along them; feet 20 to 30 m below the lowest floor, in the cloud sea, so their tops stand 10 to 60 m above the floor. |

- Placed deterministically from the open edges, like the clouds, at least 25 m apart, outside the grid, and never over a floor cell.
- They may show above the lower walls from inside an arena: that's intended, as in `concept-2`. They're beyond the level, so they hide nothing in play, and they melt into the haze (about 50–80% fogged seen from the nearest arena; from farther away, beyond 150 m, they're gone).
- **Readability:** pale ivory and gold, fogged, so a Cherub seen against one still reads. If not, they're made paler or more fogged, never the enemy.

### 7.4 Haze and particles

Haze is the fog curve of §4.2 (from 10 m; at 40 m, 30% for the scenery and 15% for sprites and effects), in the sky's color for the scenery and the fog color for sprites and effects: nothing extra. No new particles; the existing embers and feathers are unchanged.

## 10. Out of scope

- **Better sprites from professional 3D models**: billboard sprites for characters, enemies and decorations, and new decorations such as statues, free-standing columns, and spires and floating islands near the level (the distant painted cards of §7.3 are in this milestone), rendered from professionally made 3D reference models (as the Blender pipeline renders today's sprites). A later milestone; the prop sheets in `assets/art-src/reference/` are kept for it.
- **Columns**, free-standing or as half-columns in front of the arcade piers like `concept-1`'s: they'd stand in the play area, so they'd need collision, and capitals modeled in code would look worse than painted ones. They come with the sprites from professional 3D models (above); a round column looks the same from every side, so it suits a sprite.
- **Level layouts like `concept-1`'s composition**: long, narrow galleries open to the sky on both sides, bridges between arenas, and views down a hall into the distance. Today's arenas are square courtyards, and the corridors must stay enclosed, because an open corridor would let a line leave it over its parapet and drop into an arena over that arena's lower wall (§3.1). New layouts have to be designed around that rule (for example, taller walls on the arenas facing an open bridge). A later milestone, with the props from professional 3D models (above).
- Other 3D geometry beyond §5.3 and §6: modeled moldings or capitals, or relief on pillars (pillars are cover, so what you see of them must be exactly what blocks: §3.2).
- Real-time lights, computed shadows (shadow maps; the contact shadows of §5.4 are painted ellipses) or post-processing of any kind, normal or PBR materials, a graphics-quality setting in the menu (the performance gate replaces it; add one only if the human asks), a different sky per arena, a day-night cycle or weather, new levels or arenas, changes to floors, collision or movement, falling off the level, water, reflections, destructible scenery, animated scenery beyond the shaft and cloud motion, new enemy or class art.
