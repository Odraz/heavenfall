# Co-op horde FPS design mechanisms beyond shooting (L4D, Vermintide 2, Darktide, DRG, KF2, WWZ, B4B, Helldivers 2), mapped to Meatgrinder

Reading guide: "Cited Findings" contain only sourced material and say whether it is a **designer statement** (D), **critic/reviewer opinion** (C), **player/community opinion** (P) or **marketing/store copy** (M). Meatgrinder mappings and cost estimates are my own reasoning and live in "Inferences". Cost scale used throughout: **S** = days, mostly data/tuning/audio on existing systems; **M** = 1-2 weeks, a new system that touches AI, net sync or bots; **L** = multi-week, new tech or lots of art. Meatgrinder context assumed: browser 2.5D billboard renderer, 1000+ enemies, 1-4 players plus bots, 4 classes with LMB/RMB/Q/E, infinite ammo, ~8-minute arena-to-arena runs ending in a boss, L4D-style director already present, revives present, no objectives/pickups/items/disablers. Playtester complaint: "only running around shooting", "no choices", "not much to do", wanted to pick up and break stuff.

## Left 4 Dead: the AI Director, special infected, anticipation, items, and why it creates stories

### Takeaway
Valve's own talks say L4D works because cooperation is the *only* winning strategy, enforced implicitly through enemy design (incapacitating specials, "grabby" commons), limited shared resources and helplessness states, and because the Director varies the *timing and location* of threats (pacing, not difficulty) using "structured unpredictability" and "dramatic anticipation". The stories come from rare, telegraphed, high-drama events that land differently each run, not from scripted set pieces.

### Cited Findings
**Overall structure (Booth, GDC 2009 "Replayable Cooperative Game Design: Left 4 Dead", slides) — all D**
- Cooperation principles: "Ensure cooperation is the only winning strategy", "Treat entire Survivor team as 'the player'", "Penalize non-cooperative behavior harshly — Abandoning the team = death", but "Avoid artificial/arbitrary enforcement... No invisible leashes, teleporting stragglers closer, dealing 'out of bounds' damage". — [Booth GDC 2009 slides](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/GDC2009_ReplayableCooperativeGameDesign_Left4Dead.pdf)
- The horde itself enforces co-op: "Obviously too many enemies for one Survivor to handle alone"; "'Grabby' Infected stop Survivors when they hit, making even a single Infected dangerous"; this "enforces cooperation in an implicit manner without seeming heavy-handed because it fits the expected genre behavior." — [Booth GDC 2009](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/GDC2009_ReplayableCooperativeGameDesign_Left4Dead.pdf)
- Specials: "Adds a layer of variation to the homogeneity/predictability of battling the horde"; "Each special ability exists to address specific gameplay issues"; "Each has an overwhelming or incapacitating attack which create dramatic cooperative moments." — [Booth GDC 2009](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/GDC2009_ReplayableCooperativeGameDesign_Left4Dead.pdf)
- Per-special purpose (D):
  - Hunter: "Outrun and kill stragglers and 'lone wolf' players"; "Completely incapacitating Pounce."
  - Smoker: "Pull apart tightly coordinated teams to create unexpected moments of chaos"; long-range incapacitating tongue. Booth at GDC: "So we added the Smoker so that every now and then, their perfect little plan was going to get messed up" (disciplined teams were defusing their own drama). — [Shacknews GDC report](https://www.shacknews.com/article/57892/left-4-dead-at-gdc)
  - Boomer: "Break the rule of 'shoot everything that moves', forcing players to think a bit before firing"; vomit "creates excellent moments of Dramatic Anticipation where you know the mob is coming to get you, soon..."
  - Incapacitating attacks in general: "Make players fear becoming separated from the group"; "Give players near the incapacitated victim the opportunity to be the hero and save them"; "Players really enjoy helping each other."
  - Tank: "Halts forward momentum"; "Requires full attention of entire team"; "Forces Survivors to defend instead of assault"; rock throw "makes Survivors reevaluate their environment"; music, yells and footfalls announce it.
  - Witch: breaks "shoot everything" "with higher contrast than the Boomer"; forces stealth and care with flashlights; crying audio broadcasts her presence.
  — all from [Booth GDC 2009](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/GDC2009_ReplayableCooperativeGameDesign_Left4Dead.pdf)
- Failed prototypes (D, via report): the Boomer began as an explosive bomb that dealt heavy damage when shot, which confused newcomers; a "Screamer" (freezes, runs off, screams to call enemies) had discovery problems and was merged into the Boomer. Booth: "If you can make a world full of grey boxes fun, then you've got something good." — [Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/57892/left-4-dead-at-gdc)
- Vocalizations as co-op glue: automatic barks improve awareness ("Behind you!", "Hunter's got Zoey!", "Witch!"), communicate short-term goals ("Get to the chopper!") and build camaraderie ("Thanks for that"). — [Booth GDC 2009](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/GDC2009_ReplayableCooperativeGameDesign_Left4Dead.pdf)
- Limited resources: "Limited resources that are required for success encourage cooperation"; "Obvious benefit to sharing (keep extra gun alive)"; "Minimal sharing UI doesn't get in the way"; "Sharing behavior breaks the ice, builds group solidarity." — [Booth GDC 2009](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/GDC2009_ReplayableCooperativeGameDesign_Left4Dead.pdf)
- Helplessness: "A player in a clearly helpless and dependent state demands cooperation"; "Obvious that you will likely be in a similar situation soon"; "Easy to assist helpless player"; "Benefit clearly outweighs cost (usually)." — same source

**Dramatic anticipation and structured unpredictability (D)**
- Dramatic anticipation: "Set up moments where event X implies interesting event Y after a short delay"; summary slide: "If an event is exciting, it will be more so if it broadcasts its impending arrival." Listed examples: infected breaking through doors, Boomer vomit, Tank music/footsteps, Witch sobbing, event music, finales/crescendos ("start with an angry crowd shrieking in the distance"), watching the escape vehicle arrive while fighting, a visible mob climbing a fence, "Third Strike" (revived twice, dies next time), limping when injured, ledge hanging ("the 'cliff hanger'"), incapacitation, rescue closets (hearing trapped friends), and **car alarms: "Double anticipation: Of not setting it off, and of the mob that comes when you do."** — [Booth GDC 2009](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/GDC2009_ReplayableCooperativeGameDesign_Left4Dead.pdf)
- Structured unpredictability: "Collections of interesting possibilities selected at runtime using intentionally designed randomized constraints"; "Low probability + High drama = Memorable"; "Designers often want everything to be experienced, every time - resist the temptation"; "Set up many possible moments, knowing few will happen at any run"; "Combinations of randomized events generate memorable stories." — same source
- Avoid scripted "when/where": manually placed triggers kill replayability ("Players learn all script locations quickly") and kill cooperation ("Players expect everyone to have memorized all encounters - Becomes a race"); "'what' can be ok." — same source

**Adaptive dramatic pacing (D)**
- Inspired by Counter-Strike's "spiky" pacing: "Constant, unchanging combat is fatiguing"; "Long periods of inactivity are boring." — [Booth GDC 2009](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/GDC2009_ReplayableCooperativeGameDesign_Left4Dead.pdf)
- Intensity: one float per survivor, raised by damage taken, incapacitation, being pulled off ledges, and nearby infected deaths (inversely proportional to distance); decays to zero, but not while infected are actively engaging. Director tracks the max of the 4. — same source
- States: Build Up (full threat population until peak threshold), Sustain Peak (3-5 s more), Peak Fade (minimal population until intensity decays; waits for "a natural break in the action"), Relax (30-45 s minimal population, or until survivors travel far enough). Boss encounters are not affected by adaptive pacing. — same source
- "Algorithm adjusts pacing, not difficulty — Amplitude (difficulty) is not changed, frequency (pacing) is"; "Survivor Intensity estimation is crude, yet the resulting pacing works." — [Booth AIIDE 2009 "The AI Systems of Left 4 Dead"](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)
- Population layers: wanderers, mobs (20-30 commons "at unexpected times"), specials, bosses "a few times per map", weapon caches ("2nd tier" weapons), scavenge items (pipe bombs, Molotovs, pain pills, extra pistols). — [Booth GDC 2009](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/GDC2009_ReplayableCooperativeGameDesign_Left4Dead.pdf)
- Mobs: randomized 90-180 s intervals on Normal; none in Relax; Boomer vomit forces a mob and resets the interval; mob size grows from minimum just after a spawn to maximum later; **75% of mobs come from behind** because wanderers/specials/bosses are usually engaged ahead. — same source
- Specials: individually randomized intervals, none in Relax, spawn in any area not visible to survivors; Smokers prefer areas above the team. — same source
- Bosses: placed every N units along the escape path ± random; "Three Boss events are shuffled and dealt out: Tank, Witch, and Nothing"; no successive repeats. — same source
- Items/weapon caches: designers place many candidate locations, the Director chooses which exist. Why designer-placed: "Prediction of possible locations beneficial in this case", "Allows visual storytelling", solves placement (gun racks, leaning against walls). — same source

**Bots (D)**
- Survivor bots let Valve "assume baseline 4 player Survivor team for game tuning"; drop-in/out was "incredibly valuable in the wild." — [Booth GDC 2009](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/GDC2009_ReplayableCooperativeGameDesign_Left4Dead.pdf)
- Bot trust rules: bots use simulated senses, aim and reaction times ("Players need to believe bot replacements are 'fair'"); "Difficult issue: Rescuing incapacitated humans"; "SurvivorBots prioritize human teammates over bots"; "SurvivorBots cannot deal friendly fire damage"; "SurvivorBots never use Molotovs"; a bot far out of place "is teleported near the team when no human is looking at it." — [Booth AIIDE 2009](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)
- Booth's conclusion: "Random players in the wild will actually cooperate! If game is structured to facilitate it (ie: no way to win otherwise)." — [Booth GDC 2009](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/GDC2009_ReplayableCooperativeGameDesign_Left4Dead.pdf)

### Inferences
Mechanism → Meatgrinder mapping → cost:
- **Disabler special (Hunter/Smoker role).** E.g. a "Pouncer" Blessed that leaps on a straggler and pins him (no shooting, slow HP drain) until a teammate hits it, and a "Chain-puller" that drags a player out of the group from range. This is the single strongest anti-"only running around shooting" lever in the source: it creates "be the hero" moments and punishes lone wolves without leashes. Must ship together with a Darktide-style "clutch" escape (see next section) so solo/bot games are not unfair. Bots need a "rescue pinned teammate" priority above everything except own survival. Cost **M** (new enemy behaviour + player disabled state + net sync + bot priority + strong audio cue).
- **"Don't shoot it" special (Boomer role).** E.g. a bloated Cherub/Chorister that, when killed close, splashes players and *calls a mob onto them* (or blinds), so players must kill it at range or avoid it. Directly adds the "think before firing" choice. Cost **S-M** (reuses ranged enemy; needs a director hook "force mob near victim").
- **Dramatic anticipation layer.** Telegraph every mob with a distant shriek/choir swell and directional audio; telegraph boss arrival with music and footfalls; give the "last legs" state (revived twice) a visible/audible marker. Cheapest high-value item. Cost **S**.
- **Structured unpredictability / boss deck.** Shuffle per-arena "event cards" (mini-boss / elite pack / nothing) without repeats; designers place candidate spawn points and candidate breakable/pickup spots per arena, director chooses a subset per run. Cost **S** on top of the existing director.
- **Mobs from behind (75%)**, mob size ramp after spawn, no specials during Relax. Pure director tuning. Cost **S**.
- **Car-alarm / crescendo props (player-triggered horde).** Breakable bells, reliquaries or alarm-idols that, if hit, summon a mob — "double anticipation". This also answers "wanted to break stuff". Pair with a reward (see tomes below) so breaking is a choice, not only a trap. Cost **S-M** (breakable billboard prop + director "force mob" call).
- **Limited, shareable consumables** (pills/molotov/pipe-bomb analogues). The source frames scarcity + sharing as co-op glue, but Meatgrinder is infinite-ammo and memory notes say pickups come after the base loop. A minimal version: one throwable slot (e.g. holy-water flask = area denial, bell = lures horde like pipe bomb) found at designer-placed, director-chosen spots. Cost **M** (pickup system, slot UI, bot use rules — L4D simply forbade bots Molotovs).
- **Bot trust rules** are directly reusable: bots never harm players, prioritize humans for revives, teleport to the group when unseen. Cost **S**.
- **Vocal barks.** Short text/voice callouts from bots and player characters ("Pouncer on Binder!", "Mob behind!") improve awareness for a 1000-enemy screen where readability is hard. Cost **S** (text barks) to **M** (voice).

### Gaps
- Booth's GDC slides do not explain crescendo events or finales mechanically beyond "shrieking in the distance"; I did not retrieve a primary source on L4D2's finale types (gauntlet/scavenge gas-can finales) or the L4D2 Charger/Jockey/Spitter purpose statements. Well-known but unsourced here: L4D2 scavenge finales have players carry gas cans (carrying blocks using your weapon) while hordes come.
- No primary source retrieved on the medkit-vs-pills design (medkit = big slow heal that occupies a slot vs pills = fast temporary HP) or the molotov/pipe bomb/bile jar split; these mechanics are widely known but not verified this session.
- A Game Developer video "Designing mandatory co-op play in Left 4 Dead" exists ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/video-designing-mandatory-co-op-play-in-i-left-4-dead-i-)) but was not fetched.
- No Noclip/GMTK material on L4D was retrieved.

## Vermintide 2 / Darktide: horde + special + elite, melee depth, careers, scarcity, tomes/grimoires, criticism

### Takeaway
Fatshark designers explicitly frame the fun as the *mix* (horde + elites + specials at once), with each category given a different job: hordes are fun to kill en masse, elites "break your flow", specials "enforce and challenge teamplay" and force prioritization. Crucially, Darktide lets every special be "clutched" solo, because otherwise the loop becomes toxic. Risk-reward carry items (tomes/grimoires) add choice but the dice-roll payoff is the most criticized part.

### Cited Findings
- (D) Vermintide 2 designer Mats Andersson (via PCGamesN, summarized by search): roughly 28 enemy types vs 12 in the first game; a duel against one Chaos Warrior "is not engaging at the same level as a Chaos Warrior combined with a horde and a couple of berserkers"; Fatshark wants players to answer overwhelming numbers by coordinating roles (one stuns the horde, another handles the elite). — [PCGamesN Vermintide 2 enemy guide/interview](https://pcgamesn.com/warhammer-vermintide-2/vermintide-2-enemy-guide) (note: content seen only through a search summary, not fetched directly)
- (D) Darktide combat designer Mats, "Dev Blog: Hybrid Combat" (Apr 2024): "We've opted to call this Hybrid Combat - not FPS with an optional melee weapon"; "Elites break your flow, demand your immediate attention, and will end you unless dealt with properly"; "We throw Specials at you to enforce and challenge teamplay"; "In Darktide when hordes of enemies close in on you, you swap your Lasgun for your Thunder Hammer"; problem introduced by "lots of ranged enemies", answered with "sprinting, vaulting, suppression, enemy positioning anchors, and combat vectors". — [Darktide Hybrid Combat dev blog](https://www.playdarktide.com/news/dev-blog-hybrid-combat)
- (D) Darktide "101: Enemy Design and Prototyping" (Daniel, programmer/designer, Sept 2024):
  - Horde: "designed to be numerous, varied, and fun to kill in masses"; "We wanted hordes to feel like a wave rather than individual enemies"; avoidance so enemies don't walk the same path; hordes vault over cover.
  - Roamer archetypes: melee "Rush into melee, forcing players to kill them quickly or switch to melee combat"; assaulters at 8-12 m; riflemen "Stay at a distance, use cover, and take potshots."
  - Specialists split into **Disablers** ("try to disable one player, forcing others to respond": Pox Hound, Scab Trapper) and **Scramblers** ("force players to split up or create chaos to disorient them": bombers, Poxburster, flamers, snipers, Mutant). Mutant: "more of a scrambler than a disabler... charge a player, grab them, deal damage, and then throw them unpredictably."
  - "It's crucial that specialist enemies have clear audio and visual cues."
  - **Clutch rule:** "We also allow players to 'clutch' against all specials, meaning a single player can handle these threats alone if they take the right actions" (push a Hound/Poxburster, dodge a net, sniper shot or Mutant charge); "the ability to clutch is crucial for individual skill expression"; "Not allowing a single player to survive alone would also result in a more toxic gameplay loop."
  - Prototype lessons: "getting charged constantly got too tiring" (pauses added to Mutant); players "got annoyed by [Poxburster] in close quarters", so a fuse and stagger counterplay window was added.
  — [Fatshark forums dev blog](https://forums.fatsharkgames.com/t/darktide-101-enemy-design-and-prototyping-dev-blog/98452)
- (C) PC Gamer's Vermintide 2 review: the director mixes spawn locations so revisited missions don't feel repetitive; example of the director summoning an armored ambush behind a team already fighting a Chaos Warrior; on one run nothing attacked at all, which kept the reviewer on edge. — [PC Gamer review](https://www.pcgamer.com/warhammer-vermintide-2-review/) (via search summary)
- (C) Greek review: specials/elites/mini-bosses need more than standard tactics and arrive "at the worst moment"; Assassins and Sorcerers can break up prepared parties. — [gameover.gr review](https://www.gameover.gr/2018/04/02/warhammer-vermintide-2-review) (paraphrased via search)
- (D) Composer Jesper Kyd: war drums warn of an impending Chaos horde; each Skaven special has its own audio cue. — [Dread Central](https://www.dreadcentral.com/editorials/272672/interview-jesper-kyd-on-vermintide-ii-soundtrack/) (via search summary)
- Tomes/grimoires (mechanics from guides/community): each main level hides 3 tomes and 2 grimoires; tomes occupy the healing slot, grimoires occupy the potion slot and cut the party's max health (sources disagree: 25% vs 33%) and are destroyed if dropped; carried to the end they add better dice to the end-of-mission loot roll. — [PC Gamer locations guide](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/vermintide-2-tome-grimoire-locations-guide); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/552500/discussions/0/1694914736000681505)
- (P) Criticism: even flawless play with all books may not yield top loot because of dice luck; players asked for rewards tied to performance (e.g. bonus for everyone surviving); others defended randomness. Some groups experience friction when a teammate grabs a grimoire and makes the run harder. — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/552500/discussions/0/1694914736000681505)
- (D/C) Darktide launch: new content was paused Jan 2023 to fix progression, stability and performance after negative reception; design director Victor Magnuson (2025): "It took a while to get there, but now we feel we're in a good place", crediting a class overhaul that made classes more complex. — [PC Gamer interview](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/fps/it-took-a-while-to-get-there-but-now-we-feel-were-in-a-good-place-fatsharks-design-director-talks-doing-years-of-repair-work-on-warhammer-40-000-darktide/); [Wikipedia Darktide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warhammer_40,000:_Darktide)
- (P) Darktide melee complaints: push felt weaker/shorter than Vermintide 2, dodge distance shrinks after 3-4 consecutive dodges, some enemies can't be pushed. Anecdotal. — [Steam thread on push](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1361210/discussions/0/4629233576852908634/); [Steam thread on dodge](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1361210/discussions/0/3594464430434824967/)

### Inferences
- **Category jobs as a design checklist.** Meatgrinder currently has horde (Blessed) and ranged (Cherubs, Choristers) = Fatshark's "horde + riflemen". Missing: **elites** (break flow, demand attention) and **specials** (disablers + scramblers). Adding one elite and one disabler gives the "horde + special + elite" mix Andersson describes. Cost: elite **S-M** (bigger billboard Blessed with armor/shield or charge attack; mostly stats + one behaviour), disabler **M** (see L4D section).
- **Elites as class prompts.** Make each elite most cleanly answered by a different class tool, without hard locks: armored "Penitent" broken by Betrayer's Silver Bullet pierce; a charging "Flagellant" stopped by Binder's bind; a shielded caster pulled by Fallen's taunt or leap slam. This turns "which ability now?" into a choice and makes classes feel needed. Cost **M** (per-elite tuning + per-ability interactions).
- **Clutch rule for disablers.** Every pin must have a solo escape: e.g. dash (Betrayer), leap slam (Fallen), taunt shout, or a mash/well-timed melee. This keeps bot-filled and solo runs fair and, per Fatshark, avoids toxicity. Cost **S** once the disabler exists.
- **Scrambler special.** Something that throws/knocks players apart or sets a floor-denial zone forces repositioning; "Poxburster" equivalent (runs in and explodes; must be shot at range, can be shoved) is cheap and readable. Cost **S-M**.
- **Telegraph everything.** Unique audio per special/elite and a horde drum, as Kyd and the Darktide blog stress, matters even more with 1000 billboards on screen (memory: HUD readability). Cost **S**.
- **Tome/grimoire-style relics** = the cheapest "pick up stuff + choice" mechanic. Mapping: arenas hide 1-2 "Cursed Relics" (designer-placed, director-chosen). Carrying one costs the party something visible (e.g. -20% max HP or carrier can't use E) and pays at the end in score/medals on the planned score sheet. Avoid the criticized dice: pay deterministically. Cost **S-M** (pickup + carried-state + score hook; no inventory system needed).
- Melee block/push/dodge depth is mostly Fatshark's genre identity; for a Doom-style shooter the transferable part is "each enemy category forces a different verb", which Meatgrinder can deliver through LMB/RMB/Q/E rather than a block system. Adding block/push would be **M-L** and off-premise.
- Lesson from Darktide's launch: progression/complexity problems overshadowed good combat; keep M12 scope on the base loop (memory: scope discipline).

### Gaps
- I could not fetch a primary Fatshark statement on ammo scarcity, careers/talents design intent, or Vermintide 2's "conflict director" internals (the official term in sources found is "AI director").
- The Andersson/PCGamesN quotes came via a search summary; the original article was not opened.
- No primary source on how Fatshark keeps hordes from being boring beyond the mix and wave feel; no Fatshark GDC talk found.

## Deep Rock Galactic: objectives, terrain, class interdependence, swarms vs objectives, social glue, progression

### Takeaway
DRG's designers framed it as "Left 4 Dead with Minecraft": shooting is the punctuation, while the main activity is a mission objective in a procedural cave with a team whose four classes each hold a different traversal tool, so "if you're missing one class, you always feel like you're missing out on some mechanics." Swarms are scheduled against the objective with a ~20 s warning. Primary designer material on swarm pacing and social glue ("Rock and Stone", beer) was thin in what I could retrieve.

### Cited Findings
- (D) Mikkel Martin Pedersen (game director): "We had this idea of mixing Left 4 Dead with Minecraft"; "users go in and explore a procedurally-generated cave system... and then search for precious minerals and work together as a group"; "There aren't that many games that are pure co-op." — [PCGamesInsider interview, 2018](https://www.pcgamesinsider.biz/indie-interview/66706/we-feel-the-co-op-shooter-market-is-underdeveloped-ghost-ship-games-tells-us-about-deep-rock-galactic/)
- (D) Pedersen on why every player must matter: his first co-op session with a skilled colleague: "He was super good and I was really bad, so I was like a brick around his leg"; the game was built so newcomers and veterans both feel they add value. On classes: each class has traversal tools that help the team explore, and "If you're missing one class, you always feel like you're missing out on some mechanics." On community: "you can just jump in, play with strangers, and have a great experience." — [Game Rant interview](https://gamerant.com/deep-rock-galactic-interview-season-1-co-op-design-battle-pass-progression/)
- (D) Early-access interview: Gunner "is basically a huge gun on legs, and he is there primarily to protect the group"; Driller's drills "can very quickly make tunnels for the entire team to use"; Engineer sets sentries and platforms and platforms are part of the terrain; Scout's flare gun lights caves via dynamic lighting; class duplicates allowed ("if you want two Scouts and two Gunners, then you can"). — [Co-Optimus interview p.2](https://www.co-optimus.com/editorial/1978/page/2/indie-ana-co-op-and-the-deep-rock-galactic-interview.html) (via search summary)
- (M) Store copy: "Destroy everything around you to reach your goal. There is no set path"; teams can drill straight to the objective or build path networks. — via [Co-Optimus/press summary](https://co-optimus.com/editorial/1978/page/3/indie-ana-co-op-and-the-deep-rock-galactic-interview.html)
- (D, reported) Mission Control exists because Pedersen felt nonlinear, chaotic caves needed a "foreman" to explain what's going on and put things in context. — reported in search results around [Ghost Ship/PSU coverage](https://www.psu.com/news/rock-and-stone-ghost-ship-games-ceo-soren-lundgaard-talks-deep-rock-galactics-upcoming-playstation-launch/) (exact page of origin not verified)
- Mission loop: drop pod, complete objective (mine, repair equipment, etc.), then extract. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Rock_Galactic)
- (P, community wiki) Swarms: Mission Control announces an imminent swarm ~20 s ahead so players can prepare; delay between swarms on Hazard 1 is ~350-500 s. Work-in-progress fan data. — [DRG wiki Swarm/WIP](https://deeprockgalactic.wiki.gg/wiki/Swarm/WIP)
- (C) Reviewer: "a really nice balance is hit between quieter moments where players can freely explore and those where they'll need to hunker down and defend themselves." — [Frostilyte mini review](https://frostilyte.ca/2021/08/04/mini-review-deep-rock-galactic/)
- (D) Progression: seasonal free Performance Pass layered on character/weapon progression; "The fact we aren't monetizing ours I think makes it clear we're doing it for fun." — [Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/deep-rock-galactic-interview-season-1-co-op-design-battle-pass-progression/)

### Inferences
- **Objective as the main activity, horde as the pressure.** DRG's structural lesson for Meatgrinder: give each arena a *thing to do* that the horde interrupts. See the "Objectives under pressure" section for concrete arena objectives. 
- **Class tools that help the team, not only kill.** DRG interdependence is via traversal/utility. Meatgrinder classes are combat-only; a cheap analogue is giving each class one *team utility* tied to objectives or breakables, e.g. Fallen's leap slam breaks barricades/seals, Binder's chains pull a relic/lever from range, Betrayer's Silver Bullet shoots distant switches/bells, Heretic Saint's censer purifies a corruption zone so others can stand there. This creates "we need the Saint here" moments without hard locks (keep duplicate classes viable; bots fill missing roles). Cost **M** (objective props that respond to specific abilities + bot logic to use them).
- **Terrain destruction** at DRG scale is **L** and probably off-premise for a 2.5D billboard engine; a cheap substitute that answers "break stuff" is destructible props/walls as discrete objects (breakable seals, pillars, braziers that explode, gibbable statues). Cost **S-M**.
- **Telegraphed swarm against objective** = ~20 s announcer warning ("They're coming!") before a scripted-by-director wave. Meatgrinder's director already has build-up; adding a voiced/text "foreman" warning is **S**.
- **Social glue.** A dedicated "salute" shout key (DRG's "Rock and Stone!") that bots answer, plus a hub moment between runs, is **S** and strongly fits drop-in co-op with strangers. (Design intent of "Rock and Stone" not sourced; see Gaps.)
- **Mission Control/foreman voice** is useful for "what do I do now" once objectives exist. Cost **S** (text) to **M** (voice).

## Killing Floor 2, Helldivers 2, World War Z, Back 4 Blood: trader, Zed Time, gore, stratagems, friendly fire, swarm tech, cards

### Takeaway
KF2 shows that weapon feel and spectacle (gore, slow-motion) can be the core, and that a between-wave trader turns "shoot" into "choose". Helldivers 2 adds choice through called-in stratagems and a strict systemic rule that friendly fire is always on, which its director says creates improvised thinking and comedy. WWZ shows crowd physics (zombie piles) making defend objectives interesting, but critics found its objectives generic. Back 4 Blood's card system shows the risk of over-complicating L4D's simplicity.

### Cited Findings
**Killing Floor 2**
- (D) John Gibson (Tripwire) on Zed Time: "In Killing Floor 2 it was much more challenging because we set out to resolve players' biggest complaint from the first game - getting Zed Time when nothing was going on around them"; Zed Time triggers by random chance on each kill, and the chance increases when the player does things that "will look cool for them to see in slow motion - blasting a zed with a shotgun up close, getting a headshot, killing multiple zeds at the same time." High-frame-rate mocap animations were recorded for Zed Time. — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/killing-floor-2/killing-floor-2-zed-time-trigger) (via search summary)
- (D) Gore: blood persists for the whole match ("every drop of blood stays on the map for an entire match"); the M.E.A.T. system, inspired by Soldier of Fortune's GHOUL; reported 19 dismemberment points (5 on the head); to Tripwire the gore system is "the most important feature." — [PC Gamer gore system](https://www.pcgamer.com/killing-floor-2-gore-system/); [Wikipedia KF2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_Floor_2) (via search summary)
- (D, reported) Tripwire wanted melee that "never felt tacked on", i.e. that you would still want to play with no guns; reload animations performed by a combat specialist; fire rates matched to real counterparts. — [Rely on Horror preview](https://www.relyonhorror.com/in-depth/hands-off-preview-killing-floor-2s-bullets-blades-and-blood-made-my-nose-bleed/) (via search summary)
- (C) Previews/reviews: weapons "feel as chunky as ever and there's a sense of weight that few first person games can replicate"; "all of this blood and gore wouldn't count for much if the weapons weren't fun to use"; shotguns differ in knockback (double-barrel launches Clots, AA-12 cuts cleanly). — [GameRevolution preview](https://www.gamerevolution.com/previews/65122-killing-floor-2-preview); [New Game Network review](https://www.newgamenetwork.com/article/1583/killing-floor-2-review/) (via search summary)

**Helldivers 2**
- (D) Johan Pilestedt: "The most important thing when we make games is believability"; "Things should be consistent in the game world and therefore, we must have friendly fire"; if bullets kill enemies "then logic dictates that your bullets must also be able to kill your friends"; friendly fire creates "a complexity to the gameplay where players have to actively think during combat sequences"; "the chuckles that happen when you accidentally carpet bomb your friends allow for a playfulness"; it "makes the players a true interactable part of the game world" and "plays a great part in positioning and helps with team play." — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/helldivers-2/friendly-fire)
- (D, reported) The rule extends to stratagems: supply pods can kill teammates, so players think about timing, beacon placement and warning squadmates; the approach is "inherently systematic." — [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/helldivers-2-friendly-fire-always-on-arrowhead-ps5-pc-1851329269); friendly fire will not become optional — [Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/139269/helldivers-2-friendly-fire-stays-on)

**World War Z (Saber)**
- (D/M) Saber built its own "Swarm Engine" for up to 500 zombies on screen, moving as one horde or breaking into individuals. — [WWZ dev diary press release](https://www.focus-entmt.com/uk/news/world-war-z-dev-diary-takes-you-behind-the-scenes-of-the-intense-co-op-zombie-shooter) (via search summary)

**Back 4 Blood (Turtle Rock)**
- (C) Bit-tech: initially "aghast at the confusing mess", calling it "a massive overcomplication of Left4Dead's simple and elegant multiplayer fun", though the reviewer warmed to it; "a good enough successor... without being brilliant"; acts take 5-6 hours. — [Bit-tech review](https://bit-tech.net/reviews/back4blood-review/1)
- (P) Card-system complaints: launch version gave one card per mission; frequent rebalancing undercut deckbuilding; a card added to remove attachments was seen as patching a base-game issue with a card. — [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/924970/discussions/0/3873717500290570910); [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/924970/discussions/0/596262211620471437)

### Inferences
- **Trader between arenas (KF2) = the most direct fix for "no choices".** Between arenas (Meatgrinder already goes arena to arena) offer a short pick: spend kill-currency on one of 2-3 class-specific weapon upgrades/passives (e.g. Silver Bullet pierces +2, censer radius, slam leaves fire). Keeps distinctive guns while giving per-run choice. Cost **M** (UI that must not move/resize per memory note, upgrade data, bot auto-pick, net sync). Watch B4B's lesson: keep the option count tiny and the effects readable (memory: one visible effect per ability).
- **Zed-Time-style slow-motion on spectacular kills** (multi-kill with Silver Bullet, leap slam into 20 Blessed). Pure spectacle, cheap in single player; in multiplayer, either global short slow-mo for all (KF2-style, needs host-authoritative time scale) or local-only visual effect (screen flash + sound) to avoid desync. Cost **S** (local VFX) to **M** (shared time scale).
- **Gore/persistent blood** reinforces "the horde is meat": billboard gib sprites, persistent floor decals, dismember frames for Blessed. Cost **M-L** (mostly art; decal persistence vs performance for 1000+ enemies must be benchmarked when the user asks).
- **Stratagem-like call-ins.** Helldivers' "call it in, it lands after a delay, anyone under it gets hit" is a natural shape for the planned M13 ultimates (dramatic anticipation + positioning). Cost **M**, out of M12 scope.
- **Friendly fire**: not recommended in full for 1000-enemy AoE with bots (bots would need FF-aware aim; L4D bots simply cannot deal FF). A cheap middle ground: ally *knockback* or *stagger* from big AoE (leap slam, explosions) without damage, giving a light positioning consideration. Cost **S**, risk of annoyance.
- **Crowd piling (WWZ)** makes defend points interesting visually; with billboards, a cheap version is enemies stacking against a barricade and breaking it. Cost **M**.

## Team dependency: what makes players need each other without being annoying, and what works with bots

### Takeaway
The sources agree on a pattern: create **helpless states** that only teammates can end (pins, incapacitation), make the *rescue easy and heroic*, make abandoning the group implicitly dangerous (stragglers get hunted), but **always leave a skilled solo out** (Darktide "clutch") and **never use heavy-handed enforcement** (Valve: no leashes or out-of-bounds damage). Bots work when they follow simple trust rules and when the game can assume a full party for tuning.

### Cited Findings
- (D) Valve: incapacitating specials "Make players fear becoming separated from the group" and "Give players near the incapacitated victim the opportunity to be the hero"; "Players really enjoy helping each other." — [Booth GDC 2009](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/GDC2009_ReplayableCooperativeGameDesign_Left4Dead.pdf)
- (D) Valve: "Players rebel against overtly heavy-handed punishments" — no invisible leashes, teleporting stragglers or out-of-bounds damage; enforce cooperation implicitly through genre-fitting enemy design. — same source
- (D) Valve: helplessness works because "Easy to assist helpless player" and "Benefit clearly outweighs cost (usually)"; you will "likely be in a similar situation soon." — same source
- (D) Fatshark: disablers "try to disable one player, forcing others to respond", but every special can be clutched solo because otherwise the loop becomes "more toxic." — [Fatshark dev blog](https://forums.fatsharkgames.com/t/darktide-101-enemy-design-and-prototyping-dev-blog/98452)
- (D) Fatshark: "getting charged constantly got too tiring" — disablers need cooldowns/pauses or they become annoying. — same source
- (D) Ghost Ship: class tools create dependency ("If you're missing one class, you always feel like you're missing out on some mechanics") yet duplicates are allowed. — [Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/deep-rock-galactic-interview-season-1-co-op-design-battle-pass-progression/); [Co-Optimus](https://www.co-optimus.com/editorial/1978/page/2/indie-ana-co-op-and-the-deep-rock-galactic-interview.html)
- (D) Arrowhead: friendly fire "plays a great part in positioning and helps with team play." — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/helldivers-2/friendly-fire)
- (D) Valve bots: tuned for an assumed 4-person team; bots prioritize humans, never deal friendly fire, never use Molotovs, teleport to the group when unseen; "Difficult issue: Rescuing incapacitated humans." — [Booth AIIDE 2009](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)
- (D) Valve: automatic vocalizations ("Hunter's got Zoey!") provide awareness and camaraderie without voice chat. — [Booth GDC 2009](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/GDC2009_ReplayableCooperativeGameDesign_Left4Dead.pdf)

### Inferences
- Ranking for Meatgrinder by value/cost (my judgment):
  1. **Pins with clutch escape + bot rescue priority** (M). Biggest gain in "need each other" moments; works with bots if bots treat "teammate pinned" as top priority and humans can clutch.
  2. **Straggler hunter behaviour** (S once a pouncer exists): the director preferentially sends the pouncer at the player farthest from the group, the Hunter's stated purpose. Implicit cohesion without leashes.
  3. **Healer/tank roles with explicit "save" verbs**: Heretic Saint's censer could free a pinned ally or revive faster; Fallen's taunt pulls a disabler off a teammate. Gives classes team-facing choices. **S-M**.
  4. **Barks/callouts** from bots for pins and specials (**S**), important because humans with bots lack voice chat.
  5. **Shared-resource sharing** (pass a healing item) is valuable per Valve but needs an item system (**M**) — defer until pickups are in scope.
- Annoyance guards: disabler cooldowns, max one active pin per player, clear audio cue before the pounce, and never two disablers at once in Relax phase (Valve: no specials in Relax).
- Full friendly fire is the riskiest dependency mechanic with bots and 1000-enemy AoE; skip or limit to knockback.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data (e.g. playtest metrics) on how often pins should occur before players find them annoying; Fatshark only reports qualitatively that constant charges were tiring.
- No source on how Fatshark's or Ghost Ship's bots handle rescues.

## Objectives under pressure: why "do X while the horde comes" beats "kill everything"

### Takeaway
Designers' statements converge: shooting alone flattens into routine; tension comes from a *competing demand* — a goal the team must progress while the horde interrupts — because it forces the team to split attention, choose who defends and who works, and move through space (DRG: mine/repair then extract; L4D: crescendos, finales, escape vehicle; WWZ: defend points with piling hordes). Critics note objectives become stale if they are just labels ("defend this, gather that"), so the objective must change how players move and prioritize.

### Cited Findings
- (D) Valve's anticipation list includes finales/crescendo events that "start with an angry crowd shrieking in the distance", "Watching the escape vehicle arrive while fighting for your life", and car alarms (player choice whether to risk the mob). — [Booth GDC 2009](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/GDC2009_ReplayableCooperativeGameDesign_Left4Dead.pdf)
- (D) Valve on the Tank: "Halts forward momentum", "Forces Survivors to defend instead of assault" — i.e. a forced change of activity is itself a pacing tool. — same source
- (D) Valve: "Constant, unchanging combat is fatiguing"; peaks and valleys are needed. — same source
- (D) Ghost Ship: core loop is explore + "search for precious minerals and work together as a group"; mission objective then extraction. — [PCGamesInsider](https://www.pcgamesinsider.biz/indie-interview/66706/we-feel-the-co-op-shooter-market-is-underdeveloped-ghost-ship-games-tells-us-about-deep-rock-galactic/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Rock_Galactic)
- (C) DRG balances "quieter moments where players can freely explore and those where they'll need to hunker down and defend themselves." — [Frostilyte](https://frostilyte.ca/2021/08/04/mini-review-deep-rock-galactic/)
- (C) WWZ's horde behaviour adds a new dimension to defend-a-point encounters, but objectives "are not especially innovative: defend this, gather that, or kill a set number of enemies." — [Xbox Tavern review](https://www.xboxtavern.com/world-war-z-review/) (via search summary)
- (D) Arrowhead: systemic rules make players "actively think during combat sequences" rather than solving fixed puzzles. — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/helldivers-2/friendly-fire)
- (D) Fatshark: specials force players "to quickly decide which threats to focus on first" — prioritization under pressure is the fun. — [Fatshark dev blog](https://forums.fatsharkgames.com/t/darktide-101-enemy-design-and-prototyping-dev-blog/98452)

### Inferences
Concrete arena objectives for Meatgrinder (arena format, ~8-minute run, so 1 objective per arena and short), ordered cheapest first:
- **Destroy N targets** (idols/reliquaries/spawn shrines spread across the arena; each destroyed one spikes the director). Satisfies "break stuff", forces splitting up (which disablers then punish). Cost **S** (breakable prop with HP + objective counter UI + bot targeting).
- **Hold point / ritual circle** (stand in a zone for X seconds while hordes come; progress pauses if nobody is in it). Forces positioning and gives the tank/healer clear jobs; Choristers/Cherubs shooting into the zone create pressure. Cost **S-M** (zone + progress bar + director "siege" mode + bot "stand in zone" behaviour).
- **Carry** (carry a relic/head/bell to the exit gate; the carrier is slowed or can only use melee/one ability). Classic L4D2 scavenge/WWZ shape; creates escort-the-carrier teamwork and a real choice of *who* carries. Cost **M** (carried state that changes the player's loadout + drop/pick logic + bots).
- **Activate in sequence** (light 3 braziers, then the gate opens; each lighting triggers a crescendo). Moves the fight through the arena. Cost **S-M**.
- **Escort** (a slowly moving cart/procession) is the most expensive (pathing object, its own HP, bot behaviour) and in critic opinion among the most disliked objective types; I found no source for that last claim, so treat it as unverified. Cost **M-L**.
- Design guard from the WWZ critique: each objective type must change *how players move and prioritize* (split vs stack, hold vs advance, who carries), not just relabel "kill everything".
- Combine with the director: objective events should be the "Build Up" trigger, and the extraction gate can mirror L4D's "escape vehicle arriving while fighting" (dramatic anticipation at arena end). Cost **S** on top of objectives.
- Scope note (memory: core fun first / scope discipline): the cheapest bundle that addresses all playtester complaints is probably (a) one disabler special with clutch, (b) one elite, (c) "destroy targets" + "hold point" objectives, (d) breakable props that summon mobs, (e) anticipation audio. Pickups, trader and ultimates can follow. This is my inference and should be confirmed with the user before adding enemies.

### Gaps
- No designer statement found that explicitly argues "objectives beat pure killing"; the case is assembled from Valve/Ghost Ship/Fatshark statements about pacing, prioritization and activity changes plus critic opinion.
- No sources retrieved on Helldivers 2's objective design (e.g. terminals, extraction under pressure) or Vermintide 2's event objectives (e.g. defend/carry events during hordes).
- No Game Maker's Toolkit or Noclip analysis was retrieved for any of these games in this session.
