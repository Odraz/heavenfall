# Milestone 10 Specification: Beautiful levels

The levels look dull next to the concept art (`assets/art-src/reference/concept-1.webp` to `concept-3.webp`): a flat gradient sky, flat pale fog, four repeating textures on bare boxes. This milestone makes the world look like the concept art: a painted sky full of floating spires, richer marble and gold surfaces, warm atmosphere, and real architecture (arches, columns, balustrades) around the arenas. It changes how the world is drawn, nothing else: no gameplay, simulation, protocol, enemy or class changes.

**The overriding rule is performance.** The game exists to fight hundreds to thousands of enemies at once (MVP §12: 1 500 living enemies at 60 FPS on an integrated GPU). Every feature here is chosen to cost almost nothing per frame, and every stage has to prove it (§2).

All numbers are **initial values**, as in the MVP.

## 1. Goals and rules

**Goals:**
1. A screenshot of the game next to the concept art reads as the same world: bright painted sky, ivory marble and gold, soft warm haze, grand architecture.
2. Nothing in this milestone changes the frame rate by more than the gate allows (§2.3).

**Rules:**
- **Bake, don't compute.** Light, shadow and ambient occlusion are computed **once, when the level is built**, and stored in vertex colors, as the terrain's shading is today. No real-time lights, no shadow maps.
- **No post-processing.** No bloom, outline, SSAO, color-grading or any other full-screen pass. The ink outlines are painted into the textures.
- **No transparency over large areas.** Transparent and additive surfaces are limited to the few small quads of §5; each is a handful of pixels deep on screen, not a screen-sized layer, so they can't multiply the cost of the enemy swarm.
- **Merge and instance.** Static scenery is merged into a few meshes, or drawn with `InstancedMesh`, so draw calls stay flat as the scenery grows.
- **Scenery is decoration only.** It doesn't collide, doesn't block line of sight, isn't in the map's grid, and the simulation, flow fields and bots never see it. It sits **outside the walkable area** or above it, never on it, so it can't hide enemies or change what a player can see in a way the host doesn't know.
- **Enemies stay readable.** The MVP's rule holds: white and gold enemies with dark outlines must stand out against the world. If a texture or prop makes a Blessed hard to see against it, it's wrong, not the enemy. Floors stay **mid-to-light and low in contrast**; strong detail goes on walls, props and the sky.

## 2. Performance

### 2.1 Budgets

Added by this whole milestone, measured against the code at the start of stage 1:

| Item | Budget |
|---|---|
| Draw calls | **at most +10** |
| Static scenery triangles | **at most 150 000** in total, however many are in view |
| GPU texture memory (with mipmaps) | **at most +120 MB** |
| Download added to the first load | **at most +12 MB** (sky and tiling textures without transparency are shipped as JPEG or WebP, see §3.3) |
| Per-frame JavaScript | **nothing new** except moving the sky with the camera. Anything animated moves in a vertex shader, driven by one shared time uniform |
| Full-screen passes | **0** |
| Real-time lights and shadows | **0** |
| Level build time (baking, merging) | **at most +300 ms** |

### 2.2 Cheap by design

- **Sky:** the existing dome with a texture instead of a gradient: still one draw call. Drawn **after** the opaque terrain with the depth test on, so the pixels the terrain already covers are skipped instead of being painted twice. The agent measures this ordering against drawing it first (§2.3) and keeps the faster.
- **Fog:** the existing linear fog, with its color matched to the sky's horizon so distance blends into the sky. Free.
- **Textures:** every tiling texture is at most **1 024 × 1 024** at runtime, mipmapped, anisotropy 4 as today. Several terrain looks share **one atlas per mesh**, so adding a texture variant adds no draw call.
- **Light shafts, clouds:** a few dozen quads in total, additive, distant, drawn as one instanced mesh (§5).
- **Props:** textured with the **existing terrain textures** (world-aligned UVs, as the terrain is), so they add no textures and merge into the terrain's meshes or one prop mesh.

### 2.3 The performance gate

The bench's FPS is capped at the display's refresh rate (60), which hides a slowdown until it's large. So this milestone measures the **uncapped** frame time too.

- **Stage 1 first adds `npm run bench -- --uncapped`**: the same benchmark (`?bench=1`, 1 500 enemies) with Chromium started with `--disable-frame-rate-limit` and `--disable-gpu-vsync`, reporting average frame time in ms alongside FPS. If the flags don't uncap it on the machine, say so in `bench.md` and use the capped numbers with the **1%-low FPS** as the sensitive measure.
- **Baseline:** before changing anything, record the uncapped result at the start of stage 1 in `docs/bench.md` (row *M10 baseline*). Run it three times and keep the median.
- **After every stage:** run it again (median of three) and record it. **A stage passes** when all of these hold:
  - capped average FPS is at least 58 (the MVP's target);
  - average simulation time is at most 8 ms (unchanged, since nothing here touches the simulation);
  - the **uncapped average frame time is at most 6% above the baseline**, and **at most 10% above after the last stage**;
  - the budgets of §2.1 hold, checked with `renderer.info` (draw calls, triangles, textures) printed by the benchmark.
- **If a stage fails**, cut in this order until it passes, and record what was cut in `decisions.md`: fewer props → fewer light shafts → the sky drawn first instead of last (if that was faster, keep it) → lower texture sizes → fewer texture variants. Never cut the sky.
- Benchmark runs need an active, uncovered desktop (see the M8.5 note in `bench.md`). A throttled run (under 5 FPS) is repeated, not recorded.
- The final performance on the target laptop is a human checkpoint (§10).

## 3. The sky

### 3.1 What it looks like

A bright painted sky like the concept art: big sunlit clouds, floating spires, towers and arches in the distance, warm haze at the horizon, rich blue overhead. It replaces the gradient in `makeSky()` ([scene.ts](../src/render/scene.ts)).

### 3.2 How it's mapped

Image generators can't produce a clean equirectangular panorama, so the sky is a **wide horizon band** with plain color above and below it:

- `sky-day` is a 21:9 band that wraps once around the player (360°) **with square pixels**, so it isn't stretched: its height covers 360° × 9 ÷ 21 ≈ 154° of elevation. The horizon is the row where the cloud sea meets the haze (found by eye, recorded as a constant). Claude makes the left and right edges meet (cross-fading the seam) when preparing it.
- **Above the band**, the shader blends from the band's top-row color (averaged) to the zenith color, a deeper blue sampled from the band, so there is no visible edge.
- **Below the horizon band**, it blends to the fog color. The player never sees below the terrain except across a cliff edge, where this reads as haze.
- The shader samples **one texture** with the view direction (`atan2` for the horizontal position, the elevation for the vertical), so the sky stays a single draw call with one texture lookup per pixel.
- **Fog color** (`FOG_COLOR`) is sampled from the band's horizon row, so far terrain fades into the sky without a visible line. The fog distances (40 to 150 m) stay as they are unless a screenshot shows the distance reading badly.
- **Size:** the source is 21:9 at the largest size Gemini gives. At runtime it's **4 096 × 1 755** at most (about 38 MB with mipmaps), shipped as JPEG or WebP. If the memory budget or the load time suffers, use 2 048 wide and say so in `decisions.md`.

### 3.3 Image formats

Source images may be PNG or JPEG (`.jfif` and `.jpg` included). Wherever this document or art-prompts.md names a source as `.png`, a JPEG with the same base name is accepted.

Images without transparency (the sky, the tiling terrain textures) are converted to **JPEG or WebP** by the build script, quality about 88. Images with transparency or additive effects stay PNG. The conversion is a build-time step in `scripts/`, as `cutout.py` is today.

## 4. Surfaces and baked light

### 4.1 Textures

New tiling textures (prompts in art-prompts.md), each 4 × 4 m and repeating like the existing four, except the cornice:

| File | Used for |
|---|---|
| `tex-floor-plain.png` | Variation for floors, so large floors don't visibly repeat a 2 × 2 tile pattern. |
| `tex-floor-medallion.png` | One large gold medallion, placed at the center of arenas and in front of doors. |
| `tex-wall-window.png` | Walls with a tall arched window painted in recess, sky-blue glass with a dark inner edge. |
| `tex-wall-pilaster.png` | A fluted pilaster strip, placed between windows. |
| `tex-cornice.png` | A horizontal gold-and-ivory cornice, the top 2 m of every wall. Tiles left to right only. |

**Preparing them** (Claude, by script, from the human's images):
- `tex-floor-plain`: its gold lines are 4 m apart, but `tex-floor`'s are 2 m apart, and its marble veins break at the edges. Shift it by half its size (so the cross moves to the edges and hides the broken veins), then paint the same thin gold cross through the new center. The result is a 2 × 2 grid like `tex-floor`'s. Match its brightness to `tex-floor`.
- `tex-wall-window`: `tex-wall` and `tex-wall-pilaster` have a carved frieze band along the bottom, which lines up across neighboring wall tiles; the window wall has none. Copy the band from `tex-wall` into its bottom rows (below the window frame).
- `tex-cornice`: crop to the band and cross-fade the left and right edges.

The existing `tex-floor`, `tex-riser`, `tex-wall` and `tex-door` stay. **The agent may ask the human to regenerate them at 2 048 px** if they look soft next to the new ones; that's a decision, not a requirement.

- **Choosing a look per cell:** deterministic, from the cell's coordinates and the level data, never random per session, so every client builds the same world. Walls alternate window, pilaster, window along their length. Floors use `tex-floor` mostly, with `tex-floor-plain` in a coarse pattern (blocks of about 4 × 4 cells), `tex-floor-medallion` where §4.3 says.
- **One atlas** per terrain mesh (tops, risers, walls) holds that mesh's textures, so adding variants adds **no draw calls**. Atlas cells are padded so mipmaps don't bleed between them.
- **Cornice:** the top 2 m of every wall column is built as its own quad strip using `tex-cornice`, merged into the wall mesh.

### 4.2 Baked lighting

The terrain already shades by face direction (vertex colors, [terrain.ts](../src/render/terrain.ts)). Replace that with a baked result of the same cost at runtime:

- **Sun:** one fixed direction per level (low and warm, from the side), so faces toward the sun are lighter and warm-tinted, faces away are cooler.
- **Shadow:** a cell is in shadow when a wall or a higher floor lies between it and the sun (a march along the sun direction on the heightfield, at level build time).
- **Ambient occlusion:** each vertex darkens by how many neighbors are higher than it (corners, floor-to-wall joins, the bottom of stairs).
- **Result:** vertex colors, tinted warm in the light and cool-blue in shadow. Nothing else changes at runtime.
- **Rule:** the darkest shadow is still **mid-brightness** (no more than 40% darker than the lit floor), so enemies stay readable in it.

### 4.3 Placement markers

Levels already name arenas, entries and exits. The medallion goes at **each arena's center** and **in front of each door** (one cell out), derived from existing level data. No new level-file fields unless the agent can't derive them; if it adds one, it records it in `decisions.md`.

## 5. Atmosphere

All of it is decoration around the play space, tiny in pixels and count.

| Effect | What | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Light shafts** | 12 to 24 tall, thin, additive quads using `fx-lightshaft.png`, slanting through the gaps between props and walls toward the baked sun direction, each 1 to 3 m wide and 8 to 20 m tall, placed deterministically around arenas. Drawn as **one instanced mesh**, depth-tested, no depth write. Their brightness breathes ±10% over about 8 s in the vertex shader. | 1 draw call |
| **Distant clouds** | The existing `decor-cloud-tuft` sprite, 20 to 40 of them, placed beyond the walls and below the terrain's edge, drifting slowly in the vertex shader, to give depth under the floating level. | uses the existing sprite atlas |
| **Haze** | Done by the fog color of §3.2. Nothing extra. | 0 |

- **Quads facing the camera that are large on screen are forbidden** (that's the overdraw trap). Shafts are at least 6 m from any point a player can stand on, and fade out (in the shader) as the camera gets closer than 10 m.
- **Particles**: no new ones. The existing embers and feathers are unchanged.

## 6. Architecture

### 6.1 The props

Modeled in Blender (the `scripts/blender/` pipeline) from the prop sheets, exported as small low-poly meshes, and placed around and beyond the arenas:

| Prop | Triangles (each) | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Arch** | at most 400 | Spanning the open ends between wall columns, above head height (at least 6 m up, never over a walkable cell at head height), and along the outer walls. |
| **Column** | at most 250 | In rows along arena edges, **inside the wall line** (never on a walkable cell), and as free-standing rows beyond the walls. |
| **Balustrade** | at most 200 per 2 m section | Along cliff edges and terrace rims, at the edge of the walkable cells (just outside them). |
| **Spire / dome cluster** | at most 1 500 | Beyond the walls, 40 to 120 m out, mostly hidden by fog: the far skyline. |
| **Floating island** | at most 1 500 | 60 to 160 m out, above and below the level, with a tiny building on it. |

- **UVs and textures:** world-aligned like the terrain, using the existing terrain textures, with gold accent as vertex colors. No new textures.
- **Merged:** all static props of a level are baked into **one mesh** (or one per distance band, at most 3) at level build time. No per-prop objects remain at runtime.
- **Culling:** the far skyline and islands are separate meshes from the near props, so the engine culls them by frustum.
- **Placement:** deterministic from the map (§1 *Scenery is decoration only*). A function takes the map and returns the prop list; a unit test checks that **no prop's footprint overlaps a walkable cell** (§7).
- **Budget:** the triangle budget of §2.1 is the cap. The agent places props until it's reached, near props first.

### 6.2 Asset format

Props are exported from Blender as **glTF (`.glb`)**, loaded once at the start, merged and discarded. If `GLTFLoader` costs more than it's worth in bundle size, a tiny custom binary format is allowed. Record the choice in `decisions.md`.

## 7. Tests

### 7.1 Unit tests (additions)

| Area | Required checks |
|---|---|
| Baked lighting | A cell behind a wall in the sun's direction is darker than the same cell in the open. The darkest vertex color is at least 60% of the brightest. Baking a map twice gives identical colors. |
| Texture choice | The same map always yields the same texture per cell. Walls alternate window and pilaster. A medallion sits at each arena's center and one cell in front of each door, and nowhere else. |
| Scenery placement | For `sandbox` and `pearly-gates`: no prop footprint overlaps a walkable cell, and the placement is identical on two runs. Total triangles are within §2.1. |
| Scenery is invisible to the simulation | The simulation's performance test (MVP §13.1) is unchanged and still passes. |

## 8. Stages

1. **Gate and sky.** The uncapped benchmark and the baseline (§2.3), the JPEG/WebP build step (§3.3), the sky and fog color (§3). *Art checkpoint:* `sky-day.png`.
2. **Surfaces.** The atlas, the new textures and their placement (§4.1, §4.3), the cornice, and baked lighting (§4.2). *Art checkpoint:* the five textures. The tests of §7.1 for baking and texture choice.
3. **Atmosphere.** Light shafts and distant clouds (§5). *Art checkpoint:* `fx-lightshaft.png`.
4. **Architecture.** The props, merging and placement (§6). *Art checkpoint:* the five prop sheets. The scenery tests of §7.1.

## 9. Out of scope

Real-time lights, shadows or post-processing of any kind, normal or PBR materials, a graphics-quality setting in the menu (the performance gate replaces it; add one only if the human asks), a different sky per arena, a day-night cycle or weather, new levels or arenas, water, reflections, destructible scenery, animated scenery beyond the shaft and cloud motion above, new enemy or class art.
