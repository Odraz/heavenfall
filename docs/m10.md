# Milestone 10 Specification: Beautiful levels

The levels look dull next to the concept art (`assets/art-src/reference/concept-1.webp` to `concept-3.webp`). Every level is a sealed box: every wall is 16 m tall, so from the middle of a 40 m arena the walls hide everything below about 36° above the horizon. Above them is a flat gradient sky, and the walls themselves are four repeating textures on bare boxes. This milestone opens the world up and makes it look like the concept art:
- walls only as tall as they need to be;
- open edges with balustrades and arcades where the level meets the sky;
- a painted sky with a sea of clouds below the level;
- richer marble and gold surfaces with baked sunlight;
- light shafts and distant spires.

It changes how the world is drawn. It makes **one simulation change**: walls get their own heights, which line of sight uses (§3). It changes no movement, enemies, classes or protocol.

**The overriding rule is performance.** The game exists to fight hundreds to thousands of enemies at once (MVP §12: 1 500 living enemies at 60 FPS on an integrated GPU). Every feature here is chosen to cost almost nothing per frame, and every stage has to prove it (§2).

**You never see into another arena.** Lower walls and open edges must not show one arena's enemies or layout from another (§3.1).

All numbers are **initial values**, as in the MVP.

## 1. Goals and rules

**Goals:**
1. A screenshot of the game next to the concept art reads as the same world: bright painted sky, open edges over a sea of clouds, ivory marble and gold, soft warm haze, grand architecture.
2. Walls are as low as the "never see another arena" rule allows, not 16 m everywhere.
3. Nothing in this milestone changes the frame rate by more than the gate allows (§2.3).

**Rules:**
- **Bake, don't compute.** Light, shadow and ambient occlusion are computed **once, when the level is built**, and stored in vertex colors, as the terrain's shading is today. No real-time lights, no shadow maps.
- **No post-processing.** No bloom, outline, SSAO, color-grading or any other full-screen pass. The ink outlines are painted into the textures.
- **No transparency over large areas.** Transparent and additive surfaces are limited to the light shafts and clouds of §6, which fade out before they grow large on screen.
- **Merge and instance.** Static scenery is merged into a few meshes, or drawn with `InstancedMesh`, so draw calls stay flat as the scenery grows.
- **What you see is what the host knows.** A wall is as tall in the simulation as it's drawn (§3.4). Props are decoration: they don't collide and aren't in the map's grid. They sit where they can't hide an enemy or block a shot that the simulation lets through (§7).
- **Enemies stay readable.** The MVP's rule holds: white and gold enemies with dark outlines must stand out against the world, **now including the sky and the cloud sea behind the open edges**. If a texture, prop or the sky makes a Blessed hard to see against it, it's wrong, not the enemy. Floors stay **mid-to-light and low in contrast**; strong detail goes on walls, props and the sky.

## 2. Performance

### 2.1 Budgets

Added by this whole milestone, measured against the code at the start of stage 1:

| Item | Budget |
|---|---|
| Draw calls | **at most +10** |
| Static scenery triangles (props, cliffs, parapets) | **at most 150 000** in total, however many are in view |
| GPU texture memory (with mipmaps) | **at most +120 MB** |
| Download added to the first load | **at most +12 MB** (the sky and the tiling textures are shipped as JPEG or WebP, §4.3) |
| Per-frame JavaScript | **nothing new** except moving the sky with the camera. Anything animated moves in a vertex shader, driven by one shared time uniform |
| Full-screen passes | **0** |
| Real-time lights and shadows | **0** |
| Level build time (heights, baking, merging) | **at most +300 ms** |
| Simulation | the simulation performance test (MVP §13.1) is unchanged and passes |

### 2.2 Cheap by design

- **Lower walls cost nothing.** Pixels that were wall become sky: one texture lookup per pixel, about the same cost.
- **Sky:** the existing dome with a texture instead of a gradient, still one draw call. It's drawn **after** the opaque terrain with the depth test on, so pixels the terrain already covers are skipped. The agent measures this order against drawing it first (§2.3) and keeps the faster.
- **Fog:** the existing linear fog, with its color matched to the sky's horizon (§4.2). Free.
- **Textures:** every tiling texture is at most **1 024 × 1 024** at runtime, mipmapped, anisotropy 4 as today. The terrain's looks share **one texture array** (WebGL2 `DataArrayTexture`, one layer per look, chosen per vertex), so adding a look adds no draw call (§5.1).
- **Light shafts, clouds:** a few dozen quads in total, drawn as one instanced mesh each (§6).
- **Props:** textured with the terrain's texture array (world-aligned UVs, like the terrain), so they add no textures, merged into at most 3 meshes (§7).

### 2.3 The performance gate

The bench's FPS is capped at the display's refresh rate (60), which hides a slowdown until it's large. So this milestone also measures the **uncapped** frame time.

- **Stage 1 first adds `npm run bench -- --uncapped`**: the same benchmark (`?bench=1`, 1 500 enemies) with Chromium started with `--disable-frame-rate-limit` and `--disable-gpu-vsync`, reporting average frame time in ms alongside FPS. If the flags don't uncap it on the machine, say so in `bench.md` and use the capped numbers, with the **1%-low FPS** as the sensitive measure.
- **Both maps.** Stage 1 also adds `--map <id>`, which passes `map=<id>` to the page. The gate runs on `sandbox` (as today) **and** on `pearly-gates` (the swarm at Arena 1's entry), because the scenery matters most there.
- **Baseline:** before changing anything, record the uncapped result on both maps in `docs/bench.md` (rows *M10 baseline*). Run each 3 times and keep the median.
- **After every stage:** run it again (median of 3 on each map) and record it. **A stage passes** when, on both maps, all of these hold:
  - capped average FPS is at least 58 (the MVP's target);
  - average simulation time is at most 8 ms;
  - the **uncapped average frame time is at most 6% above the previous stage's result**, and after the last stage **at most 10% above the baseline**;
  - the budgets of §2.1 hold, checked with `renderer.info` (draw calls, triangles, textures) printed by the benchmark.
- **If a stage fails**, cut in this order until it passes, and record what was cut in `decisions.md`:
  1. fewer far props (spires, islands);
  2. fewer light shafts;
  3. arcades reduced to plain balustrades;
  4. the sky drawn first instead of last (if that was faster, keep it);
  5. smaller textures;
  6. fewer texture looks.

  Never cut the sky or the open edges.
- Benchmark runs need an active, uncovered desktop (see the M8.5 note in `bench.md`). A throttled run (under 5 FPS) is repeated, not recorded.
- The final performance on the target laptop is a human checkpoint (§11).

## 3. Wall heights and open edges

Today every wall cell is 16 m tall, in the drawing and in the simulation. From this milestone each wall cell gets **its own height**. It's computed from the map when the map is loaded, the same way on the host and every client, so the protocol doesn't change.

### 3.1 The rule: zones never see each other

- **Zones.** Each arena's floor cells inside its `rect` form one zone. The remaining floor cells (the Lobby and the corridors) are split into connected zones (4-neighbors). Door cells belong to the arena whose `doors` or `exitDoors` list them.
- **Headroom.** The highest point anything can reach above the floor, plus 0.5 m, computed in code from the constants so a future flyer raises it automatically:
  - a player's eye at the top of a jump: `PLAYER_EYE + JUMP_VZ² ÷ (2 × GRAVITY)` ≈ 2.8 m;
  - a Cherub's top: `CHERUB_HOVER` + its height = 4.8 m;
  - a launched enemy's top (M9 §3.2): 1 m + 1.6 m = 2.6 m.

  So `HEADROOM` = 4.8 + 0.5 = **5.3 m**.
- **Zone top** `T(Z)`: the highest floor in the zone + `HEADROOM`. In a zone with the Gatekeeper's `B` cell, it's at least the `B` cell's floor + the Gatekeeper's height (6 m) + 0.5 m.
- **The guarantee.** With all doors closed, no straight line from a point in one zone's space (floor up to its zone top) reaches a point in another zone's space without passing below the top of a cell in between. Doors are openings, as today: through an open door you see the corridor or arena beyond, as now.

  Why lower walls are safe: if every wall around zone Z is at least `T(Z)` tall, nothing inside Z rises above its walls. A line that leaves a zone over a wall must climb to get over it, and then it passes above everything in the next zone. So the only places a view can leak are open edges (§3.3).

### 3.2 Wall heights

Each wall cell is one of these kinds:

| Kind | Which cells | Height |
|---|---|---|
| **Wall** | A wall cell with a floor cell among its 8 neighbors, except open edges | `max(T(Z))` over the zones of those neighboring floor cells, and at least **6 m** above the highest of those floors (so a wall still reads as architecture). |
| **Pillar** | Every cell of a group of wall cells (4-connected) that doesn't touch the grid's border | As *Wall*. **Pillars are cover and must work exactly as today:** the boss arena's 6 pillars hide players from Judgment, and Arena 3's hide them from the Choristers. In the boss arena the zone top is 14 m, above the Gatekeeper's eye (7.5 m dais + 5 m = 12.5 m), so every line from that eye to a player stays below the pillar tops and is blocked exactly when it's blocked today. §8.1 tests it. |
| **Open edge** | See §3.3 | The highest neighboring floor + **1.35 m** (`PARAPET`). Above a jump's 1.22 m peak, so it can't be climbed and doesn't feel like an invisible wall. |
| **Void** | Wall cells with no floor cell among their 8 neighbors, and everything outside the grid | Nothing: not drawn, and lines pass through. |
| **Door** (closed) | Door cells | As *Wall*, computed from the zones on both sides. |

In the Pearly Gates this gives about 6 m (the Lobby), 7.3 m (Arena 1), 8.8 m (Arena 2), 12.3 m (Arena 3) and 14 m (the boss arena) above the map's zero, instead of 16 m. Measured from each arena's own floor, that's about 6–9.5 m. The later arenas stay taller because their terraces and the Gatekeeper reach higher. Walls between arenas are as tall as the taller side needs.

`WALL_TOP` (16 m) stays as the cap and as the height above which projectiles are removed. A map whose computed heights exceed it fails level validation.

### 3.3 Open edges

An open edge is a wall cell where the level meets the sky. It's drawn as a low parapet carrying a balustrade (§7), with a cliff below it.

- **Candidates:** wall cells next to floor cells of **exactly one** zone, in a wall group that touches the grid's border (so not a pillar). Each candidate has an **outward direction**: away from the floor cells next to it (the sum of the directions from those floor cells to it, normalized).
- **Facing pairs close.** Two candidates of different zones *face each other* when each lies in front of the other (in the half-plane of its outward direction) within 150 m. Every candidate that faces a candidate of another zone becomes a *Wall* instead. This closes the pockets of empty space between arenas, where one arena could see another across the gap.
- The rest are open edges. In the Pearly Gates they're mainly the outer sides of the map: the north side of Arenas 1 and 2, the east side of Arenas 2 and 3, the south side of Arenas 3 and 4, and the west side of the Lobby and the boss arena. Every arena gets at least one open side.
- **The unit test of §8.1 is the judge.** If it finds a line between zones, the agent turns the open edges along it into walls until it passes, and records the change.

### 3.4 What changes in the simulation

- `map.top` for a wall cell is its height from §3.2 instead of 16 m. For void cells and outside the grid it's −∞, so lines pass through. Closed doors use their *Door* height.
- Line of sight and hitscan (`los.ts`) are otherwise unchanged. **Movement doesn't change:** it uses `solid`, which is unchanged, so walls of any height (open edges included) still block players, enemies and Cherubs completely.
- **What this changes in play:**
  - Shots and lines of sight over an open edge now pass, as they look. They go out into the sky, or across a bay of the same zone.
  - No line between a player's eye and anything in play passes over a *Wall*, *Pillar* or *Door* (§3.1), so every result inside a zone is the same as today. §8.1 tests this. That includes cover: hiding behind a pillar from Judgment, a Chorister or a Cherub works exactly as now.
  - A projectile flying over an open edge carries on until its range ends (the censer breaks in the air at 25 m) or it leaves the grid.

### 3.5 How it's drawn

The terrain draws the heightfield of §3.2:
- **Vertical faces** wherever a cell is higher than its neighbor, including wall faces that look onto void (seen from open edges).
- **Tops** of every wall and open-edge cell. Wall tops are rarely seen; parapet tops always are.
- **Cliffs:** below every open edge, and below every wall face that looks onto void, the face continues down to **30 m below the map's lowest floor**. Its vertex colors fade to the fog color at the bottom, so the level floats on the cloud sea.
- **Doors:** the closed-door columns are drawn to their *Door* height.

## 4. The sky

### 4.1 What it looks like

A bright painted sky like the concept art: big sunlit clouds, floating spires, warm haze at the horizon, rich blue overhead, and a sea of clouds below. It replaces the gradient in `makeSky()` ([scene.ts](../src/render/scene.ts)). Through the open edges, the cloud sea below the horizon is what lies under the level.

### 4.2 How it's mapped

`sky-day` (3 168 × 1 344) is a **horizon band** that wraps once around the player (360°), with plain color above it:

- **Horizon row:** the row where the cloud sea meets the haze (about 65% down; found by eye and recorded as a constant).
- **Vertical scale:** the horizon row is at 0° elevation and the top row at about **+70°**. Rows below the horizon use the same degrees per row, so the bottom row is about −38°. The clouds are slightly squashed vertically, about 1.4× compared with the horizontal scale, which painted clouds tolerate. The agent may adjust the angles if a screenshot looks better, and records the choice.
- **Above the band**, blend from the band's top-row color (averaged across the row) to a **zenith color**, a deeper blue sampled from the top of the band, by about 85°. **Below the band**, blend to the fog color by about −50°.
- **Seam:** Claude makes the left and right edges meet when preparing the image, by cross-fading about 5% of the width.
- **One texture, one lookup per pixel:** the shader samples the band with the view direction (`atan2` for the horizontal position, the elevation for the vertical).
- **Fog color** (`FOG_COLOR`) is sampled from the band's horizon row, averaged across it, so distant terrain fades into the horizon without a line. The fog distances (40 to 150 m) stay as they are unless a screenshot shows distance reading badly.
- **Size:** shipped at the source's 3 168 px width (no upscaling), as JPEG or WebP, about 23 MB on the GPU with mipmaps.
- **Readability:** if Blessed don't read against the horizon haze seen through an open edge, the prepared image's horizon is made slightly darker or more saturated. The enemies are never changed.

### 4.3 Image formats

Source images may be PNG or JPEG (`.jfif` and `.jpg` included). Wherever this document or art-prompts.md names a source as `.png`, a JPEG with the same base name is accepted.

Images without transparency (the sky, the tiling terrain textures) are converted to **JPEG or WebP** by a build-time script in `scripts/` (as `cutout.py` is today), at a quality of about 88. Images with transparency or additive effects stay PNG.

## 5. Surfaces and baked light

### 5.1 Textures

New tiling textures (prompts in art-prompts.md), each 4 × 4 m and repeating like the existing four, except the cornice:

| File | Used for |
|---|---|
| `tex-floor-plain` | Variation for floors, so large floors don't visibly repeat a 2 × 2 tile pattern. |
| `tex-floor-medallion` | One large gold medallion, at the center of arenas and in front of doors. |
| `tex-wall-window` | Wall with a tall arched window painted in recess, sky-blue glass. |
| `tex-wall-pilaster` | Wall with a fluted pilaster strip. |
| `tex-cornice` | A horizontal gold-and-ivory cornice band. Tiles left to right only. |

**Preparing them** (Claude, by script, from the human's images):
- `tex-floor-plain`: its gold lines are 4 m apart, but `tex-floor`'s are 2 m apart, and its marble veins break at the edges. Shift it by half its size (so the cross moves to the edges and hides the broken veins), then paint the same thin gold cross through the new center. The result is a 2 × 2 grid like `tex-floor`'s. Match its brightness to `tex-floor`.
- `tex-wall-window`: `tex-wall` and `tex-wall-pilaster` have a carved frieze band along the bottom, which lines up across neighboring wall tiles; the window wall has none. Copy the band from `tex-wall` into its bottom rows (below the window frame).
- `tex-cornice`: crop to the band and cross-fade the left and right edges.

The existing `tex-floor`, `tex-riser`, `tex-wall` and `tex-door` stay. **The agent may ask the human to regenerate them at 2 048 px** if they look soft next to the new ones; that's a decision, not a requirement.

**The texture array.** All terrain looks (the existing four, the five new ones) are layers of one `DataArrayTexture` at 1 024 × 1 024, mipmapped. Each vertex carries its layer. UVs stay world-aligned and repeat as today, so there are no atlas seams. The terrain becomes **one draw call** instead of three, which pays for the new meshes. Fog and vertex colors work as today; a small custom shader replaces `MeshBasicMaterial` where needed.

**Choosing a look:** deterministic, from the cell's coordinates and the level data, never random per session, so every client builds the same world.
- **Floors:** `tex-floor` mostly. `tex-floor-plain` in a coarse pattern (blocks of about 4 × 4 cells). `tex-floor-medallion` on the 4 × 4 m block at each **arena's center**, and on the block **in front of each door** (one cell into the arena), derived from the existing level data, so no new level fields are needed. Medallions go nowhere else.
- **Walls, vertically,** measured from the floor the face looks onto:
  - **0–4 m:** the decorated band. Along the wall, 4 m segments (aligned to world coordinates, as the UVs are) alternate window, pilaster, window. A segment that isn't fully wall (cut by a corner or a door) uses plain `tex-wall`.
  - **From 4 m up to the cornice:** `tex-wall`.
  - **The top 1.5 m:** `tex-cornice`, one strip around every wall, merged into the terrain mesh.
- **Open edges:** the parapet uses `tex-riser`. The cliff below uses `tex-riser` too, with its baked fade (§3.5).

### 5.2 Baked lighting

The terrain already shades by face direction (vertex colors, [terrain.ts](../src/render/terrain.ts)). Replace that with a baked result of the same cost at runtime:

- **Sun:** one fixed direction for all levels, **about 55° above the horizon**, from the side (not along a map axis). Faces toward the sun are lighter and warm-tinted; faces away are cooler. The elevation keeps wall shadows short: an 8 m wall casts about 5.6 m of shadow.
- **Shadow:** a vertex is in shadow when the heightfield of §3.2 rises between it and the sun (a march along the sun direction, at level build time).
- **Ambient occlusion:** each vertex darkens by how many neighboring cells are higher than it (corners, floor-to-wall joins, the bottom of stairs).
- **Result:** vertex colors, tinted warm in the light and cool-blue in shadow. Nothing else changes at runtime.
- **Rule:** the darkest shadow is still **mid-brightness**: its luminance is at least 60% of the lit floor's, so enemies stay readable in it.
- Props get the same sun in their vertex colors from their face directions, without the shadow march.

## 6. Atmosphere

All of it is decoration, small in count and on screen.

| Effect | What | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Light shafts** | 12 to 24 tall, thin, additive quads using `fx-lightshaft.png`, 1 to 3 m wide and 8 to 20 m tall, slanting along the sun direction. They stand along the sunlit side of arenas, through arcades and in front of walls, placed deterministically. Drawn as **one instanced mesh**, depth-tested, without depth writes. Peak brightness is low (enemies passing through one stay readable). Their brightness breathes ±10% over about 8 s, in the vertex shader. | 1 draw call |
| **Clouds below** | The existing `decor-cloud-tuft` sprite: 20 to 40 of them in the void beyond the open edges, 10 to 30 m below the level and 20 to 100 m out, drifting slowly in the vertex shader. They give the cloud sea depth as you move. | 1 draw call |
| **Haze** | Done by the fog color of §4.2. Nothing extra. | 0 |

- **Overdraw:** a shaft fades out in the shader as the camera gets closer than **8 m**, so a shaft never covers much of the screen.
- **Particles:** no new ones. The existing embers and feathers are unchanged.

## 7. Architecture

### 7.1 The props

Modeled in Blender (the `scripts/blender/` pipeline) from the prop sheets, exported as small low-poly meshes, and placed by the rules below:

| Prop | Triangles (each) | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Balustrade** | at most 200 per 2 m section | On every open edge, along its inner side (the cell edge next to the floor), so the collision edge and the railing match. Height 1.1 m, standing on the parapet. |
| **Column** | at most 250 | In **arcades**: on the open edges of the Lobby and the corridors, one column every 4 m, standing on the parapet cell. A column's cell becomes a *Wall* cell at the column's height, so line of sight matches it. |
| **Arch** | at most 400 | Between arcade columns, and over every doorway. **Its underside is at least `HEADROOM` above the floor beneath it**, so nothing in play (a jump, a Cherub, a shot between two things in the zone) can touch it, and it's never in the simulation. Its top is at most the wall height beside it. |
| **Spire cluster** | at most 1 500 | In the void, 60 to 140 m from the nearest open edge, 3 to 6 of them, within about ±15° of the horizon as seen from the level, so the haze matches the sky behind them. |
| **Floating island** | at most 1 500 | In the void, 60 to 140 m out, 2 to 4 of them, within the same angles. |

- Arenas get balustrades only (open terraces, like `concept-2`). The Lobby and the corridors get arcades (like `concept-1`).
- **UVs and textures:** world-aligned, using the terrain's texture array, with gold accents as vertex colors. No new textures.
- **Merged:** all static props of a level are baked into **at most 3 meshes**: near props (balustrades, columns, arches), and the far props in one or two meshes so the engine culls them by frustum. No per-prop objects remain at runtime.
- **Distance:** everything is within 170 m of the level, inside the sky dome (180 m) and the camera's far plane (200 m).
- **Placement:** deterministic, from the map and the wall heights of §3. A function takes the map and returns the prop list.
- **Never on play space:** no prop's footprint overlaps a floor cell, and nothing but the arches hangs over one (§8.1).
- **Budget:** the triangle budget of §2.1 is the cap. The agent places props near ones first and far ones last, until it's reached.

### 7.2 Asset format

Props are exported from Blender as **glTF (`.glb`)**, loaded once at the start, merged and discarded. If `GLTFLoader` costs more than it's worth in bundle size, a tiny custom binary format is allowed. Record the choice in `decisions.md`.

5. **Architecture.** The props, their placement and merging (§7): the parapets become a low curb carrying the balustrade, plus arcades, doorway arches, spires and islands. The scenery tests of §8.1. *Art:* the five prop sheets.

## 10. Out of scope

Real-time lights, shadows or post-processing of any kind, normal or PBR materials, a graphics-quality setting in the menu (the performance gate replaces it; add one only if the human asks), a different sky per arena, a day-night cycle or weather, new levels or arenas, changes to floors, collision or movement, falling off the level, water, reflections, destructible scenery, animated scenery beyond the shaft and cloud motion, new enemy or class art.
