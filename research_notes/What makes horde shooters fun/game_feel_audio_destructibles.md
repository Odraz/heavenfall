# Game feel, combat audio and destructible environments for Meatgrinder

Scope: what makes shooting and killing feel good (juice), how weapon/combat audio is built and mixed (especially with hundreds of simultaneous deaths), where to get usable SFX, browser audio constraints, and breakables/hazards/pickups, with a view to a solo developer on a WebGL/WebAudio billboard FPS.

Source quality: many primary talks (GDC Vault, YouTube) could not be fetched directly. Several findings come from secondhand summaries of those talks, which are labelled as such. License terms come from the official pages where it was possible to fetch them (Sonniss, Freesound). For Kenney and ZapSplat they come from third-party summaries, so check them before shipping.

## 1. Gun feel and juice: established techniques, and what matters for horde killing

### Takeaway
Juice means layering several small, fast feedback channels on every action: sound first, then camera kick, screenshake, weapon-sprite recoil, shell ejection, muzzle flash, hit reaction and particles/gore. The best-documented recipe (Vlambeer's Nuclear Throne pistol) is cheap to build and applies to Meatgrinder's painted FP sprites almost unchanged. In a horde game the per-kill feedback has to be cheap and has to aggregate. Gore detail and hitstop are strong for single kills, but they have to be throttled or replaced by "mass" feedback once dozens die per second.

### Cited findings
- Jan Willem Nijman (Vlambeer) gave the step-by-step breakdown of one Nuclear Throne pistol shot (from a Rock Paper Shotgun interview, summarized by Infovore). The order: (1) the sound effect plays first; (2) a shell ejects at low speed (2–4 px/frame at 30fps, 320×240) at aim direction +100–150°; (3) the bullet flies at 16 px/frame with a random 0–4° spread; (4) the camera kicks back 6 px against the aim and "adds 4 to the screenshake", which decays quickly; (5) "weapon kick" is set to 2, so the gun sprite jolts back and slides quickly into place; (6) on shotgun reloads, reverse weapon kick makes the reload look manual; (7) the bullet is circular on frame 1 and bullet-shaped after, a cheap fake muzzle flash. — [Infovore, "Making Game Feel"](https://infovore.org/?p=5275)
- "The art of screenshake" was given about a year after "Juice it or lose it" and asks why one game plays great while a similar one feels terrible. The video is linked from GMTK. — [Infovore](https://infovore.org/?p=5275); Kenney's list of must-see indie dev videos also includes it — [Kenney](https://kenney.nl/learn/must-see-videos-for-indie-developers)
- "Juice it or lose it" (Martin Jonasson & Petri Purho, Nordic Game Conference 2012) demonstrates the technique on Breakout. Secondhand notes list tweening/easing, particles (smoke, shattering, trails), squash and stretch for impact, screenshake, screen flashes, syncing the environment to the music rhythm, and sound effects and music as separate juice items. One developer's lesson from building a juicy demo was to add SFX and music much earlier. — [RPG Playground research notes](https://rpgplayground.com/?p=3339)
- Counterpoint: Folmer Kelly (GDC Europe Indie Summit) argued that juice can cost immersion. — [RPG Playground](https://rpgplayground.com/?p=3339)
- Steve Swink's "game feel" lives in "what the game does in the tenth of a second after you act". — quoted in [PSU, reward design article](https://www.psu.com/news/why-winning-feels-so-good-reward-design-from-arcades-to-balatro/)
- Hitstop (hitlag/hit pause) freezes the action briefly when a hit lands. Street Fighter/Guilty Gear use it prominently, Tekken and Virtua Fighter barely use it (Sakurai's Famitsu column, fan translation). In SF2 a hitstop of about 10 frames widened the cancel window. Sakurai caps the maximum hitstop and avoids long freezes in Smash because frozen players in free-for-all matches are open to a third player. That applies directly to 4-player co-op. — [Source Gaming translation of Sakurai](https://sourcegaming.info/2015/11/11/thoughts-on-hitstop-sakurais-famitsu-column-vol-490-1/); [critpoints.net](https://critpoints.net/2017/05/17/hitstophitfreezehitlaghitpausehitshit/); [SmashWiki Hitlag](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Hitlag)
- Smash characters vibrate during hitstop, horizontally on the ground and vertically in the air. — [SmashWiki Hitstun/Hitlag](https://ssbwiki.com/Hitstun)
- Implementation caution: setting the global time scale to 0 also freezes UI, audio and effects and "feels wrong". The recommendation is to freeze only the attacker/target animations. — [Godot forum, hitstop](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/how-should-i-implement-hitstop/45146)
- Killing Floor 2's M.E.A.T. (Massive Evisceration and Trauma) system was inspired by Soldier of Fortune's GHOUL. Its features: chunks blown off heads to reveal the skull, then exploding heads with brain cross-sections; zeds cut in half vertically or horizontally; more than 20 detachable parts (up from 5 in KF1); 95 death animations per zed; persistent blood on maps; slow-motion "ZED time". — [Wikipedia, Killing Floor 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_Floor_2); [VGTimes (RU)](https://vgtimes.ru/pc-news/20521-sistema-meat-v-killing-floor-2-pozvolit-potroshit-vragov-na-kusochki.html); [Co-Optimus](https://co-optimus.com/article/12782/it-s-raining-meat-in-these-killing-floor-2-screens.html)
- Darktide melee: "each swing of a sword or axe" brings heavy blood spray, and any body part hit can be dismembered. — [Checkpoint Gaming review](https://checkpointgaming.net/reviews/2022/12/warhammer-40000-darktide-review-in-progress-hacking-slashing-shooting-fun); ESRB lists dismemberment/decapitation leaving body parts and large blood stains — [ESRB](https://www.esrb.org/ratings/38884/warhammer-40000-darktide/)
- Performance precedent for horde gore: Vermintide 2 on console reportedly dismembers only on critical killing strikes, while PC dismembers on every killing strike. This was a 2019 change made for performance. (Community post on a dev tracker, not confirmed by Fatshark.) — [DevTrackers](https://devtrackers.gg/darktide/p/7e705bdf-dismemberment-on-console)

### Inferences
- The Nuclear Throne recipe maps directly onto Meatgrinder's FP sprites. Each weapon needs: a sound on frame 0, camera kick opposite the aim (FP equivalent: a small pitch-up and FOV punch), additive trauma-style screenshake that decays fast, sprite kick with a fast ease back, a fake muzzle flash (one bright frame plus a light), and an ejected casing for the revolver and shotgun. Reload reverse-kick fits the revolver and shotgun reloads.
- Hitstop in a 1000-enemy co-op game should be local and short. Freeze the struck enemy sprite and give a 1–3 frame view micro-freeze only on the local player's heavy hits (chains finisher, shotgun point-blank), never global time. Cap it, as Sakurai does, and ignore it during mass kills. Otherwise it stacks into stutter.
- The KF2/GHOUL/Darktide detail level (per-limb wounds) is wrong for 1000+ billboards. The horde-scale equivalent is what Meatgrinder already has (burst into two halves, feathers, light). The Vermintide-console precedent supports the rule "full gore for crits, big hits and nearby kills; a cheap pop for the rest". A "ZED time"-style slow-motion moment on big multi-kills is a known horde-game punctuation device.
- In hordes, feedback should aggregate. A multi-kill gets one bigger event (a bigger sound, a stronger shake, a kill-streak counter) rather than N copies of the single-kill event.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch the original "Art of screenshake" or "Juice it or lose it" videos and transcripts. The full Nijman technique list (he mentions about 30 tricks, including permanence of corpses/shells, enemy knockback, slower enemies, and bigger bullets) is not verified here from a primary source.
- I found no primary source on Doom 2016/Eternal's visual weapon-feel or glory-kill design.
- I found no developer source on hit markers or kill confirms for horde FPS.

## 2. Weapon and combat audio: construction, why synthesized SFX annoy, and mixing hundreds of deaths

### Takeaway
AAA gun sounds are built from separate layers (transient/onset, body, mechanics, sub/thump, a short tail/space layer), and the mix is built around the player's weapon. In DOOM 2016 weapons were "the main character" and were never ducked. Horde mixes survive through priority and culling: Frostbite's HDR audio uses loudness as priority and pre-culls inaudible sounds. Variation (multiple takes, pitch/volume jitter, no immediate repeats) prevents fatigue, although pitch/volume jitter alone still wears out. Single-layer oscillator/noise synthesis with no variation and no voice cap matches the documented causes of "annoying" audio.

### Cited findings: gun construction
- Layer model used by practitioners and tools: **transient/onset** (the "attention grabber", a high click for definition), **body** (the anchor; Chuck Russom would keep this layer if he could only keep one), **mechanical** (action cycling, conveys material and condition), **sub/thump** (weight), **tail** (reverberant decay, baked in or added by the engine; keep it short, because long tails muddy fast action). Krotos Weaponiser is organized as Onset/Body/Thump/Tail. Diagnosis: a strong body with no transient sounds muffled, a sharp transient with no body sounds thin and toy-like. — [Designing Sound, Chuck Russom](https://designingsound.org/2010/04/chuck-russom-special-gun-sound-design/); [Splice](https://splice.com/blog/?p=16617); [Violet Recording](https://violetrecording.com/how-to-design-weapon-sounds/); [Lior Ortega](https://www.liortega.com/post/first-person-shooter-gun-sound-design); [80.lv Weaponiser](https://80.lv/articles/weaponiser-a-weapon-sound-design-solution)
- Processing per layer: EQ, saturation, transient shaping ("sharpen the attack without making it harsh"), and deliberate clipping as a tool. — [Splice](https://splice.com/blog/?p=16617)
- NPC weapons are split into close and distant detail so the player's own weapon keeps frequency space. — [Splice](https://splice.com/blog/?p=16617) / [Lior Ortega](https://www.liortega.com/post/first-person-shooter-gun-sound-design)
- Craft variations so repeated shots don't fatigue the player. — [Violet Recording](https://violetrecording.com/how-to-design-weapon-sounds/)

### Cited findings: DOOM (2016), id Software audio team Q&A (Chad Mossholder, Ben Carney)
- Audio director Chris Hite "decided early on that the weapons were really the main character in DOOM". "Weapon sounds never get ducked by anything, not even VO", and the rest of the mix was built around the weapons. — [ADSR Sounds Q&A](https://www.adsrsounds.com/news/the-sound-design-of-doom-exclusive-qa/)
- "We baked special reverbs into the weapon fire that play as a layer of the weapon based on what space you are in" (the tail as a separate, space-dependent layer). — [ADSR](https://www.adsrsounds.com/news/the-sound-design-of-doom-exclusive-qa/)
- Gauss cannon trick: "an initial impulse that quickly chops off to silence" before the main shot. The silence gap makes the shot feel huge. — [ADSR](https://www.adsrsounds.com/news/the-sound-design-of-doom-exclusive-qa/)
- BFG: sweeping oscillators layered with electromagnetic sounds and several explosions. Armor pickup: large metal impacts, slightly distorted and layered. Ben Carney's first asset was the super shotgun shell ejection. — [ADSR](https://www.adsrsounds.com/news/the-sound-design-of-doom-exclusive-qa/)
- Gore: a big "Gore" recording session with "all kinds of meats, vegetables and strange gooey things". Demon voices mix human performances with synthetic elements, pitched down and processed. The Spider Mastermind's sounds were distorted so they cut through. — [ADSR](https://www.adsrsounds.com/news/the-sound-design-of-doom-exclusive-qa/)
- An outside review describes the DOOM pistol as layers of "low-end mechanical punch and synthetic highs". The same review says the weapons lack a reverb tail (a reviewer's opinion). — [Richmond MUS226 blog](https://blog.richmond.edu/mus226-sp22-02/?p=34)
- Chad Mossholder (Doom Eternal lead audio designer) describes SFX, music and gameplay as one cohesive unit. He mixed 50 of Eternal's 59 soundtrack tracks per id's statement. — [DoomWiki, Chad Mossholder](https://www.doomwiki.org/wiki/Chad_Mossholder)

### Cited findings: mixing many sounds (HDR, priority, culling)
- Frostbite HDR audio (DICE, Battlefield: Bad Company era) measures loudness at the listener and scales all sources to fit a window within roughly a 130 dB real-world range, much like HDR lighting. Loudness is "a good approximation for importance, especially in FPS games". The system works as a culling algorithm: "play the right sounds rather than all sounds". It operates on logical loudness values, not the waveform. — [DICE slides, "Adaptive mixing in Frostbite"](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/adaptive-mixing-in-frostbite/3128152)
- Sounds are pre-culled before creation when the HDR mixer judges them inaudible, so the game can play fewer, more expensive sounds. A 64-player game may want to play hundreds at once. Practitioners report that tuning dB SPL values ends up far from real-life values, and that attack/release settings can cause pumping. — [Designing Sound, HDR audio in Wwise](https://designingsound.org/2013/06/finding-your-way-with-high-dynamic-range-audio-in-wwise/)
- Battlefield 4 took player foley out of the HDR system so explosions would not cull your own footsteps. In other words, the local player's sounds are exempt from culling. — [MCV, Ben Minto on BF4](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/heard-about-battlefield-4/)
- Vermintide 2: normal hordes are announced by a sound cue (a horn). Ambush hordes have no cue except Skaven whispers. The team moved away from 2D "stinger" cues toward announcing bosses through the sound of their movement. A Fatshark audio team member admitted the cues were inconsistent. — [DevTrackers, Vermintide 2](https://devtrackers.gg/vermintide2/p/70cbb6f7-sound-cannot-hear-behind-you)
- Vermintide 2 uses zones of lower audibility in levels "so you get natural dynamics", plus attenuation curves tuned through playtesting. — [Vermintide dev blog, Sound Design](https://www.vermintide.com/news/developer-blog-sound-design)

### Cited findings: why repetitive/synthesized audio becomes annoying, and the fixes
- Most repetition problems come from a single fixed asset firing with no variation. The fix is several versions, randomized pitch and sample choice, and layering. — [IRPR Sound guide](https://sounddesign.irpr.agency/guides/why-is-my-game-audio-repetitive/)
- An audio-synthesis patent notes that randomizing pitch and volume alone "is not convincingly natural" and that a small set of takes is "quickly exhausted, causing ear fatigue". It proposes recombining segments of recordings. — [USPTO patent 10453434](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10453434)
- Footsteps pattern: pick a random variant per event and nudge pitch and volume by a small random amount. — [Bugnet footstep variation](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-implement-footstep-variation-to-avoid-repetition)
- An indie developer randomized pitch across 3–6 values for about 90% of their game's sounds with one line of code. — [itch.io post](https://itch.io/post/10660663)
- Vampire-Survivors-likes: one developer admitted their gem pickup sound was "too loud and annoying". Another clone's SFX "step on each other", with only a few able to play at once, and that developer said sound design was one of the last features implemented. — [itch.io](https://itch.io/post/1903845); [itch.io](https://itch.io/post/6856787)

### Cited findings: dynamic music
- The Left 4 Dead AI Director controls horde timing, special placement, item drops and "what music to play", using music among its emotional cues. — [Wikipedia, Left 4 Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_4_Dead); [Inverse](https://inverse.com/gaming/left-4-dead-valve-15-year-anniversary); [Game Developer, "The Discomfort Zone"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-discomfort-zone-the-hidden-potential-of-valve-s-ai-director)
- Vermintide 2's score (Jesper Kyd) uses crescendoing war drums to warn of a coming Chaos horde, and "the tide of battle" can be read from the score's ebb and flow. — [Dread Central interview](https://www.dreadcentral.com/editorials/272672/interview-jesper-kyd-on-vermintide-ii-soundtrack/)
- Mike Morasky's Portal 2 score is generated in real time by an adaptive system that reacts to player actions. — [Plogue interview](https://plogue.com/mike-morasky.html)

### Inferences
- **Likely root causes of the playtesters' complaints**, based on the findings above and standard audio practice (not verified against the Meatgrinder code): (a) each event is one deterministic synth patch, so the same waveform repeats, which is the "single fixed asset" failure; (b) raw oscillators and white noise have lots of energy at 2–5 kHz, where hearing is most sensitive, and nothing natural in their spectrum, so they read as harsh and "toy-like" (transient without body); (c) a horde has no voice cap, so hundreds of identical events sum into a buzzing wall and clipping; (d) there is no player-first priority, so the player's weapon competes with enemy deaths. Music is recorded and sounds fine, which makes the synthesized SFX stand out by contrast.
- **Recommended Meatgrinder audio architecture (WebAudio)**:
  1. Switch the weapon, impact and death sounds to recorded or layered samples (sources in section 3). Keep synthesis only for UI blips or as a sweetener layer.
  2. Build each weapon from 2–4 short layers played together (transient, body, mech, tail), with 3–6 variants per layer, random pitch of ±3–8% and volume of ±1–3 dB, and no immediate repeat (shuffle bag). Combining layer variants multiplies the effective variety.
  3. Use per-category voice caps with stealing: for example the player's weapon is never culled (the DOOM/BF4 rule), at most 6–12 concurrent enemy-death voices, at most 2–4 of the same sample within about 50 ms. If the cap is hit, drop the quietest or farthest.
  4. **Mass-kill aggregation**: count deaths per short window (for example 100 ms). Play at most one or two individual death sounds, plus one "crowd" layer scaled by the count (a pre-made "many bodies tearing" sound or a crunch/splatter swell, chosen and pitched by tier). The density of the sound then grows without growing the voice count, much like Vampire Survivors folding gems into one red gem.
  5. Build a cheap HDR/priority approach. Give each sound a priority and loudness. Compute an approximate perceived level (distance attenuation multiplied by loudness) before creating a node, and skip anything below threshold. That pre-culling also saves CPU.
  6. Put a gentle bus compressor/limiter (DynamicsCompressorNode) on the SFX bus, with music on its own bus and light sidechain ducking of music only on huge events. Following DOOM, do not duck the player's weapon.
  7. Low-pass distant enemies (BiquadFilterNode) instead of spatializing 1000 PannerNodes. A few spatialized voices plus bucketed "left/right/behind" ambience is cheaper.
- **Dynamic music**: since the music is already recorded, the cheapest Director-style approach is 2–3 intensity stems (calm/combat/climax) looped in sync and crossfaded by a combat-intensity metric (kills per second, enemies near players). A horde-warning stinger or drum swell would follow Vermintide's horn/drums pattern.

### Gaps
- I found no primary source on how Vermintide/Darktide, Left 4 Dead or Vampire Survivors technically limit concurrent death/impact voices. Vampire Survivors' 400-gem cap is documented (section 5), but its role in audio is unconfirmed.
- No DOOM 2016/Eternal primary source states numbers of variations per sound or voice limits.
- I found no primary source detailing Mick Gordon's dynamic combat-music system for DOOM 2016 (the Bethesda article page had no technical content).
- jsfxr's limits were not researched from a source. My inference is that it produces single-layer chiptune-style oscillator sounds with no recorded texture, which suits retro games but does not fix the "harsh/toy-like" problem.

## 3. Practical resources: SFX libraries, licenses, browser formats

### Takeaway
For a commercial-capable game, the safest sources are Sonniss GDC bundles (royalty-free, no attribution, no AI training, no resale as SFX), Kenney (CC0), and Freesound filtered to CC0, or to CC-BY with credits kept. Avoid CC-BY-NC and Sampling+. ZapSplat's free tier needs attribution, and paid tiers remove that. For the browser, Opus is the most efficient format, but Safari's decodeAudioData support has been inconsistent. Shipping AAC (.m4a) or MP3 as a fallback, and testing a real decode, is the safe approach.

### Cited findings
- **Sonniss GDC Game Audio Bundle license (v2.0, effective 27 Aug 2026)**: worldwide, non-exclusive, royalty-free; personal and commercial projects; "without attribution"; unlimited projects for life. You may not distribute or sub-license the effects "as sound effects", may not sell them as they come, may not modify them "with intent to claim authorship", and may not use them for AI training. Redesigned sounds are still licensed material. Downloads made before the effective date fall under the version current at download time. — [Sonniss license](https://sonniss.com/gdc-bundle-license)
- Sonniss bundles are large, multi-GB yearly drops (for example about 27.5 GB in 2024). — [GameFromScratch 2024](https://gamefromscratch.com/sonniss-27-5gb-sound-effect-giveaway-at-gdc-2024/)
- **Freesound** licenses are CC0 (no attribution, do as you like, don't claim authorship), CC-BY (credit required, commercial OK), CC-BY-NC (credit required, no money from the work) and Sampling+ (retired; about NC, plus no ads). Search has a license filter, including an "approved for Free Cultural Works" option (CC0 + CC-BY). — [Freesound FAQ](https://freesound.org/help/faq/)
- **Kenney** audio packs (Impact Sounds, Interface Sounds, RPG and others) are CC0 with optional credit. Only Kenney's logo is off-limits. (Third-party guide citing Kenney's support page; OpenGameArt mirrors list CC0.) — [gtstu guide](https://gtstu.com/?p=5011); [OpenGameArt, Kenney 50 RPG SFX (CC0)](https://opengameart.org/content/50-rpg-sound-effects)
- **ZapSplat**: the free tier requires attribution ("a simple line of text" crediting ZapSplat.com). Paid membership removes it (about $4–5/month per a third-party listing, may be outdated). Standalone redistribution of the SFX is not allowed on any plan. — [ZapSplat licensing for beginners](https://www.zapsplat.com/copyright-and-audio-licensing-for-beginners); [licenseorg summary](https://licenseorg.com/guide/music-audio/zapsplat)
- **Safari and Opus**: Safari 15 reported WebM Opus as playable via canPlayType but failed in decodeAudioData, which broke games that relied on detection. By Safari 17.4 WebM Opus was reportedly consistent across iOS and macOS, though a related bug stayed open. — [WebKit bug 226922](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=226922); [WebKit bug 245428](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=245428); Construct ships a WebAssembly Opus decoder fallback for this reason — [Construct blog, Opus audio](https://www.construct.net/en/blogs/construct-official-blog-1/opus-audio-construct-891)
- Ogg Opus/Vorbis in Safari: an MDN change claims support from Safari 18.4. Independent 2025 testing calls it incomplete and buggy (for MediaRecorder, not decode). — [frequal.com](https://frequal.com/java/OggOpusStillNotWorkingInSafari18_4.html); [WebKit bug 238546](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=238546)
- Format and decoding reference for the Web Audio API — [zpl.fi, File formats in Web Audio API](https://zpl.fi/file-formats-in-web-audio-api/); a report says Safari's Promise form of decodeAudioData sometimes rejects MP3 that the callback form decodes (secondary blog) — [Bugnet](https://bugnet.io/blog/fix-webaudio-decodeaudiodata-rejects-mp3-on-safari)
- Third-party 2026 guide: some popular SFX sites are non-commercial only, which "can quietly sink a game you plan to sell". — [Cinevva guide](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/free-sound-effects-music)

### Inferences
- Workflow for a solo developer with AI help: pick about 30–60 source sounds from Sonniss and Kenney (gunshots, shotgun mech, revolver cylinder, chain rattles, metal impacts, flesh/gore squelch, bone crunch, feathers/wings, censer swing and chain, ceramic/pottery smashes, coins/gems). Trim each to short one-shots, normalize, export mono 44.1/48 kHz, and encode to Opus/WebM with an m4a (AAC) fallback chosen by an actual test decode. Keep a `licenses.md` listing each file's source and license (needed for CC-BY credits, and a good record for Sonniss and ZapSplat).
- Size budget: short mono one-shots at about 64–96 kbps Opus are a few KB each, so 200 variants fit in roughly 1–3 MB. Decode once into AudioBuffers at load or after the first user gesture, then reuse them (AudioBufferSourceNode is cheap and single-use, AudioBuffer is shareable).
- Latency: pre-decoded AudioBuffers started with `start(ctx.currentTime)` are effectively immediate. Avoid `<audio>` elements and per-shot fetches.
- Sonniss forbids redistribution "as sound effects". Shipping the files inside the game build is how licensed games normally work, but don't publish a raw "assets/sfx" folder as a separate download or open-source the raw files under a permissive repo license. If the repo is public, keep licensed SFX out of it or clearly excluded.
- jsfxr/procedural synthesis can stay for UI ticks, but it is the wrong tool for "flesh, metal, wood, ceramic" material sounds, which are what horde-killing feel relies on (DOOM recorded meat and vegetables for gore).

### Gaps
- I could not fetch Kenney's or ZapSplat's official license pages directly. Verify at kenney.nl/support and zapsplat.com/license-type before shipping.
- OpenGameArt licenses vary per asset (CC0, CC-BY, CC-BY-SA, GPL). Not individually researched.
- Safari decodeAudioData support for Opus in 2026 is not confirmed by a current source.

## 4. Interactive and destructible environments: breakables, barrels, hazards, environmental kills

### Takeaway
Breakables work because they combine instant, juicy feedback (a shatter sound, debris, particles) with a small gamble (hidden loot) at near-zero risk. They disappoint when the payoff is empty or when breakable-looking objects don't break. Exploding barrels (Doom E1M1 onward) are the classic tactical hazard: chain reactions, luring enemies, and knockback by distance band. In a 2.5D billboard renderer, breakables and barrels are essentially one more sprite type with HP that spawns particles and pickups on death, so they are cheap.

### Cited findings
- Juice framing: a convincing break animation confirms the action and gives a sense of accomplishment, while a swing that does nothing feels unclear. — [ArkTimes, block breaking animation](https://2fwww.arktimes.com/?p=32661); "excessive positive feedback" as the definition of juice — [Theseus thesis, Choosing Juice](https://theseus.fi/handle/10024/92268)
- Terraria pots drop coins and/or random common items when broken with any weapon or tool. — [Terraria wiki via search](https://terraria.fandom.com/wiki/File:Coin_portal.png); a Minecraft suggestion asks for pots holding one item that stays unknown until broken — [Minecraft feedback](https://feedback.minecraft.net/hc/en-us/community/posts/360074694512/comments/360013551491)
- Pot colour variants can signal contents before breaking (readability versus surprise). — [QuestMaster wiki, Pot](https://questmaster.wiki.gg/wiki/Pot)
- "A Game About Breaking Pots" builds its whole loop on shattering pots for gold and upgrades, with special pots that bounce, teleport, fly or clone themselves. — [Deku Deals](https://www.dekudeals.com/items/a-game-about-breaking-pots)
- Failure modes: a jam reviewer called pots a time-wasting chore when the reward never came ("drop bonus time or ANYTHING"). A player was "disappointed" when pots that looked smashable didn't smash. — [itch.io](https://itch.io/post/7693460); [itch.io](https://itch.io/post/16579353)
- Doom's exploding barrel appears on E1M1 and is described in the manual as fuel or toxic-waste drums, part of the "dangerous Doom environment". It has 20 HP, a 128-unit blast radius, and clustered barrels chain-react. — [DoomWiki, Barrel](https://doomwiki.org/wiki/Barrel); [DoomWiki, Hazard](https://doomwiki.org/wiki/Hazard); [DoomWiki, Barrel frag](https://www.doomwiki.org/wiki/Barrel_frag)
- Doom 64 added activatable traps (dart launchers, homing fireballs). Heretic's pods and Hexen's mushrooms are variants of the barrel. — [DoomWiki, Exploding barrel](https://doomwiki.org/wiki/Exploding_barrel)
- Half-Life 2 barrels can be pushed, thrown, rolled or "cooked" as improvised grenades. — [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/most-explosive-red-barrels)
- Warframe barrel knockback uses distance bands: 0–2 m knockdown, 2–6 m heavy stagger, 6–10 m light stagger. — [Warframe wiki](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Explosive_Barrel)
- Darktide players complain that barrel knockback on players is overtuned and throws them unpredictably (user comments). — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1361210/discussions/0/3709306945125540029)
- Darkwood barrels explode when shot or touched by fire, and can be used to lure enemies or as clutter that slows and blocks them. — [Darkwood wiki](https://darkwood.fandom.com/wiki/Exploding_Barrel)

### Inferences
- **Cost in Meatgrinder's renderer**: a breakable vase/urn is a static billboard with HP that is already hittable by the existing hit tests (the same as an enemy that doesn't move or think). On death: swap to a "broken" sprite or remove it, spawn 6–15 shard particles (reuse the feather/gib particle system with a ceramic tint), play a ceramic-smash sample (3–5 variants plus pitch jitter), and roll a loot table. The CPU cost is negligible next to 1000 enemies. Networking only needs a prop ID and a "broken" event plus the loot seed.
- **Loot inside**: the itch.io failure case says pots need a payoff. Small, frequent rewards (a health orb, a little ability charge, coins/score) plus a rare jackpot (big heal, temporary power) is the standard. Colour-coded vase variants (gold urn equals better loot) give readability.
- **Hazards that fit the core fun** (hordes plus co-op AoE): explosive barrels/censers-of-oil let players delete clumps of horde, and chain reactions read as spectacular mass kills. Use Warframe-style distance bands so enemies are knocked back. Consider not knocking players back, or only weakly, given the Darktide complaints. Spike pits, ledges or fire that enemies can be knocked into reward the shotgun and chains knockback. Mass-kill audio aggregation (section 2) handles the resulting kill spikes.
- Scope discipline (per project memory): breakables with simple loot match the playtester request ("pick up stuff and break stuff") and are low-risk. Traps and hazards are a separate feature to confirm with the user.

### Gaps
- No sources were found for Hades' or Diablo's breakables, traps or environmental kills, or for Zelda pot design rationale, despite searching. Treat any claims about those games as unverified.
- No sources on destructible cover in horde FPS.

## 5. Pickup feel: magnetism, sound, colour coding, sparkle

### Takeaway
Vampire Survivors shows the pattern: gems are pulled in within a magnet radius, a vacuum item collects everything, each pickup makes a satisfying sound that fills a visible bar, and there is a hard cap (400 gems) beyond which the XP folds into one red gem. That cap bounds both rendering and audio density. Late-run pickup audio becomes a dense "symphony", which is satisfying only if the sound is soft and varied. Loud, repeated pickup sounds are a known annoyance.

### Cited findings
- Gems are pulled in once inside the player's Magnet radius. The Vacuum item pulls every gem on the map. An audio issue with the Vacuum was fixed in August 2023. — [Vampire Survivors wiki, Vacuum](https://vampire.survivors.wiki/w/Vacuum); [Fandom, Experience Gem](https://vampire-survivors.fandom.com/wiki/Experience_Gem)
- If more than 400 gems are on the ground, no new gems drop. Further XP accumulates into a single red gem. — [Fandom, Experience Gem](https://vampire-survivors.fandom.com/wiki/Experience_Gem)
- Reviewers describe a "satisfying sound" as the blue XP bar fills, and late-game collection as "a certifiable symphony of sounds". — [The Vibes review](https://www.thevibes.com/articles/lifestyles/53373/vampire-survivors-an-escalating-dopamine-rush-disguised-as-a-retro-game)
- A community suggestion is to merge nearby gems to cut the count drastically. A VS-like developer called their own pickup sound "too loud and annoying". — [Reddit r/DestroyMyGame](https://twt.omada.cafe/r/DestroyMyGame/comments/1nf60bd/destroy_our_wip_sounds_and_visual_fx); [itch.io](https://itch.io/post/1903845)
- DOOM 2016's armor pickup was built from large metal impacts, slightly distorted and layered, so even pickups got AAA layering. — [ADSR](https://www.adsrsounds.com/news/the-sound-design-of-doom-exclusive-qa/)

### Inferences
- For Meatgrinder: pickups (from vases or elite drops) should (1) pop out with a small arc and bounce, (2) glow or sparkle in a colour code (health red, ability charge class-coloured, score gold), (3) be magnet-pulled when a player is within a radius, with an accelerating ease-in, (4) play a soft, short, pitched tone on collection. Rising pitch for rapid consecutive pickups (a "combo pitch ladder") is a common juice trick, though I found no source for it here. Cap concurrent pickup voices and merge pickups past a threshold, VS-style.
- Co-op: decide whether pickups are per-player or shared. Shared health orbs create co-op moments, but that is a design choice for the user.

### Gaps
- I found no sourced material on Diablo's loot-drop sound and beam colour coding or on Diablo's breakables. My general knowledge (tier-coloured beams and distinct drop sounds for rare items) is unverified here.
- No source for "rising pitch on consecutive pickups" as a documented technique.
