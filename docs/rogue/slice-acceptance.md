## Content completeness

The catalogs define 20 combat templates, four specials, fallback layout, one elite, one hazard, one complete boss, 84 talent nodes including 12 alternative abilities, eight items, twelve relics and six replacement combinations. Four deeds and five Edicts have eligibility and settlement rules. Later heavens and mini-bosses are explicitly deferred rather than empty required content.

Current catalog rows and normative core clauses control implementation. Older paper encounter counts, prototype measurements and illustrative stock draws must not overwrite them. In particular, the Bell's elite mixture, grounded area reach, silent trumpet and revised boss cycle replace earlier narrative assumptions.

## Evidence required from a playable slice

These are acceptance observations of the playable slice. Automated tests cover the pure logic along the way ([implementation.md](implementation.md), *Verification*); the items below need the running game and, where stated, humans.

- **Complete runs:** finish with every fresh class solo and with mixed-level co-op; exercise death, revive, reload, reconnect, shops and victory settlement. No mandatory route should require a particular unlocked node, consumable or human teammate.
- **Build decisions:** observe at least two plausible approaches per class after unlock exhaustion, including the §6.1 veteran variants. Each route/ability/combo choice should change target, timing, position or risk. A nine-point allocation and up to four Rites must stay legal through substitution, respec and restoration of learned effects at run end. Check that fixed offers and passing feel like choices rather than forced downgrade menus.
- **Collected-account replay:** observe three different generated runs per class with collection and learned points already complete. Record whether the player voluntarily changes a route, active ability, Rite substitution or item plan and why, without counting forced randomness as preference. Ask whether they want another attempt and which decision they want to try differently. Compare two viable routes/builds for actual sacrifices; a higher raw number of reward cards is insufficient.
- **Meaningful choice rate:** initial target at least 15 informed decisions per human per ten simulation minutes. Count a deliberate item use, target/state setup, route choice, reward pick, bargain or Bell decision; repeated trigger presses, automatic feedback, forced actions and passive following do not satisfy the target. Ask each player to explain one benefit they gave up; do not inflate the count by crediting one leader's route choice to every follower.
- **Pacing:** initial full-run target about 18–25 simulation minutes; the 14-minute Sundial should reward a deliberate shorter route. Room timing bands in game.md and the class-specific boss model are tuning hypotheses. Solo overlay wall time is not run-clock time. Investigate a monotonous safe ring or an ability always cast on cooldown without thought.
- **Cooperation:** observe useful rescues, shared setup and newcomer decisions without making a healer mandatory. Have each participant explain what another class did that changed their own action. Validate the exact solo escape and bot priorities rather than assuming a party mechanic works alone.
- **Readability and feel:** identify the Dominion's warning, ranged threat, pickup and boss telegraph in a crest crowd. State colors, source geometry and sounds must reveal consequences. Current-kit H hints must explain a Rite replacement and the floor/item controls. Frequent combat sounds must meet core §1.3's recorded/layered variant and licensing requirements; placeholders do not pass. Record which sound becomes annoying and where the second half of a run becomes repetitive.
- **Horde reference:** controlled four-human base-kit crest reaches at least 1,000 simultaneous living enemies. Stronger builds may erase the crowd earlier. Record intended/delivered counts, peak alive and arrival duration; never force this target through invulnerability or surprise enemies. The authoritative global cap remains 1,500.
- **Performance:** existing [benchmark](../bench.md) targets are average FPS ≥58 and average simulation work ≤8 ms on the documented Intel Iris Xe, headed Chromium/ANGLE, 1920×1080 reference setup. Include the additional four dry-ground navigation fields, escape cache and active avoiding enemies in the hazard/crest workload. Generation/stamping/validation ≤300 ms, client map build ≤1.2 s and typical Loading ≤3 s are initial budgets. Measure the current full-content implementation; old layout measurements do not establish compliance.
- **Protocol and admission boundaries:** observe stale prior-run binary input/snapshots, duplicate targeted requests, held-key release/loss, inconsistent or missing snapshot parts, bounded reliable catch-up overflow/resync and mismatched dirty builds. A denied admission write creates no playable body/charge; a duplicate ready/resume never stakes again. Required full state fits its byte/queue budget without truncation.
- **Lifecycle correctness:** observe the reviewed boundary cases above on actual transport/storage. A retry must not duplicate a charge, XP, deed or claim; a stale snapshot must not restore spent inventory or remove newer progress. Debug runs never save progression.

Balance, dominance, long-session fatigue and repeated-run enjoyment remain observational questions. A finite single dungeon cannot prove infinite distinct experiences. If a player always chooses the same route or combination, revise that choice's opportunity cost using evidence; adding catalog quantity alone is not a sufficient fix.

## Implementation dependency order

The order is core §14.10 / [implementation.md](implementation.md) (revision 9): each contract arrives with its first consumer, and three fun gates pause expansion until the human is satisfied.

1. R0 preserved baseline, records and a prepared R1.
2. R1 generated floor, room lifecycle, doors, trickle, discovery and map, run identity (Gate A).
3. R2 props, halos/motes, the Dominion, Refiner's Fire, crest, the full template pool, recorded combat sound (Gate B).
4. R3 durable profile/admission, XP, recovery, absolute settlements, the Pardoner, inventory, eight consumables, clear rewards and previews.
5. R4 provenance and targeted input, the complete pilot class, legal resolver, Confessional and fixed Rites (Gate C); R5 the remaining class catalogs.
6. R6 key/chest, relics/combos/bargains, Chart from the amphora, Pilgrim's Return, Bells/Sundial, deeds/Edicts and competition.
7. R7 full Gatekeeper and ending; R8 required feedback/asset completion; R9 complete-run evidence, the benchmark on the full workload and final human acceptance.

This order does not cut content from the vertical slice. Enemy/hazard support precedes their room/Bell use, source and modifier resolution precedes claimable Rites and exhausted-collection fallbacks, and durable run/profile transactions precede persistent rewards. Each dependency must exist before its dependent mechanic can be called complete; historical prototype support alone does not count as a shipped feature.
