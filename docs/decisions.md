# Implementation decisions

Choices made where [mvp.md](mvp.md) is silent or ambiguous, one per line (§0).

- §2.1 — TypeScript 7 (the native `tsc`), Vite 8, Vitest 5, Three.js 0.186 and Playwright 1.63 are the latest stable versions at project start.
- §2.2 — The worker runs its first tick as soon as it starts; later ticks follow the 30 Hz accumulator.
- §2.5 — `seed` is read as a non-negative decimal integer taken modulo 2³²; any other value means a random seed.
- §2.5 — The F3 overlay also shows the local player's position and yaw; the screenshot helper (`scripts/screenshot.mjs`) steers by it.
- §2.5 — Dev keys `K` and `G` and the singleplayer pause reach the worker as internal messages, like the other host-only worker messages. The dev keys also work with `bot=1`, since the full solo run test presses `K` while the bot plays.
- §2.5 — Benchmark 1%-low FPS is 1000 ÷ the average frame time of the slowest 1% of frames (at least one frame) in the 30 s window.
- §2.5 — Bot: between line-of-sight checks it keeps aiming at the enemy it chose while that enemy is still in the interpolated set; it stops when within 0.2 m of its goal point; when it's in the goal's cell or next to it, it heads straight for the goal point. Its Shadowstep goes in its movement direction, or forward when it stands still.
- §3 — When the result overlay appears it replaces the death text.
- §3 — The player name is saved to `localStorage` (key `heavenfall.name`) on every edit, as typed; the field accepts at most 16 characters, and the trimmed name is used.
- §3 — The dungeon picker's only entry is preselected.
- §3 — Pause doesn't open in the benchmark. `Esc` while Pause is open does nothing; only `Resume` closes it. While Pause is open the local player, bot included, gets no input. In singleplayer the client's clock for interpolation, displayed cooldowns and the local Shadowstep and Falling Star timer stands still while paused, matching the paused simulation.
- §3 — `Back to title` and `Leave game` end the session (the worker is terminated) and reset the debug object's game fields; at Results they keep their final values. Ending a game also releases its WebGL context, so several games in one page don't pile up contexts.
- §5.2 — Feet are compared with floors using a 1e-6 m tolerance, so a jump peaking exactly at a ledge height still lands on it.
- §5.4 — Projectiles test the heightfield with their center point; their radius only enlarges the target cylinders. A projectile removed to make room for a new one (the 400 cap) doesn't explode.
- §5.6 — Knockback moves the enemy on the ticks after it's applied: 6 ticks at 20 m/s for the 0.2 s knockbacks, 12 ticks at 10 m/s for Falling Star's 0.4 s (M9 §3.2). A pulled enemy doesn't act during the 0.3 s pull and shows the rooted flag during it. Slow also slows Cherub strafing.
- §5.7 — Singleplayer regeneration starts 8 s after the last damaging hit or the start of the game, and only while alive.
- §6 — A cooldown starts on the tick the ability is used and counts down from the next tick. Chains of Tartarus and Discord start their cooldown even when they affect no enemy; only Falling Star is exempt (§6; Kiss of Betrayal, also exempt, was removed in M9).
- §6.1 — Falling Star's landing applies its damage first, then knocks back the survivors; an enemy exactly on the landing point is pushed along a fixed angle from its slot number.
- §6.2 — Before M9 §2.8 the Censer dealt 40 dmg to the enemy it hit plus splash to the nearest others (10 to 6 within 2 m, then 20 to 8 within 2.5 m from M8 §8); since M9 §2.8 it deals 40 to the enemy hit only and leaves an incense cloud.
- §6.3 — The Chains destination walk starts from the floor of the cell under the Binder's feet and checks each 0.25 m point's cell.
- §7.1 — Blessed speed is 4 m/s (spec: 6), so every class outruns them. Since M9 §2.7 the Fallen and the Binder (6 m/s) outrun them only by 2 m/s.
- §7.1 — A Cherub following the air field moves straight toward its target's horizontal position when in the target's cell or next to it (only the Blessed have a stop distance).
- §7.1 — A started wind-up finishes even if line of sight or range is lost; only silence or losing the target cancels it. The projectile aims at the target's body center when it's fired. Choristers stand still while their target is in range or while winding up; Cherubs keep strafing while winding up.
- §7.1 — Enemy state `attacking` is sent while a Blessed is in melee range and on the tick a cast fires.
- §7.2 — An enemy also picks its first target when it spawns; ties in distance go to the lower player index.
- §7.2 — The Gatekeeper measures distance from its eye to the player's cylinder, and checks line of sight from its eye to the player's body center when it retargets and twice per second for its target, like other enemies.
- §7.4 — A Gatekeeper cast that becomes allowed when another one ends (Judgment after a Volley fires) starts on the next tick. A Volley cancelled by silence counts as cancelled, so the next one is due 4 s later; a Volley started before its target left line of sight still fires. Summoned enemies join the boss arena's spawn queue like a wave.
- §7.4 — Judgment's glow is the Gatekeeper's sprite glowing white-gold plus an additive sphere around it growing from 3 m to 9 m radius over the cast; the completion flash is a white full-screen overlay fading over 0.6 s.
- §7.3 — "Every 0.25 s" for flow fields is tick-based: player index *i* recomputes on ticks where ⌊(tick + 2i) ÷ 7.5⌋ changes (alternating 7- and 8-tick gaps, 0.25 s on average, never on the same tick as another player). Doors opening or closing recompute every field on the next tick.
- §7.3 — Separation can't carry an enemy further in a tick than it walks (its speed × 1/30 s, or its own walk if that was longer); pushes are scaled down to fit. Without this, the crowd behind shoved the front of a swarm forward at 9.2 m/s sustained, faster than the Fallen.
- §7.3 — Separation treats bodies as cylinders: two enemies overlap only if their circles and their height ranges both overlap. Enemies at exactly the same spot are pushed apart along a fixed angle derived from their slot numbers.
- §8.1 — Loader errors that aren't tied to one cell (an empty grid, fewer than 4 `S` markers) name row 0, column 0. A grid that's too large names the first row or column past 256. Grids of different sizes name the first missing row.
- §8.2 — Spawn-point budgets grow every tick in every arena, even while idle, so they are full when combat starts. The cap of 2 applies to the budget carried into the next tick (after placing), so a busy spawn point sustains exactly its rate; capping before placing would lose some (45 instead of 50 at the spec's rate).
- §8.2 — Each spawn point places at most 10 enemies per second (spec: 50), so the swarm builds up over several seconds instead of appearing at once. The benchmark (§2.5) still reaches 1 500 enemies before measuring, about 19 s after the start in the sandbox.
- §8.2 — Wave progression is checked each tick before placing, so a wave that starts can place its first enemies in the same tick.
- §8.1 — A `decor` table error (a reserved or multi-character key, an unknown ID) names the first cell using the key, or row 0, column 0.
- §8.3 — The Pearly Gates' doors sit just outside each arena's `rect`, like the sandbox's. The corridors out of the Lobby and into the boss arena are 4 m wide; the others and the Cloudbridge walkways are 3 m. The Cloudbridge has 1 m support columns at the inner corners where routes turn beside the pit: steering toward a diagonal neighbor's center (§7.3) can clip the pit cell at an inner corner and walk off the edge, which trapped the bot in a fall-and-climb loop; a wall there rules out the diagonal step.
- §8.4 — Sandbox doors sit just outside the arena's `rect`, in the corridor, so entering the `rect` means passing the door.
- §9.3 — The host's floor check raises the feet to the highest floor among the cells the player's circle overlaps (walls and closed doors excluded). The speed check's first interval is measured from when the simulation started.
- §9.3 — The client counts every E press, including a Falling Star it doesn't execute for lack of an ally target; the host then ignores it.
- §9.4 — Cooldowns are sent rounded to whole milliseconds.
- §9.4 — Clients derive enemy height as the highest floor under the enemy's circle (walls excluded); Cherubs add 4 m and move toward it at up to 6 m/s, starting at the target height when a slot first appears.
- §9.4 — Interpolation draws the enemies, projectiles and other players of the snapshot at or before the render tick, moved toward the next snapshot where present; an enemy's state and flags switch to the next snapshot's halfway between them. Feather and censer bursts play after the same render delay, so they line up with the enemy or censer vanishing on screen.
- §10 — Damage taken compares HP + shield between snapshots; healed means HP went up; shield applied means the shield went up, broken means it went to 0.
- §10 — Ability VFX: taunt ring red to 15 m, heal ring green to 15 m, landing shockwave a ring to 5 m 0.4 s after the press, shield bubble a translucent blue sphere (visible from inside too), chain lines from the destination fanning out over the Binder's 30° cone, grey burst a sphere growing to 8 m, afterimage 3 darkened copies of the Betrayer's sprite at the start point (others see it, the user doesn't).
- §11.1 — Terrain is unlit, but faces get a fixed per-vertex tint by orientation and walls darken toward their base, so edges and ledges stay readable without lighting.
- §11.1 — All world billboards share one atlas material, so they are one `InstancedMesh`; billboards face the camera's horizontal right vector. Status tints are a per-instance multiply color plus a glow color and amount.
- §11.2 — The muzzle flash and the icons are drawn as HUD images straight from their files, not from the atlas; the atlas holds only world sprites. Other players are drawn as billboards (hidden while dead).
- §11.1 — The Blessed's model is built from primitives in code (no external model), with a toon shader, inverted-hull ink lines and a 2 px outer contour added in post. Its halo always faces the camera so it reads as a ring from every direction, and shrinks away during the death animation. The atlas height is rounded up to a multiple of 64 instead of a power of two (4096 × 3776), since WebGL 2 mipmaps any size.
- §11.2 — The Blender models are built from generated reference images (a front, a front/side/back turnaround, and a sheet per first-person weapon), listed in `docs/art-prompts.md` and saved in `assets/art-src/reference/` with the concept art. Generated images use only Gemini's aspect ratios and are cropped to size.
- §11.2 — Player models hold their weapon with both hands through IK on the forearms, so the hands stay on it through the walk. Molten cracks (the Fallen's armor) are the edges of a Voronoi pattern in each part's own space, half of them masked out by noise, so they read as veins and stay put on moving parts. A character atlas is the narrowest power-of-two width from 1024 up that keeps it no taller than wide (Fallen: 2048 × 1984).
- §11.1 — Cast release frames (Chorister 7–8, Cherub 5–6, Gatekeeper Volley 4) start on the tick the state becomes `attacking` and play to the end, though `attacking` lasts only that tick. Judgment's loop starts with the cast. Cherub wingbeats are offset per slot, so a flock doesn't flap in unison. The Chorister's death topples it forward like a statue rather than kneeling, since its floor-length robe can't fold; the Gatekeeper leans on its key as it kneels. A Gatekeeper corpse at the front of the corpse list stops the removal of expired ones behind it, so it is never removed with them.
- §11.1 — First-person weapon frames are the bottom half of a view from the eye with a 50° vertical field of view (narrower than the world's 75°, as is usual for weapons, so the parts nearest the eye don't balloon), rendered 2:1 so nothing is cut off on wide screens. Each weapon is held right of center, angled in toward the crosshair, so its side shows. The HUD draws the frame over the bottom half of the screen at the column the render put it, and the muzzle flash at the frame's muzzle point. The Chain Gun's left hand braces the receiver's side rather than the top handle, which would block the view down the barrels.
- §11.2 — The Censer Launcher is the pistol-gripped hand cannon of its weapon sheet, in first person and in the Heretic's hand. Atlas budget: only other players' class atlases and the local class's weapon atlas load, so the worst case is the four enemy atlases, the three largest player atlases and one weapon atlas: 54.4 million pixels.
- §11.2 — `scripts/cutout.py` (Python with Pillow and NumPy) turns the generated 2D images in `assets/art-src/` into sprites in `assets/sprites/<name>.png`. It keys out the green by how much a pixel's green exceeds its red and blue, unmixing the edge colors from the background, so no green fringe is left; then it crops to the pixels at least 50% opaque and resizes to the height it lists for that sprite. The atlas packs each sprite at its own size; with every placeholder replaced, it loads only PNGs. Decorations are cut out at about 170 px per meter of their height (at least 192 px). The lily urn's stems are pale green, so for it pixels with less than 15% of the background's greenness are kept as they are. Ability and class icons have a painted background, so they aren't keyed: any light frame around the painting is trimmed and the icon is cropped to a centered square, 128 px (twice the 64 px ability slot, for high-DPI screens). The muzzle flash is cropped symmetrically around the image center, since the HUD centers it on the muzzle point.
- §11.1 — The terrain textures are 1024 px PNGs in `assets/textures/`, resized from the generated images with wrap-around padding so they stay seamless; the generated images already tiled, so no seams needed fixing. They load with the atlases, before the game starts. Each texture's v runs up from world height 0, so the wall's frieze sits at 0, 4, 8 and 12 m. The riser's grooves end at the bottom of the texture and are cut at the top, so it doesn't repeat vertically; no shipped riser crosses a multiple of 4 m (the tallest is 3 m), so the cut never shows. Gemini saves JPEG; the sources are converted to PNG unchanged.
- §11.1 — Effect textures aren't drawn additively (spec): Heaven is bright, and adding a red or green tint onto ivory marble washes it out to near white, so the taunt and heal rings stopped reading by color. Instead `scripts/cutout.py` turns each image's brightness into opacity (black becomes transparent, as additive blending would make it) and the effects use normal alpha blending, tinted by material color; the chain keeps its own colors with its opacity raised 2.5×. Uses: *ring* on a flat ground quad (taunt, heal, landing shockwave; the shockwave's dark inner ring is replaced by an orange glow, since a dark tint needs no texture); *beam* and *chain* on strips along each line that turn to face the camera every frame (tracers 0.1 m wide, chains 0.3 m with the texture repeating every 0.52 m); *glow* on camera-facing sprites (the Judgment glow, moved toward the camera by the Gatekeeper's radius so it shows over its billboard, and a new 2 m flash on each censer explosion); *smoke* on 12 camera-facing puffs spreading in a ring to 8 m at the Discord impact point, since an image wrapped around a sphere left gaps at eye level. The shield bubble has no effect texture and stays a plain translucent sphere. The chain image's side seam (one link bright on one side, dark on the other) is smoothed over a band at each side.
- §11.2 — UI images: the panel and buttons are 9-slice `border-image`s (panel slice 96 px drawn 40 px wide, keeping its corner ornaments; buttons slice 32 × 60 px drawn 16 × 30 px, keeping the end ornaments). The bar frame is a 9-slice too (slice 25 × 120 px, keeping the rims and end ornaments), drawn at about 0.3 of its size over the HP bar and the Gatekeeper's HP bar, and at 0.2 over its thinner cast bar. The frame is drawn over the bar rather than around it, reaching past it, so the trough and fill run under the rims and up to the tips of the frame's curved ends. Its inside is cleared like the slot frame's. The slot frame is a fixed 64 px square, so it's drawn over the icon at full size rather than sliced; its generated inside was unevenly green with a smudge, so the script clears everything within the rim. The logo image replaces the Title's text heading (its alt text is the name). The title painting, darkened toward the bottom, is the background of the Title, Singleplayer Setup and Results screens; it's a JPEG, being a full-screen painting. Disabled buttons are the normal button greyed out; secondary buttons are it darkened.
- §11.2 — The logo is generated without the halo (it made the word hard to read), and Gemini draws its letters touching however the prompt asks, so `scripts/cutout.py` spaces them: it cuts between the letters along the cheapest top-to-bottom paths (through transparent pixels and the dark outline, not the gold), moves each letter right by 10% of the lettering's height per letter, and redraws a thin ink outline around everything so the cut edges get theirs back. The floating embers are dropped: a few pixels each, their color was mostly the green they were mixed with.
- §11.2 — Cinzel is the variable-weight build from `@fontsource-variable/cinzel` 5.3.0 (OFL-1.1, license in `assets/ui/cinzel-OFL.txt`): one WOFF2 for Latin and one for Latin Extended, so names with accents (such as Czech ones) keep the font; the second loads only when such a character appears. It's used for headings (screen titles, class names, the dungeon option, the tagline, Paused, Victory/Defeat, the boss name and the HUD's center and result texts), buttons and the HUD numbers (HP, cooldowns, enemies remaining). Labels, descriptions and the name field stay in the system font for legibility at small sizes.
- §2.2 — The worker keeps a map from connections to player IDs: a connection gets a player ID when its `hello` is accepted, and the main thread routes `ctrl` messages, snapshots and inputs by it. Before that, the worker answers a connection directly (its `reject`).
- §3 — The host checks a `hello` in this order: version, game in progress, password, full; so `Game is full` is only shown to someone with the right password. Names are trimmed and cut to 16 characters on the host; an empty name becomes `Player`.
- §3 — Host Setup's and Join's password fields are plain text fields (a game password is read out to friends, not an account secret). While `Create` or `Join` is working, the screen shows `Creating the game…` or `Connecting…`; `Back` cancels it.
- §3 — The Join field drops spaces and shows the ID in upper case; `Join` is enabled once it has 6 characters. Enter in either field presses `Join`.
- §3 — The Lobby lists the connected players in id order in its 4 slots (empty ones say `Waiting for a player…`), with the class each picked. A player may switch to another free class while the Lobby is open, which frees the old one; picking one's own class again changes nothing. The host's `Copy` button copies the game ID and says `Copied` for 1.5 s.
- §3 — Loading builds the game (scene, terrain, sprites), compiles its shaders and uploads its textures before sending `ready`, so entering the game at `go` doesn't stall. The host is never dropped for the Loading timeout. A player who left or was dropped during Loading stays in the simulation's player list as disconnected, so every player keeps the index `start` gave it; the party size counts only those at `go`.
- §9.1 — Registering a game ID: a peer that isn't confirmed within 10 s counts as failed; any error other than `unavailable-id` (or the 5th `unavailable-id`) shows `Couldn't reach the matchmaking server`. If the host loses the signaling server later, existing connections continue and it reconnects every 3 s so new clients can still join.
- §9.1 — ICE uses Google's public STUN server and the Metered TURN relay (added after the first real playtest, where players on different networks couldn't connect): UDP and TCP on port 80, and TLS on port 443, which passes strict firewalls. Its credentials are in `src/net/peerConfig.ts`; browsers need them, so they are public. PeerJS's default list has its own relay, whose servers no longer resolve, so it's replaced.
- §9.1 — The host pairs a client's `ctrl` and `snap` connections by the client's peer ID. A connection that closes counts as a disconnect, like the heartbeat timeout, on both sides. `Join` succeeds once `welcome` has arrived and `snap` is open; both must happen within the 10 s.
- §9.1 — Heartbeat: a side whose own heartbeat check runs more than 2 s late (its main thread was blocked, such as by loading) gives the other side a fresh 5 s, since it couldn't have heard anything meanwhile. During Loading the host doesn't time out a client that hasn't sent `ready` (the 20 s Loading timeout covers it), and a loading client allows the host 25 s of silence, since the host may be loading too. Without this, four players loading at once on a slow machine dropped each other.
- §9.1 — Closing on purpose waits 1 s for the last messages: after a `reject` the host closes the connection 1 s later; a leaving host sends `leave` to every client and destroys its peer 1 s later; a leaving client sends `leave` and destroys its peer 1 s later. At Results the host destroys its peer at once (clients ignore it after `gameOver`).
- §9.1 — The host also skips a snapshot part while PeerJS's own send queue for that client isn't empty, besides the 64 KB `bufferedAmount` check.
- §9.4 — Remote clients get the snapshot of every tick divisible by 3, all on the same ticks, plus the last one at the result.
- §4 — When the page becomes hidden while fire is held, fire is released and one input is sent at once: a hidden page draws no frames and sends no input, so the host would keep firing for a player who switched tabs.
- §10 — Party frames sit in the bottom-left column above the own frame (M9 §6.4; they were on the left at 30% of the screen height), in player id order, each with the class icon, name, HP (no text when dead, M8 §3.1) and the HP and shield bar in the bar frame at 0.2 of its size. The ally target's frame gets a gold border and name.
- §13.2 — Multiplayer test: the client pages are 800 × 450, so four software-rendered games on one machine block each other less. A keeps only its last 90 ticks, so when a client page was frozen for longer (its newest tick is older than A's oldest), that client is read again every 200 ms, up to 3 s, before comparing; the counts must still match on every sample.
- M8 §6.1 — Opening the Join screen from an invite link removes only `join` from the URL; other parameters stay. The `Join game <ID>` button follows the same name rule as the other Title buttons.
- M8 §6.4 — Bot auto-join needs `bot=1`, `autojoin=1` and a valid `join`, and doesn't apply with `dev=1`. A `name` that isn't a valid player name means `Bot`. The bot picks again on every lobby update while it has no class, so losing a race for a class to another player picks the next free one.
- M8 §3.3 — The muzzle flash is 16% of the screen height wide (the MVP's was 10%), so it shows around the larger barrels it's drawn behind. It's a sibling drawn before the weapon frame, not inside it, and gets the same recoil and bob offset: the weapon frame's transform would otherwise isolate the flash's screen blend from the game view.
- M8 §3.4 — While airborne (including a Falling Star leap) or dead, the bob's target amplitude is 0 and it eases there like any change; the phase doesn't advance. A horizontal move of more than 2 m in one frame (a teleport) doesn't count as walking.
- M8 §3.5 — The grey chevron shows only while there's no ally target; its ally must be in line of sight, like the target. A chevron's tip is 2.15 m above the ally's feet. Only the Fallen and the Heretic Saint get chevrons, as only they use ally targets.
- M8 §2.1 — A tooltip shows the item's name alone (no key), in a panel drawn with the panel image at 18 px. Ability icons on the cards are 32 px inside the 40 px slot frame. The weapon and passive names are underlined with dots, so they read as hoverable.
- M8 §2.2 — The hints panel sits 64 px from the top (since M9 §6.4 there's no `H · Hints` prompt). Class hints without an ability or attack get an empty icon space, so the lines align. The panel closes at the result. `H` works with the bot too.
- M8 §2.3 — The glint's band is a 115° light gradient swept with the Web Animations API; the slot's gold glow is a drop shadow fading back to the ready glow.
- M8 §3.1 — Billboards are alpha-tested, so the `!`'s fade (and any billboard opacity) is drawn as a dither, like the near fade. The `!` scales 0 → 1.2 over 0.1 s and 1.2 → 1 over 0.05 s. Heal column embers are 0.2 m ember sprites glowing green, starting up to 0.5 m above the feet with lives of 0.56–0.8 s, so the column fills from the feet up. The mote is 5 glow sprites, each lagging the one before by 8% of the flight; it flies from the Heretic's current position to where the target stood at the cast. A Communion that heals the local player also gives the green vignette from the event, besides the MVP's vignette when HP rises. A party frame's damage pulse plays when its HP plus shield went down since the previous snapshot.
- M8 §9.1 — The synthesizer's low-pass is a 2-pole biquad (Q 0.707) whose cutoff sweeps exponentially; noise draws a new seeded random value each cycle of its `freq`, so a lower `freq` sounds darker. Sounds are rendered one per task after the audio context starts (all at once froze the page for 0.4 s), and a sound asked for before it's rendered doesn't play. The context starts on the first pointer or key press anywhere, which on a normal page load is on the Title.
- M8 §9.1 — Triggers: own shots on the local cosmetic shot; others' shots from `shots`; hit and kill ticks with the hit and kill markers; a death chirp for every non-boss enemy that disappears (the spec names the Blessed; Choristers and Cherubs are Heaven's too), at its feather burst; wind-ups when a Chorister's or Cherub's state turns to wind-up; orbs and arrows when a new projectile of that kind appears, except the orbs of a Volley, which get the one *Volley fired* sound when the Gatekeeper's Volley cast ends; the thud when own HP drops while a Blessed within 1.6 m is attacking; the Judgment sounds from `bossCast`. Abilities sound at the user, except Chains, Discord and Field of Blood, which sound where they take effect; Falling Star's landing plays 0.4 s after its leap. `healed`, `shield up` and `shield broken` are the local player's own.
- M8 §9.2 — Loop points are found by `scripts/music-loops.mjs` (Web Audio decoding in headless Chromium, then tempo and bar estimation and spectral similarity at the seam; details in the script). A track always starts from its beginning, so its opening plays each time it starts (an arena sealing, an arena cleared), and then it loops the region it found; at the human's request the loop may start anywhere after the intro. The next likely track and the stings are fetched and decoded ahead; a track's file is fetched again when it's needed after being released.
- M8 §9.3 — The sliders are native range inputs, 1% steps, in a Settings view: a `Settings` button (the Title's last button; on the Pause overlay between `Resume` and `Leave game`) replaces the panel's contents with the title `Settings`, the sliders and `Back`, keeping the panel's size so it doesn't move. Opening Pause shows its own contents again.
- M8 §5 — The benchmark's arena still enters combat at once (MVP §2.5). The snapshot's `countdown` is rounded up to whole tenths; the HUD shows whole seconds rounded up, and the large number and its tick change with each second from 5 to 1.
- M8 §4.1 — The host keeps a dead player's position (it ignores their input) with the feet dropped to the ground at death, so the snapshot's feet are the soul's ground point. Clients time the rise from when they first see the player dead; a player first seen already dead (for example by a joiner) floats at once. The ±0.1 m bob fades in over 0.5 s after the rise.
- M8 §4.2 — A hitscan ray "stops" at the terrain or its range, or at the first enemy it hits (since M9 nothing pierces; the Silver Bullet stops where M9 §2.4 says); a soul it passes before that counts. A censer explodes on a soul only when the soul is nearer along its path than any enemy. A revived player's shield is cleared, and the enemies' flow fields are refreshed at once.
- M8 §4.3, §4.5 — The dead player's camera is the soul's ground point + 1.6 m + the rise (without the bob). Their revive bar is 240 × 8 px under the text.
- M8 §3.1 — The revive pillar is the beam texture on a camera-facing vertical strip; its 40 embers burst up and out and slow down. A dead player's party frame greys the icon and name only, so the ember revive bar keeps its color.
- M8 §9.1 — The revive hum plays while the local player's own progress rose within the last 0.4 s, or while a local shot hit a soul within the last 0.3 s (a client-side ray test against the souls it draws); its pitch is 0.8–1.6× with the progress.
- M8 §6.4 — For the bot, "no living enemy within 6 m" is horizontal distance; it picks the nearest soul in line of sight by 3D distance to the soul's center (ground point + 1.9 m).
- M8 §6.2 — "Loading in progress" is the game's Loading, between `start` and `go`; once the game runs it takes joiners even while another joiner is loading. Players in the game can't change class.
- M8 §6.2 — The in-progress Lobby lists every connected player in its 4 slots, those in the game and those waiting, and shows the client the game ID without the copy button. A joiner's `start` lists the players in the game or loading into it, itself included.
